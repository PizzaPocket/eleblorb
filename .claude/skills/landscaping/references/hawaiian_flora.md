# Hawaiian flora for the Ocean Kingdom

A working palette for islands modelled on the Hawaiian ecosystem and climate,
used by `docs/architecture/ocean_landscape.md`. Names give the Hawaiian name,
the common name and the botanical name. Hawaiian spellings should be checked
with the same fluent reviewer as the village's dialogue.

## Status, and why it matters

Hawaiian landscapes carry their history in their plants. Use the status column
to tell that history correctly.

- **Native:** arrived without people; many found nowhere else.
- **Canoe plant:** brought by the Polynesian voyagers who settled the islands,
  for food, fibre, medicine and building. In the game they belong where people
  live and farm, because Kai Mālie's ancestors carried them.
- **Modern:** introduced since the 1800s and now part of everyday Hawaiian
  village life (plumeria, mango). Use sparingly, around homes and the store.
- **Excluded:** invasive species that damage real Hawaiian ecosystems. Never
  use them, even as filler: kiawe (mesquite), strawberry guava, miconia,
  albizia, African tulip tree, haole koa, Christmas berry, fountain grass.

## Climate and zones

Trade winds blow from the north-east. Windward faces are wet and lush; leeward
faces are dry. Zones follow water and exposure, from the reef up:

| Zone | Conditions | Typical plants |
|---|---|---|
| Reef flat | shallow warm water | *limu* (seaweeds), coral heads |
| Coastal strand | sand, salt spray, wind | *naupaka kahakai*, *pōhuehue*, *ʻākulikuli*, *hinahina kū kahakai*, *ʻilima* |
| Coastal forest | just behind the beach | *hala*, *kou*, *milo*, *niu* |
| Dry lowland (leeward) | sun, thin soil | *wiliwili*, *ʻaʻaliʻi*, *pili* grass, *ʻilima* |
| Wet valley (windward) | rain, streams, shade | *kukui* on the slopes, *hau* along streams, *hāpuʻu* and *uluhe* ferns, *ʻōhiʻa lehua* on ridges |
| Farmed land | water managed by people | *kalo* in *loʻi*, *maiʻa*, *kō*, *ʻuala*, *wauke* |
| Young volcanic ground | bare tuff or lava, little soil | lichen, *moa* (whisk fern), scattered *pili*, *ʻaʻaliʻi*, *ʻōhiʻa* as the first tree |

## Cultural care

- Grow *ʻawa*, *kī* and ferns as plants people tend and use. Do not build altars
  (*kuahu*) or shrine-like arrangements around them.
- Tradition holds that picking *ʻōhiʻa lehua* blossoms brings rain. Treat it as
  a respected tree, not a decoration.
- Plants named after or associated with specific deities stay plants, not
  symbols.
