# Architectural glossary

Use the real term in your reasoning, in code comments and in your reports, so that choices can be checked against the tradition they come from. Each entry is short on purpose. If you are about to build an element you cannot name here, stop and look it up or choose one you can name.

## Plan and massing

- **Bay.** A repeated structural division of a facade or plan, between posts, piers or windows (a three-bay house has three openings across).
- **Range.** A long single block. **Double-pile**: two rooms deep. **Single-pile**: one room deep.
- **Hall house.** A medieval house whose heart is one tall hall open to the roof with a hearth, with service rooms at one end and private rooms at the other.
- **Cross-passage.** A through passage between front and back doors in a hall house, separating the hall from the service end.
- **Cross-wing.** A secondary block set at right angles to the main range. **L-plan, T-plan, U-plan, H-plan, courtyard plan.**
- **Ell, outshut (outshot), lean-to.** A low addition under a continuation of the main roof or a sloped roof against a wall.
- **Annex, wing, pavilion.** A subordinate block; a pavilion is a distinct projecting or end volume.
- **Lobby-entry.** An entrance into a small lobby against the chimney stack (New England and Welsh houses).
- **Hearth bay.** The structural bay around the chimney.
- **Ambulatory, nave, aisle, chancel, transept, apse.** Parts of a church plan; use only for a church.
- **Great hall, solar, buttery, pantry, larder, scullery, parlour, snug.** Rooms of a medieval or vernacular house (solar is the upper private room).
- **Stabbur.** A raised Nordic storehouse on posts or stones.
- **Courtyard house (siheyuan).** Rooms around a central courtyard on an axis.
- **Piano nobile.** The principal upper floor of a formal building, above a raised basement.

## Structure

- **Foundation, footing, plinth, water table.** The base; a plinth is the visible thickened base; the water table is the projecting band that sheds water at the base of a wall.
- **Sill plate, sole plate.** The timber laid on the foundation. **Sill beam.** The lowest horizontal timber of a frame.
- **Post, stud, girt, plate, tie beam, collar, rafter, purlin, ridge board (ridge pole), common rafter, hip rafter, valley rafter, jack rafter.** Timber frame and roof members.
- **Truss.** A triangulated roof frame. Types: **king post**, **queen post**, **hammer beam**, **scissor**, **cruck** (curved blades rising from the ground or wall to the ridge).
- **Cruck frame.** A frame of paired curved timbers forming A shapes along a building.
- **Post-and-beam, balloon frame, platform frame, box frame, Fachwerk, half-timbering (colombage), pan de bois.** Timber framing traditions.
- **Infill, wattle and daub, nogging, brick nogging, plaster.** What fills the panels of a frame.
- **Jetty, jettied floor, oversailing.** An upper floor projecting beyond the wall below on joists.
- **Dragon beam.** The diagonal timber carrying a jetty round a corner.
- **Corbel, bracket, knee brace, strut.** Supports for overhangs and load.
- **Buttress.** A masonry mass bracing a wall. **Flying buttress** arches over an aisle.
- **Lintel, arch, voussoir, keystone, springing line, spandrel.** Spanning an opening.
- **Pier, pilaster, column, post.** Vertical supports; a pilaster is flat and projecting from a wall.
- **Rubble, ashlar, coursed, random, dressed stone, quoins, rusticated.** Masonry types and finishes.
- **Rammed earth, adobe, cob, wattle.** Earth construction.
- **Laft (Norwegian log), Blockbau, notched log, saddle notch, dovetail notch, Scandinavian full-scribe, chinking.** Log construction terms.
- **Stave construction.** Vertical timber posts and boards (stave church).
- **Batter.** The inward lean of a wall as it rises.

## Roofs

- **Pitch.** The slope, in degrees or rise over run. **Steep** (over 45 degrees), **medium** (30 to 45), **low** (under 30).
- **Gable, front-gable, side-gable.** Gabled roof with its gable end facing the street or the side.
- **Hip, hipped.** All four sides slope. **Half-hip (jerkinhead, clipped gable).** The gable peak is clipped by a small hip. **Gablet, dutch gable (curved parapet gable).**
- **Cross-gable.** Two gables meeting at right angles. **Valley.** The internal junction of two roof planes. **Hip line.** The external junction.
- **Gambrel, mansard (curb roof), bellcast (flared, concave eave), catslide, saltbox, skillion (shed), butterfly, sawtooth, barrel vault, flat roof with parapet.**
- **Ridge, ridge cap, finial, ridge crest, ridge tile.** Top of the roof.
- **Eave, overhang, rake, verge, fascia, soffit, bargeboard (vergeboard), eaves return, cornice return.** Edges of a roof; the rake is the sloping edge of a gable.
- **Exposed rafter tails, outriggers, brackets, knee braces.** Craftsman and bungalow eave details.
- **Dormer.** A roof-slope window with its own roof: **gabled, hipped, shed, eyebrow (swept), wall dormer (gablet), lucarne, flat-roofed.**
- **Cupola, lantern, belfry, turret, spire, spirelet, finial, weathervane.** Roof-top features.
- **Roofing materials.** Thatch (long straw, water reed), shingle (wood, shake), slate, clay pantile, plain tile, stone slab (Cotswold, Collyweston), turf (sod), copper, standing-seam metal, tin, bark, palm thatch.
- **Snow guard, scupper, gutter, downspout, flashing, drip edge.** Water and snow management.
- **Pagoda, tiered eaves, hip-and-gable (xieshan), flush gable (yingshan), overhanging gable (xuanshan), hip (wudian), dougong (bracket sets), upturned eaves, ridge beast.** Chinese roof terms.

## Chimneys and fire

- **Chimney, stack, flue, breast, shaft, pot, cap, crown, offset (weathering), cheek.**
- **Chimney breast.** The projection of the stack into a room or out of a wall; **external stack.** A stack on the outside face of a gable wall.
- **Hearth, firebox, mantel, overmantel, fire-back, inglenook (a hearth recess with seats), ingle, trivet, andiron, crane, bellows, bread oven, beehive oven.**
- **Smoke hood, louvre (smoke vent), smoke bay.** Early smoke management.

## Facade and ornament

- **Elevation.** One face of a building. **Facade.** The main face.
- **Base, shaft, capital.** Parts of a column; **pedestal.** The block beneath a column or statue (base, die, cap).
- **Order.** Classical column system (Tuscan, Doric, Ionic, Corinthian) with its entablature.
- **Entablature.** Architrave, frieze, cornice. **Pediment.** Triangular or segmental gable over a portico or opening; **tympanum** is its face. **Broken, open, swan-neck** pediments.
- **Portico, porch, stoop, veranda, loggia, arcade, colonnade, gallery, balcony, oriel, bay window, bow window.**
- **String course (belt course, band course).** A horizontal band on a wall. **Cornice, frieze, dentil, modillion, corbel table.** Eave ornament.
- **Quoin.** Stone or brick blocks marking a corner. **Pilaster, engaged column, pier.**
- **Window.** Casement (hinged), sash (sliding vertical), fixed light, mullion (vertical divider), transom (horizontal divider), muntin (glazing bar), leaded light, bullseye, lancet, oculus, Palladian, fanlight. **Sill, apron, jamb, head, reveal, architrave (surround), hood mould (drip mould), shutter (louvred, panelled, board and batten).**
- **Door.** Panelled, ledged and braced, batten, Dutch (split), double, cart door, wicket, **door case, doorstep, threshold, lintel, fanlight, transom.**
- **Arch types.** Round, segmental, pointed, ogee, lancet, horseshoe, flat (jack), corbelled.
- **Gable ornament.** Bargeboard, pendant, finial, **gable board (vindskeid)**, king-post ornament, **sunburst, pargeting** (moulded plaster).
- **Rosemaling.** Norwegian decorative painted flowers; **Dalarna horse, Falu red, tømmerhus, drachenkopf (dragon head), kerfschnitt.** Nordic and Alpine ornament terms.
- **Lattice, fretwork, moon gate, pailou, lingxing.** Chinese ornament.
- **Vigas (projecting roof beams), canales (scuppers), latillas, parapet, kiva, horno, ramada.** Pueblo and earthen terms.

## Materials and finish

- **Stone.** Limestone (Cotswold, Jura), sandstone, granite, flint, basalt, slate, fieldstone.
- **Timber.** Oak, pine, spruce, larch, cedar, bamboo, driftwood.
- **Brick.** Common, Flemish bond, English bond, headers and stretchers, glazed header, gauged.
- **Plaster and render.** Lime plaster, limewash (whitewash), stucco, rough-cast, pebbledash, pargeting, tadelakt.
- **Metal.** Wrought iron, copper, lead (flashing), zinc, tin.
- **Colour traditions.** Falu red (iron oxide), ochre, limewash white, black tar (pine tar), madder red, verdigris.

## Sustainability and site

- **Orientation, aspect, prevailing wind, solar gain, windbreak, shelterbelt.**
- **Vernacular.** Local building tradition by local builders from local materials. **Polite (high-style) architecture.** Architect-designed or fashion-led building.
- **Revival, pastiche, folly.** Later reinterpretations.
- **Streetscape, townscape, plot, burgage plot (long narrow plot with house at the front), curtilage (land attached to a house), building line, setback.**
