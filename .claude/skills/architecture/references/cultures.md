# Cultural architecture: giving each kingdom its own built identity

A kingdom should be recognisable by **silhouette alone**, in a thumbnail, from a distance, before any detail. Culture is expressed through massing, roof form, proportions, ornament motifs, materials, palette, the relationship to the ground and the shape language of openings, never through new props stapled on. Always **read the kingdom's entry in `docs/world_bible.md` first** and treat it as authoritative; the defaults below are starting points to reconcile with it. Update the bible when you establish something new.

## 1. Method for any culture (real or invented)

Answer these in writing, in order. Each answer constrains the next.

1. **Environment and hazard.** Climate, terrain, local materials (stone, timber, earth, reed, metal, coral, ice), threats (snow, heat, flood, wind, beasts, raiders).
2. **Economy.** What do they make, grow, trade? This sets building types (smithies, docks, terraces, granaries, mills, observatories) and sizes.
3. **Beliefs and symbols.** Cardinal directions, sacred numbers, animals, ancestors, elements. These set orientation, ornament motifs, the number of bays or tiers, colour taboos.
4. **Society.** Communal compounds or single-family houses, hierarchy visible in roof and height, public squares or private courtyards, defence.
5. **Technology.** Spans, joinery, glazing, metalworking, masonry skill, water control.
6. **Aesthetic values.** Austere or ornate, heavy or light, curved or straight, colourful or muted.
7. **Derive the grammar** (section 2 table): massing, roof, wall, openings, ornament, palette, ground relationship, signature silhouette.
8. **Signature motif at three scales**: in the silhouette (roof or mass), in the openings, in the ornament. Repeat it so the kingdom reads as one people.
9. **Contrast** with neighbouring kingdoms. If two kingdoms share a roof form and palette, one of them is wrong.
10. **Express it in Eleblorb's language**: culture changes the **epsilon, proportion, silhouette and arrangement** of SuperEgg solids and the shape of superellipse openings. Crisp obsidian uses flat epsilons and angular arrangement; rounded earth uses soft epsilons and battered walls; flowing ocean forms use high curvature and shells. Do not introduce unrelated primitive styles.

## 2. Grammar template (fill one per kingdom)

| Aspect | Question |
|---|---|
| Massing | Block, courtyard, stepped terrace, tower, stilted, tree-borne? |
| Roof | Type, pitch, eave, ridge treatment? |
| Walls | Material, thickness, batter, plinth, texture? |
| Openings | Shape (superellipse exponent), size, frames, shutters? |
| Ornament | Where and in what motif? |
| Palette | Walls, roof, trim, accent, taboo colours? |
| Ground | Plinth, stilts, steps, terraces, paths? |
| Signature silhouette | The one thing you would draw in a thumbnail? |
| Public heart | Square, courtyard, forge, harbour, hearth? |

## 3. Kingdom defaults

### Ohio (the starting village): English and northern European village vernacular

- **Massing.** Rectangular primary masses with gable roofs and a lean-to or cross-wing; a polite civic hall and inn among vernacular cottages. Clustered round a green and a stream.
- **Roof.** Gable at 35 to 45 degrees, tile or slate in warm clay, deep-ish eaves, bargeboards on gables, a cross-gable for the hall's bell turret.
- **Walls.** Stone plinth and ground storey, plastered or timber-framed upper storey, **jettied** (overhanging) in the older buildings. Continuous plaster and stone; no log walls here.
- **Openings.** Tall casements in rounded-superellipse frames with sills and shutters, small to the street, larger on the main room; doors with a canopy; the hall and inn with a pediment or portico.
- **Ornament.** Carved bargeboards, bracketed eaves, a pargeted (moulded plaster) panel, painted shutters, a market cross.
- **Palette.** Cream, ochre, madder red, slate, oak brown, moss green accents.
- **Silhouette.** Steep gables, big chimneys at the gable ends, a bell turret on the hall, a windmill on the rise.

### Snow Village (Ice Kingdom): Alpine and Nordic mountain vernacular

- **Massing.** Compact, wind-sheltered, smallest wall to the prevailing wind; a sheltered common yard; wood stores and vestibules as secondary masses.
- **Roof.** Steep, deep-eaved gables that shed or bear snow; snow guards at the eave; long catslides over stores; turf or shingle; doors and walkways kept off the fall line of snow.
- **Walls.** Stone foundation, stacked log courses with projecting corner notches, vertical board in gables.
- **Openings.** Small, deep-set, on the sunny side; thick shutters; enclosed porches and vestibules so heat does not escape; hearths on the gable.
- **Ornament.** Carved bargeboards and brackets with simple geometric or animal motifs, painted door panels, stacked firewood as a facade pattern.
- **Palette.** Weathered brown, charcoal, snow white, a single warm accent (red, ochre) on doors; hearth glow.
- **Silhouette.** Steep dark roofs rimmed with snow, chimneys steaming, long low woodsheds.

### Rock and Ground Kingdom: earth and stone architecture

- **Massing.** Stepped terraces, cubic masses stacked and set into slopes, cliff-cut chambers, plazas and ladders; round kivas or silos as accent.
- **Roof.** Flat with parapets and scupper spouts, walkable terraces, projecting roof beams (vigas), shade ramadas of poles; low domed ovens.
- **Walls.** Thick, battered (leaning inward 5 to 10 degrees), rounded rammed-earth or adobe corners, stone footings, buttresses. SuperEgg soft epsilons express rounded mud brick naturally.
- **Openings.** Small, deep-set, rounded, with thick reveals and painted surrounds; slit windows on exposed faces; ladders to roof entries.
- **Ornament.** Painted bands, geometric reliefs, inlaid stones, pole ends, drying strings of produce.
- **Palette.** Terracotta, ochre, sienna, sand, umber, with turquoise or white accents.
- **Silhouette.** Stepped blocks like rock itself, rounded parapets, a forest of beam ends.

### Fire Kingdom: advanced volcanic modernism

- **Massing.** Continuous asymmetrical SuperEgg shells, extruded superellipse plans, terraces, curved galleries, cantilevers and bridges grow from the caldera wall around a large lava reservoir. Buildings read as precise modern construction rather than caves, primitive stone huts or classical monuments.
- **Roof.** Shallow walkable shells, rounded terraces, folded or catenary-like canopies, with ash shedding, ventilation and concealed lava services integrated into the form. No copied domestic gables or classical pediments.
- **Walls.** Cast basalt composite and refractory masonry work with structural blackened metal and broad clear, smoky or colored glass panes. Large glazed bays bring sunlight into homes, laboratories and civic rooms.
- **Openings.** Generous superellipse portals and continuous curved glazing; pivoting, sliding or recessed metal-and-glass doors. No quaint shutters or repeated slit windows.
- **Ornament.** A restrained Gaudí-informed craft layer appears in organic hand-forged screens, branching supports, rails, handles, lighting and selected colored glass or mosaic. It never copies a named real building or imports religious and regional symbols.
- **Services.** A mostly concealed lava-duct network feeds household immersion wells, molten-floor rejuvenation rooms, baths, laboratories and forges. Show only functional manifolds, access hatches, controls and glowing service seams.
- **Arrival.** A broad dry route from the kingdom gate terminates at an open threshold on the near reservoir bank. Paired organic metal-and-glass pylons frame a tall superellipse of air and hold narrow controlled flames without reducing mount clearance.
- **Lighting.** Use small stable flames within glass capsules, forged wall cells and structural entrance beacons. Emissive glass supplies most of the glow; large turbulent flames belong only to forges, eruptions and hazards.
- **Palette.** Dark basalt and refractory shell form the visual ground, not a monochrome city. Most glazing is clear or smoky; stained glass concentrates amber, garnet, cobalt, violet, emerald and teal at thresholds, clerestories, screens and landmarks. Blackened, heat-blued and enameled iron pairs with stainless precision details and sparse protected gold or silver inlay on cooler surfaces.
- **Silhouette.** Flowing dark masses opened by large luminous glass fields, fine branching metalwork and controlled glimpses of their thermal system.

### Ocean Kingdom: harbour and shell architecture

- **Massing.** Stilted and floating buildings on docks and piers, linked by walkways; boat-shaped and spiral forms; lighthouse towers; a harbour as the public heart.
- **Roof.** Inverted-hull or barrel roofs, shell-like scalloped shingles, conical caps on towers, awnings of sail.
- **Walls.** Weathered timber, driftwood planking, coral and shell-lime plaster, rope lashings and nets.
- **Openings.** Round portholes (circles are superellipses at exponent 2), arched hatches, sliding shutters.
- **Ornament.** Nautilus spirals, rope and net motifs, shell inlays, carved sea creatures on brackets.
- **Palette.** Sea-foam, pearl, driftwood grey, coral pink, deep blue.
- **Silhouette.** A forest of stilts under curved roofs, masts and lanterns.

### Plant (Primate) Kingdom: living and tree-borne architecture

- **Massing.** Platform huts around trunks, rope-bridge networks, terraced hillside huts, overgrown ruins, round huts; buildings that grow with the forest.
- **Roof.** Broad palm or reed thatch with deep overhangs, round or conical forms.
- **Walls.** Woven bamboo, bark panels, lashed poles, living wood, plank platforms.
- **Openings.** Open-air screened windows, wide round doorways.
- **Ornament.** Lashing patterns, carved animal posts, painted masks, hanging charms.
- **Palette.** Leaf greens, thatch gold, bark brown, bright flower accents.
- **Silhouette.** Round roofs among canopy, ropes and bridges, ruins wrapped in vines.

### Sky Kingdom: celestial classical in cloud, gold and quartz

Full charter: `docs/architecture/sky_kingdom.md`; layout:
`docs/architecture/sky_kingdom_layout.md`.

- **People.** The Tempestars: a leisured, long-lived sky people with cloud lower
  bodies, in six rival courts. Not gods; no worship spaces.
- **Influences.** Greco-Roman celestial classical leads (about 55 percent):
  peristyles, tholoi, colonnades, odeon, racing ring. The Chinese celestial
  palace is a strong second (about 35 percent), limited to the Dawn Gate,
  Aethra's Hall of Mist and Pantao's Peach Garden and banquet hall, where Sun Wu
  Kong's story is set. Woven cloud and gold are the accent. Never both
  traditions' roof and wall systems on one building.
- **Materials.** Cloud is everything you stand on, gold holds it, quartz stands
  upright (columns, walls, roofs; never a floor). Every floor, podium, ramp and
  island surface is cloud and one-way: passable rising from below. Bank cloud
  is the ground; woven cloud makes awnings and curtains (no collision);
  set cloud makes floating couches, steps and moored rooms, held by gold.
- **Massing and roofs.** Halls on stepped cloud podiums banded in gold; roofs float a clear gap
  above their columns; some columns hang short of the floor; drifting stairs of
  separate slabs with a continuous walking line.
- **Hierarchy.** Podium height and the width of the floating gap; Aethra's Hall
  of Mist alone has a double floating eave.
- **Palette.** 60 percent quartz and cloud white, 30 percent sky pastels on
  woven cloud and clothing, 10 percent gold.
- **No bridges.** Tempestars float and the hero flies; courts stand apart in open
  sky.
- **Exclusions.** Copied temples or sacred emblems, altars, statues of gods,
  heavy masonry walls, solid gold buildings, pitched timber roofs, chimneys.

### The Chinese village: Chinese courtyard and temple architecture

- **Massing.** Axial, symmetrical **courtyard compounds** (the main hall on the north axis, side halls, a gate), covered corridors, a moon gate or **pailou** gate, tiered pagodas with an odd number of storeys, a market street. Hierarchy shown by height, roof type and colour.
- **Roof.** **Hip-and-gable (xieshan)** and **hip** roofs for important halls (double eaves for the highest), **flush gable (yingshan)** and **overhanging gable (xuanshan)** for ordinary houses; grey or green tile for ordinary, red walls and yellow tile reserved for imperial buildings; eaves with **upturned corners**, **ridge ornaments** (beasts, finials), bracket sets (**dougong**) under the eaves.
- **Walls.** Whitewashed plaster over a stone **plinth** with stepped balustrade, **red-lacquered columns** on stone bases carrying the roof, infill walls non-structural; carved lattice screens.
- **Openings.** Round moon gates, lattice windows in superellipse frames, red double doors with studs, an odd number of bays (3, 5, 7, 9).
- **Ornament.** Carved beams and brackets, painted eaves in blue-green, dragon and cloud motifs, lanterns, stone lions at gates.
- **Palette.** White walls, grey tile, red columns, green and blue painted eaves; gold only for imperial.
- **Silhouette.** Sweeping upturned roof corners, tiered pagodas, red columns under grey roofs.
- **Engine note.** Express upturned eaves with SuperEgg slabs curved at the corners, columns as squarish SuperEgg posts on stone bases that reach the beam, and dougong as stacked squarish blocks.

### The Western city (concrete skyline)

- **Massing.** Blocks and towers on a street grid; skybridges; podium shops under residential towers.
- **Roof.** Flat roofs with parapets, rooftop plant, antenna clusters.
- **Walls.** Concrete, glass, steel; strongly horizontal bands.
- **Openings.** Wide and modular, rounded superellipse corners to stay in language.
- **Palette.** Concrete greys with lit-window colour.

## 4. Inventing a fantastical kingdom: worked pattern

Example: **a kingdom of salt cavern dwellers.**
1. Environment: a vast salt cavern, constant dark, dripping brine, crystal everywhere. Materials: salt blocks, crystal, fungus timber, bone.
2. Economy: salt harvesting, crystal cutting, glow-fungus farming.
3. Beliefs: light is sacred; a lantern at every door; the number six.
4. Society: terraced guild halls climbing a crystal slope, a lamp-lit market at the bottom.
5. Technology: salt masonry, glass lenses.
6. Aesthetic: faceted, luminous, cool.
7. Grammar: stepped hexagonal terraces; roofs as low faceted domes; walls of pale translucent blocks with a heavy plinth; openings as lens shapes (superellipses with exponent near 3); ornament as crystal clusters at eaves and door heads; palette white, pale pink, teal glow; signature silhouette: clustered hexagonal domes glowing from within.
8. Express it with high-epsilon SuperEgg slabs for facets and soft epsilon for the domes; record it in the bible.

## 5. Checklist for a kingdom's architecture

1. Read the bible entry; the new design agrees with it.
2. I can sketch the kingdom's thumbnail silhouette in one line.
3. The grammar table is filled in and consistent.
4. Materials and climate justify the roof, walls and openings.
5. A signature motif repeats at silhouette, opening and ornament scales.
6. The kingdom looks different from every other kingdom at a glance.
7. Everything is built in the SuperEgg and superellipse language, with plain boxes only where the main skill allows.
8. Polite buildings (hall, temple, gate) and vernacular ones (homes, sheds) differ visibly.
9. The bible is updated with any new fact.
