# Architectural features catalogue: entrances, overhangs, balconies, bays and variety

Features are how buildings of one tradition become individuals. Choose them by **purpose, climate, status and style**, never at random. For each feature below: what it is, when it is right, the styles it belongs to, and how it is built here (SuperEgg parts, Boolean cuts, square-edged posts that reach their beams, graded roofs that fall away from the wall). A settlement's charter lists which of these are allowed; a village draws a varied subset, not all.

## 1. Entrances and covers over doors

| Feature | What it is | Use it for | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stoop** | A low platform and steps at the door | Any door above wet ground; the minimum entry | All |
| **Doorstep and threshold** | A single stone step | Small cottages, sheds | All |
| **Pent hood (pentice)** | A small sloped roof (lean-to) over the door on brackets | Cottages, workshops | English, French Canadian, Alpine |
| **Porch (open)** | A roofed platform in front of a door, usually gabled, on posts | Homes, inns | English, Arts and Crafts, American |
| **Enclosed porch or vestibule (gabled porch)** | A small walled room before the door | Cold climates, inns, halls (keeps heat in) | Nordic, English church porch, Alpine |
| **Portico** | A columned porch with a pediment | Halls, inns, polite houses | Georgian, classical |
| **Door case or surround** | Pilasters and a lintel or hood on the door itself | Polite houses, shops | Georgian, English |
| **Hood on brackets** | A deep sloped canopy on carved brackets | Smithies, shops, workshops | English, German |
| **Awning or shop canopy** | A fabric or board canopy over a counter or hatch | Bakeries, stalls, shops | Market towns |
| **Covered gallery or veranda** | A deep covered walk along the front, with posts | Inns, French Canadian houses, hot or snowy climates | French Canadian, Alpine, Southern |
| **Loggia** | A recessed covered gallery within the building envelope | Mediterranean, upper storeys | Mediterranean, Italianate |
| **Arcade or colonnade** | A row of arches or columns along a front | Market halls, civic fronts | Classical, medieval market towns |
| **Cart door and loading hood** | A wide opening with a sloped hood and hoist | Mills, barns, workshops | Vernacular working buildings |
| **Gate and wicket** | A large and a small door set in one frame | Courtyard houses, farms | Chinese, European farm |

**Choosing.** A home gets a pent hood or an open porch; a smithy a deep hood; a bakery an awning; an inn a veranda or gallery and a porch with the best door; a hall a portico; a mill a cart hood; a cold-climate dwelling a vestibule. Vary the **type, width and depth** between neighbouring houses; keep the charter's roof pitch and bracket motif.

## 2. Overhangs and projections

- **Eave overhang.** The roof edge projecting beyond the wall; 0.3 m (English), 0.6 to 1.5 m (Arts and Crafts, Alpine). Exposed rafter tails, bracketed, or boxed.
- **Gable overhang (verge).** The gable projecting past the end wall; supported by **outriggers** and decorated with a **bargeboard**.
- **Jetty (jettied storey).** The upper floor projecting over the lower on joists; **close-studded** or **panelled**; a **dragon beam** at the corner. Medieval timber-frame towns.
- **Cantilever or oversailing.** A projecting upper storey or balcony supported by joists carried through the wall.
- **Projecting gable (cross-gable bay).** A forward gabled bay breaking the roof line.
- **Hood mould or drip.** A projecting moulding over a window or door to shed water.
- **Corbelling.** Stepped brackets of stone or brick supporting a projecting course.
- **Bellcast eave.** A concave flare at the eave: French Canadian and Alpine.

## 3. Balconies and upper-level features

| Type | Description | Use | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Juliet balcony** | A shallow rail or balustrade across a tall French window; you cannot step out, you stand at the opening | A bedroom or upper room window of a polite house | French, Georgian, Italianate |
| **Balcony (projecting)** | A platform cantilevered on joists or brackets with a rail, entered by a door | Inns' upper rooms, civic fronts, houses | English, Alpine, Mediterranean |
| **Gallery** | A balcony running along or around an upper floor, usually under the eave | Alpine chalets, Nordic stabbur, inns | Alpine, Nordic |
| **Sleeping porch** | A covered upper porch used as a bedroom | Hot climates, bungalows | American |
| **Oriel window** | A projecting bay window on an upper floor supported on corbels | Inns, halls, jettied houses | English medieval |
| **Bay window** | A projecting window (square, canted or bow), ground or upper | Parlour, shop front | English, Georgian |
| **Bow window** | A curved bay | Shops, polite houses | Georgian |
| **Hoist door and gable hoist** | A loft door with a beam and pulley under the gable | Barns, mills, granaries, merchants' houses | Dutch, English |
| **External stair and landing** | An outside stair to an upper door | Granaries, hillside houses, workshops with upper rooms | Alpine, Nordic, Mediterranean |
| **Wall dormer and gablet** | A gable rising through the eave line | Showpiece bay, hoist, loft light | English, Cotswold |
| **Turret and belfry** | A small tower or open bell frame on the roof | Halls, schools, churches | All |

## 4. Windows and glazing variety

- **Casement** (hinged), **sash** (sliding), **fixed**, **leaded light**, **oculus** (round), **lancet**, **transom**, **clerestory** (high band under the roof), **skylight**.
- **Groups.** Single, paired (twin lights under one head), **ribbon** (a continuous band), **bay**, **oriel**.
- **Variety within a village.** A cottage has one or two small casements; a hall one tall window set; a shop a wide display window; a barn a slatted vent.

## 5. Roof and wall variations that make siblings, not clones

- **Roof.** Gable with the ridge parallel or perpendicular to the lane; half-hip or hip; cross-gable; **catslide** (a long rear slope over an outshut); **lean-to** against a side; a **gablet** over the door.
- **Wall.** Stone ground storey versus full plaster; plaster panels in different frame patterns (square panels, close studding, herringbone infill); **pargeting** or plain; a limewash tone from the palette; a repaired patch in a different tone (age).
- **Plan.** Single range, L, T, with a rear **outshut**, a side **lean-to**, an attached **workshop** or **store**.
- **Chimney.** Gable-end, central, paired; tall or short; capped differently.
- **Storeys.** One, one and a half (loft with dormers), two; storey heights vary by 0.2 to 0.4 m for age.

## 6. How to use a feature in Eleblorb

- Build each as a SuperEgg composition: posts and beams squarish and reaching what they carry; roofs as graded slabs falling away from the wall; brackets as rolled squarish blocks; rails and balusters as slender SuperEgg parts; openings as superellipse Boolean cuts; plain boxes only for floors and seam fills.
- Collision: floors, steps, balcony decks and porch roofs are landable (parkour policy); railings and posts are solid; fine trim is decorative.
- Each feature has a **clearance rule**: porches never block doors or paths; balconies stand clear of the ground by 2.3 m or more over a walkway; a veranda leaves 1.2 m of walk beneath.
- Build a **small family** of each (for example three porch types, three balcony types) as reusable builders in `EntryDressing` or a sibling class, parametrised by width, depth, post count, bracket style and roof, so a village can draw varied examples from one tradition.

## 7. Examples of a varied set for one village (English vernacular charter)

- Cottage A: single range, gable end to the lane, pent hood on two brackets, a lean-to store.
- Cottage B: L-plan, open gabled porch on posts, stone plinth and plaster, a half-hip end.
- House C: two-storey cross-wing, jettied front, hood mould on windows, an oriel on the upper floor.
- Inn: Georgian-lite polite front, five bays, a portico, a rear coaching yard, a gallery to the yard.
- Smithy: workshop bay with a deep hood, a living room above with shuttered casements.
- Hall: portico with pediment and a belfry.
Each shares pitch band, eave, plinth, palette and bracket motif; none repeats another's plan, porch and window arrangement all at once.
