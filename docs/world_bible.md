# Eleblorbs — World Bible

A living reference for the game's story, characters, and world-building facts, as they've been established during development. This is lore/narrative content only — implementation details (how something is built in Godot) belong in code, not here.

## Premise

The player wakes from a coma in a grass field with amnesia. A small town sits nearby, including an antique shop the player is meant to visit. The overarching plot conflict is a **Demon King** who wants to exterminate all elemental energy in the world.

### The Demon King's mirror world

The Demon King's domain is a mirror world: a reverse reflection of ours, inverted and mirrored beneath it. Skeletons are the first minions known to manifest from it, rising directly up out of the ground into the wasteland above.

The Demon King feeds off living things elsewhere in the world too. In the Plant Kingdom, that feeding takes the form of a curse on the monkeys living there (see Kingdoms, below).

## Protagonist

- An amnesiac player character (no name established yet).
- Has a sentient companion named **Blorbus** — in fact, Blorbus *is* one of the player's own three starting blorbs (see Blorbs, below), not a separate character. Specifically: whichever of the three is still ungemmed once the player has merged elemental gems into the other two. The second elemental transformation finishes first; then the untouched third blorb unexpectedly begins transforming too, turns a dull, greyish pink — "brain pink," not a bright bubblegum tone — and starts talking, unprompted, taking the name Blorbus for itself. Blorbus's awakening unlocks the player's ability to befriend additional wild blorbs: before the sentient Psychic blorb is present to coordinate them, no other blorb can join the starting party. Once awake, Blorbus can never be merged with a gem itself. Why it specifically wakes up this way, and what (if anything) it remembers or knows, is still unexplained — Blorbus's own account of it amounts to "I don't know why either."
- Blorbus is a **Psychic-type** blorb. His core opens into a pocket-universe storage space that functions as the player's inventory: he psychically manages the items kept there and coordinates the player's other blorbs. The inventory interface is therefore a manifestation of Blorbus himself rather than an ordinary abstract menu.
- The player's awareness can switch psychically between their own body and Blorbus whenever he is not being worn as part of the blorb suit. While inhabiting Blorbus, the player's body follows him like a companion blorb. Humongous begins as a free resident of his Crossroads home, with no automatic connection to Blorbus's core. Blorbus must first speak with him, establishing their trust and unlocking the choice to mind-merge or deliberately shrink and carry him within the pocket dimension of Blorbus's infinite core. Humongous can cross into another kingdom only after he has actually entered that core; merely meeting or mind-merging with him at home does not silently pack him for travel. A carried Humongous can later be released or mind-merged from within the core. Ending a mind merge returns awareness to Blorbus while leaving Humongous physically present. While mind-merged, Humongous can speak mind-to-mind with another living Titan and offer to envelop it. His single immense body then flows over that Titan as one continuous, form-fitting gel suit, leaving only its face exposed while Humongous's own blinking eyes sit above the opening; awareness transfers into the suited Titan until the bond is released, at which point control returns to Humongous. The Kraken is the deliberate exception to total enclosure: the continuous suit covers its central mantle and frames its face, but all twelve tentacles emerge uncovered from beneath it.
- The hero's health does not return on its own. Food restores it in amounts reflecting the food's rarity, while an inn restores the whole party. If the hero's own HP is exhausted—even while awareness is inhabiting Blorbus, Xiao Hou Zi, Manchego, or another controllable companion—the world goes black and the hero faints. They lose half their carried tokoins but keep every item, achievement, blorb, and party companion, then awaken as they did at the beginning: eyes closed, opening them before rising. They wake at the last inn used within that kingdom, or at that kingdom's entrance if they have not rested there; the outskirts instead returns them to the magical clearing. Xiao Hou Zi remains invulnerable (see the jungle biome, below, for why). If a directly controlled Blorbus melts, awareness returns immediately to the living hero rather than remaining with his melted core.

## Blorbs

Creatures the player finds and befriends, apart from the one settled giant
that remains in the wasteland.

- **Ordinary people and blorbs:** most regular people do not understand what blorbs truly are, cannot communicate with or command them, and do not own them as pets or livestock. They regard blorbs as strange, mostly harmless wildlife that may wander through a field, shelter beneath a porch, or gather near water before leaving on its own terms. Blorbs are not bought, sold, bred, tethered, or assigned to ordinary households. The hero's growing party is exceptional: Blorbus's awakened Psychic presence enables the mutual communication and coordination that makes genuine companionship possible. Any other person shown travelling with, wearing, or directing a blorb must have an explicit story reason rather than treating blorb ownership as commonplace.
- **Normal type** blorbs: a classic slime silhouette — a smooth, continuously-curved dome/blob body (no flat-cut geometry), sagging low and wide like a water balloon settled under its own weight rather than a symmetric dome. Light, neutral off-white in color, with individual blorbs varying in subtle shade. Rendered with a viscous, gooey, slightly translucent material. Two large, round eyes.
- **Size type blorb**: a single immense resident farther out in the northern wasteland beyond the main plateau, named **Humongous**. It is at least fifty times the size of a normal blorb, broadly flattened and deeply settled into the ground as though gliding across its surface. Its enormous mass makes every movement exceptionally slow, and on its own it glides without ever hopping. While Blorbus is psychically merged with it, however, deliberate control can make the entire giant jump with the same squash-and-stretch motion as a smaller blorb. Once more than half of a person's body has entered its viscous mass, they are slowly lifted to its surface, where they can walk across the giant's goo and make a superjump three times higher than a normal blorb jump; it never gives the ordinary blorb trampoline bounce. Blorbus can carry Humongous between kingdoms inside his core, but that relationship is unlocked by their first conversation and travel requires Humongous to have physically entered the core first. If he has never been carried, he remains free at home while the party visits a spoke kingdom and is still free when they return. A Humongous deliberately carried into and then released within a spoke kingdom is recalled into the core when the party returns to the Crossroads, avoiding a persistent trail of giant bodies across worlds; an eligible active mind merge can continue through a gate crossing.
- Every blorb carries a **core**: an apple-sized, many-faceted object suspended inside its body, visible through the translucent flesh. Pale and opalescent in a normal blorb.
- Giving a blorb an elemental gem transforms it into an elemental blorb of that type: the gem is thrown into the blorb, and it merges into the core through some pocket-dimension property of the core itself — no matter how large the merged object actually is, the core simply takes it in. The world stills during the brief transformation: the blorb shines white until the light fills everything, then the glare clears to reveal its new form. The merge changes both the core's glow and the blorb's whole body: a water merge turns the body a deep, viscous blue; a fire merge turns it molten orange-red, like slow-moving lava.
- Fire and Electric blorbs specifically give off real light from their core, not just a color change — a small, steady glow bright enough to notice in the dark. The other elements don't share this trait.
- The player starts the game with three companion blorbs, which stay nearby and follow once the player wanders far enough away. Whichever of the three is still ungemmed once the other two have taken an elemental gem turns pink and wakes up as **Blorbus** — see Protagonist, above.
- **Wild blorbs**, found out in the world, cannot join the party before Blorbus awakens; his sentient Psychic presence is what makes befriending and coordinating additional blorbs possible. After that awakening, a wild blorb that the player approaches notices them and starts tagging along after the party's own blorbs rather than the player directly. After it's spent enough time actually traveling with the party (not necessarily all at once — falling behind and catching back up doesn't undo the progress), it asks whether it can join. Blorbs love being bounced on, so landing on one quickly strengthens this bond. Once the player owns the corroded pocket compass (see the antique shop, below), it points them toward the nearest not-yet-partied wild blorb so they can go find it — the compass doing the elemental-tracking job its own lore already describes, rather than a glow on the blorb itself. Finding every wild blorb currently out in the field doesn't end the hunt: a fresh few appear elsewhere shortly after, so there's always another one for the compass to point toward.
- Most wild blorbs found out in the world are plain, unmerged Normal blorbs. A smaller number are found already naturally elemental — encountered already merged, rather than merged by hand with a thrown gem (see below). The electric blorb (found anywhere in the open field), the canyon biome's rock blorbs, the jungle biome's plant blorbs, and the volcano's fire blorbs (see Setting, below, for all three) were all met this way.
- **Shiny blorbs**: a rare wild variant, cosmetic only and not tied to any element — a shiny blorb carries no elemental power and can never accept a gem. It's a plain blorb with a distinctly bright, glossy, pearlescent finish rather than the ordinary off-white body most wild blorbs have. Only a handful exist in the field at once, against many plain wild blorbs, so encountering one is meant to read as a genuine rare find.
- **Levels and experience**: every blorb begins at Level 1 with its own natural variation in Strength, Defense, HP, and MP. Dealing even one damaging attack to an NME marks that blorb as a participant in the encounter, and a blorb worn on the player's own body counts as participating too, for as long as its own power is actively drawn on (streaming, or hovering) during the fight — not merely for being worn. When the NME is defeated, its XP reward is divided among every participant. Melting resets all of that blorb's current combat contributions, so it receives no XP from an earlier hit even if it reforms before the NME falls; landing a fresh hit after reforming can establish a new contribution. Harder NME types carry larger XP rewards. Each new level raises Strength and Defense and increases maximum HP and MP — HP and MP grow with the blorb's level itself now, while Strength governs how hard that blorb's own attacks land and Defense how much incoming damage it shrugs off (see Elemental Gems, below, for how an attack's own element factors in too). The Blorbs menu shows the current level and an XP bar filled toward the next one, and a passive message announces each level-up without interrupting play.

### The Blorb Suit

- A called trampoline companion can answer beneath either the hero or a directly controlled Blorbus. Fire blorbs can make that crossing over lava as well as ordinary ground.
- Free Water blorbs are true three-dimensional swimmers and can follow companions throughout the water column. Air blorbs cannot fly underwater and remain buoyant at the surface; an Air chest likewise grants flight only after its wearer leaves the water, never diving.
- A sealed Diving Helmet combined with two Rock blorbs worn as legs lets the hero walk directly along the ocean floor at a slightly reduced pace instead of swimming freely.
- A blorb's Speed stat never changes the hero's ordinary walking or running speed or animation cadence, nor a free blorb's ordinary movement. It contributes only while worn for special traversal: both equipped legs power skating, swimming draws from the worn head and leg blorbs leading the swimmer, chest flight draws from its Air blorb, and limb-powered flight draws from its leg blorbs. The contribution is deliberately exaggerated during map testing and will be reduced for progression balance later.
- Armor can be thrown into a blorb and bound into the matching equipment slot in its core. The blorb then reshapes itself into an equivalent living armor form when worn: the Dented Breastplate becomes a broad medieval blorb cuirass designed in the same visual language as the Knight's Helm. Bound armor adds to that blorb's own Defense while it is free, and the hero inherits the same complete effective Defense while wearing it. Protection varies with the object's rarity and power: practical equipment such as the Diving Helmet gives a modest boon, ordinary steel armor and the Knight's Helm give stronger protection, and unique relics such as the Nautilus Crown and especially the Lava Helm are stronger still. A blorb can bind only one head modification and one body-armor modification at a time. The **Bird Helm** is a further such head item -- permanently bound to Blorbaka's own core rather than ever thrown or found loose (see False Hero, under The town, below) -- whose worn shape is a small hemisphere capping just the top half of the head, with a hooked hawk's beak at the nose and small wing decorations where the ears sit. Wearing it grants ordinary head-armor protection like the others, but its real purpose is unique to it: it's the only way to see or reach the Sky Kingdom at all (see The Sky Kingdom, below). The **Penguin Helm** is an Ice-only head item: a sleek dark hood that closes smoothly over the whole head down past the jaw, with a slender beak pointing forward from just below the eyes. An Ice blorb that binds it wears that same hooded, beaked shape, eyes on either side of the beak.

- **Formed suits.** Some helms command a whole suit of their own element. The Lava Helm seals a complete Fire suit into living lava armour (see Fire Kingdom, under Kingdoms, below). The **Leaf Hat** commands a complete Plant suit the same way: a single broad living leaf worn crosswise over the crown, curling upward at both sides, its stem-to-tip axis running front to back, with a soft vine wrapped physically beneath it and down under the chin. Worn as the head of a full Plant suit, it lets the wearer throw that vine as a real, load-bearing line onto anything tall enough overhead and swing from it. Jumping is the whole of it: jump with something high enough in reach and the vine takes hold, so a wearer crossing a jungle leaves the ground by doing what they would have done anyway. The vine grows from whichever hand throws it and reaches its anchor as it is thrown. Swinging is a true pendulum on a line that cannot stretch, so the arc is the wearer's own weight and speed and nothing else. Near the top of an arc the free hand throws the next vine toward the next support and the spent one retracts back into the hand that cast it, so a grove or a lined street can be crossed hand over hand without touching the ground. Jumping again lets go mid-arc, keeping every bit of the momentum already gathered, and the next jump throws for whatever is in reach. With nothing high enough within reach, the throw simply finds nothing. The Penguin Helm, worn as the head of a complete Ice suit, can form the **Penguin Suit** on command: pressing all four limb buttons together forms it, and doing so again unforms it. Until then the helm rides raised on the head blorb's ordinary hat form, whose crown is rounded rather than pointed, with large eyes and a short, broad-based beak tipped slightly up where a nose would be; forming the suit brings it down over the face as the sleek hood. In the Penguin Suit the torso blorb grows even larger than the Lava cuirass into one chubby, egg-shaped body from the ankles up past broad, rounded shoulders, sloping smoothly into the hood, whose own flared base sinks into the body like a penguin's head running into its neck; the body is fattest low down at the belly and stops just above the ankles, with a broad, flat tail jutting from the low back and tipped toward the ground. Both arm blorbs flatten into penguin flippers with rounded shoulder caps that narrow to a point past the hands, and both leg blorbs shrink to flat penguin feet beneath the hem. The suit's ice runners withdraw, but any glide carries on: on ice the penguin keeps sliding on its feet, the waddle only a weak push. On foot the wearer waddles like a penguin, slowly, in short quick steps, the whole body leaning over whichever foot is planted, flippers held a little out. Pressing Jump on ice launches a fast, low, horizontal dive; landing on the ice, they shoot along on their belly as a penguin does, face lifted forward off the ice, steering a little as they go, until the slide runs out or Jump hops them back up onto their feet. Losing any piece returns the rest to their ordinary shapes at once, just as the Lava Suit unseals. A head blorb holding the Diving Helmet likewise wears a rounded crown on dry land, a small sign that it has another form.

- **The Nautilus Crown and the mermaid tail.** A blorb that binds the Nautilus Crown becomes a shell helmet: a thick, rounded cap sitting on the head from just above the eyes, the blorb's eyes looking out from its brow, with a solid nautilus spiral growing from its top and coiling back and up behind the head. Like the Diving Helmet it lets its wearer breathe underwater. Worn over two Water leg blorbs, it merges them while swimming into one mermaid tail: a single mass from above the hips, tapering smoothly to its narrowest at the ankles, then a broad two-lobed fluke that wraps both pointed feet. Both blorbs keep their own eyes and cores, down by the feet. The wearer swims with the legs together, ankles meeting and toes pointed back, kicking in unison like a dolphin, far faster than ordinary swimming; sprinting and Water jets still add to that. Back on land the legs part again.

- **Crystal Skates.** A rare pair of crystal blades, each bound into an Ice leg blorb (one per leg; they do nothing on any other element). Worn as a pair, they show as glowing crystal runners and lay a track of magic crystal ice ahead of their wearer wherever they skate: the track runs a few metres in front, bending smoothly toward wherever the skater looks and steers, climbing into the air or diving back down, so the skater can go anywhere in three dimensions on a ribbon of ice they make as they go, far faster than on ordinary Ice skates. The ice never dips below the ground or any surface beneath it, and the skater's body tips with the track, back on its climbs and forward on its dives. Behind them the track melts away a few seconds after it was laid, the melt catching up to where they stand but never out from under their feet. A jump leaves the track, keeping its speed. The formed Penguin Suit has no skates, and the Crystal Skates do nothing while it is formed.

The player can call their whole party onto their own body at once, and dismiss them again just as physically — each blorb visibly hops across the ground and up onto its spot rather than appearing there, and hops back off to land scattered around the player when dismissed. The most recently bounced-on available companion can also be called directly beneath the hero as a moving trampoline aid, even during a fall. Landed, a blorb flattens into one flexible covering over a body part: a full arm from shoulder to hand, a full leg from hip to boot, an oval covering the torso, or, on the head, an inflated spherical blorb helmet enclosing the whole head like a deep-sea diver's air supply. Every covering keeps its own blorb's elemental color and core, the core still sitting just behind its own eyes, except that a worn Rock blorb visibly hardens into the same rough stone substance as the world's natural rocks while retaining its living face and core. A worn head blorb allows underwater swimming; paired Water leg blorbs remain part of the swimmer's suit, and their separately controlled downward water streams lift the hero into a level hover while both fire together. In the water the same Water limbs work like the Fire suit's jets instead: while swimming, at the surface or diving, each Water hand or foot that fires shoots its stream straight back behind the swimmer and drives them forward, each one adding more speed, even with no direction held. Out of the water they behave as usual. A single Fire hand projects its flamethrower forward, while two active Fire hands lower beside the hips and fire downward to hover; adding two simultaneously firing Fire feet turns that hover into free directional flight. Fire feet otherwise provide downward jets without lift of their own, but each compounds the speed of chest-Air-blorb flight while firing. Paired Air feet passively hover and retain the normal walk/run movement while traversing in the camera's full direction, including elevation. Air limb powers also form silent temporary cloud platforms: an arm makes one ahead at shoulder height, while a foot makes one immediately beneath the wearer. Paired Fire leg blorbs also protect the hero well enough to walk across lava; removing either Fire leg while over lava immediately returns the hero to the pool's edge. Free Fire blorbs can travel directly over molten lava, while every other free blorb—including Blorbus under direct control—refuses bare lava but can safely occupy rock or another solid surface rising from within it. Continuous elemental jets draw on the individual blorb's power reserve and stop when it is depleted. A worn Electric hand crackles with a continuous, erratic shock rather than a directional jet — a hand-to-hand arc more than a beam — and anything it actually hits freezes in place for a moment, unable to move. A worn City hand crackles with that same kind of arc, tinted a cooler blue rather than Electric's own yellow, and instead of a freeze it leaves whatever it hits hastened but weakened — faster on its feet, but its own attacks land softer, for a few seconds after. A worn Rock hand calls up solid crags that can carry the player and companions, strike NMEs as they emerge, and grow in number, size, damage, and persistence with the blorb's level; a Rock leg raises one directly beneath the hero as a platforming skill. Rock and Ice slabs recognize existing temporary slabs as valid foundations, allowing repeated powers to form towers. A worn Plant hand fires individual seed pellets one after another at a steady, unhurried pace, rather than any continuous stream. Free-roaming Electric, City, Rock, and Plant blorbs fight with these same four powers on their own when defending themselves, exactly as free Water and Fire blorbs already stream their own elements. Paired Ground leg blorbs grow a single broad wheel between the feet, its axle spanning heel to heel, lifting the hero to ride it rather than walk — the wheel scales even the steepest climbable ground without hesitation, and rolling off a rise or a canyon lip launches the rider into a true fall, momentum intact, until gravity brings them back down. Adding a paired set of Ground arm blorbs on top does nothing by itself; only while the hero also holds down both arm powers together, with the leg wheel already active, do the raised arms grow a second wheel between the hands, and the whole body leans forward into a hunched riding crouch until that front wheel meets the ground alongside the rear one. Releasing either arm power drops the front wheel and stands the hero back upright at once.

- Any equipment or traversal form created by reshaping a blorb's own body—such as Ground wheels or a Snow snowboard—keeps that blorb element's exact body hue. This rule does not apply when a power summons separate environmental matter: Air platforms are actual clouds and therefore retain the world's natural cloud color rather than the Air blorb's body color.

### Checkpoint portals

- A **checkpoint portal** is an upright ring of piping in a super-egg outline, tall enough for the hero to walk through (in the Demo World, so wide it spans most of the valley floor and cannot be missed), its opening filled with a swirling, space-like membrane tinted the body colour of one element's blorbs (off-white for Normal blorbs, warm gold for shiny Normal blorbs). Its rim is not solid; it is a threshold rather than an obstacle.
- Portals are one-way and stand in back-to-back pairs at the border between two biomes, facing apart. Each biome's portal stands on the side you enter that biome from, its face turned toward you, and its blorbs wait just beyond it inside that biome, idling and wandering close by as if expecting the hero rather than following him.
- A portal can also bring a single blorb that takes over just one part of the suit (the Demo World's seabed portal brings a Nautilus Crown head), leaving the rest worn; it comes off with the rest at the next full swap.
- Walking, riding, jumping, flying or swimming through a portal's face with most of the body swaps the hero into that portal's suit: the blorbs he is wearing hop off, and the waiting blorbs join the party and hop on. Nobody is left behind: the blorbs that hop off stay in the party and keep following, so the party grows with every portal, and passing back through an earlier portal simply puts its blorbs back on. Passing through a portal from behind never does anything, so crossing a border changes suits exactly once, in either direction.
- Where a suit has a helm, its head blorb carries it: the Diving Helmet for Water, the Lava Helm for Fire, the Penguin Helm for Ice, the Toboggan for Snow, the Bird Helm for Air, and the Leaf Hat for Plant.
- **Space blorbs** are purplish-black elemental blorbs stippled with small white star points. A Space blorb can bind the **Space Helm**, forming a sealed astronaut-like visor when worn on the head; together with a Space chest blorb reshaped into tanks and hoses, it supplies breathable air in vacuum. A complete six-piece Space suit provides directional jets and inertial movement in zero gravity. Blorbs themselves need no air, and Blorbus propels himself psychically in space. The demo introduces them through a horizontal portal high above its volcano and includes a pressurized spacecraft branded **ASAN — Singapore Product Design**. Its cockpit controls let a party member take the ship itself as a temporary piloted body, maneuvering it inertially through space and returning awareness to the pilot upon leaving the controls. Mounted on the ship's flank is a spherical **escape pod**, reached through an opening only a blorb is small enough to pass. Once Blorbus is aboard it seals, counts itself down and drops away, flying him to the ground beside Humongous and arriving hard enough to leave a crater in the clearing that stays there afterwards. The full Space Kingdom, a physical moon, and the ASAN flag and planting story are deferred.

## Demo World

- A standalone world outside the story's geography: one long, naturally irregular island that follows a gently bending route through a biome for every blorb suit with a clear traversal power, divided by checkpoint portal borders, under the Crossroads' own sky, light and fog. Its cliff-sided flanks continue down beneath an immense spherical blue sea rather than ending at a rectangular terrain boundary; from high flight, that sea and the matching blue lower horizon make the course read as part of a small world. From west to east: the Crossroads' own clearing, with a flat-floored pit punched deep into it where the hero wakes with his five Normal blorbs and Xiao Hou Zi (Normal); its walls are sheer. The way out takes one high bounce off a blorb, onto a rock platform on the east wall, from which a short stair of rock steps, each a small hop, climbs to the rim. On the plain above, Manchego and Pandy wait stationed beside the shiny portal and join the party when the hero passes through it. Beyond it lie forest plains of the Crossroads' own trees, undergrowth and flower meadows, where wild shiny blorbs roam and can be recruited (Shiny, a suit of shiny Normal blorbs); a substantially longer patch of the Primate Kingdom's own jungle (Plant); a stretch of the Ocean Kingdom's own sea, where the island widens out and sandy beaches lined with palms, banana trees and tropical undergrowth shelve away into deep water over a rolling seabed of kelp meadows and coral reefs, its water beginning just past the portal and running to the next border, with one palm island rising from it; the Ocean Kingdom's twelve-tentacled Kraken patrols its deep middle, crown riding just under the surface, as it does in its home sea (Water). A third of the way through the water, a portal stands on the seabed; through it a single Water blorb waits wearing the Nautilus Crown, and it takes over the head alone, leaving the rest of the suit on; a long lake frozen solid to its bed, set in a snowfield, running from its portal to the foot of a great snow mountain, then that mountain's steep western face, climbed to its summit plateau (Ice); a third of the way along the ice a crystal portal stands on the frozen lake, and through it two Ice blorbs bound with Crystal Skates take over the legs, to skate a crystal track up the mountain; from the summit, a groomed snowboard course winding between raised berms down the mountain's steep eastern flank to its foot, with rough, tree-scattered mogul ground either side (Snow); a stretch of the Rock/Ground Kingdom's rugged terraces, washes and halfpipe canyon surrounding a continuous, gently weaving dirt-bike circuit; the circuit replaces any route-crossing canyon with broad rollers, banked turns, and deliberately paired jump faces and landings while climbing steadily to cloud height (Ground); the dirt circuit ends at an organic cliff lip high among the clouds, whose edge wanders in and out rather than ruling a straight line across the valley, and falls to a chasm floor that still stands well above the surrounding sea (Air); in that floor lies a lava lake with an organic shoreline, shelving into its own basin, running the length of the chasm until a full-size Fire Kingdom volcano rises straight out of its far end, the volcanic body itself serving as the lake's far shore with no wall or seam between them, its mouth brimming with lava (Fire). The Fire portal stands between the lake and the volcano. The gate into the Air stretch is three times the usual size, easy to hit flat out on a dirt bike. All snow on the island is snow-white. The island crown rolls throughout, and its lakes lie deep in basins punched well below it. It exists for demonstrating and testing each suit, switching between characters and riding, and will grow as suits and biomes are added.

## Elemental Gems

- Used to turn a normal blorb into an elemental blorb (thrown into it; see the merge mechanic above). Some blorbs are also found already naturally elemental out in the world, without ever needing a gem thrown at them — see Blorbs, above.
- Elemental types confirmed so far: **Water** (blue), **Fire** (orange/red), **Electric** (yellow, crackling), **City** (a saturated, glassy azure — a cooler cousin of Electric's own crackle, deliberately more saturated than Air's own pale, wispy sky-blue so the two don't read as the same thing), **Rock** (warm brown-grey, shared exactly by natural rocks, hardened Rock-blorb armour, and Rock constructs), **Ground** (a distinct, darker loamy brown — its own element, not a synonym for Rock), **Air** (a naturally hovering blorb with wings), **Plant** (leafy green), **Ice** (pale glassy blue-white), **Snow** (near-white, matte), and **Wood** (warm cedar brown).
- Physically, gems are small (apple-sized), faceted, crystalline objects.
- The Fire Gem and the Electric Gem are both bought from the antique dealer in town, using tokoins.
- The Water Gem isn't sold anywhere; it rests in the fountain at the center of the plaza, free for anyone who finds it.
- The Rock Gem and the Ground Gem are both found out in the canyon biome (see Setting, below) rather than sold — the Rock Gem lying on the canyon floor, the Ground Gem waiting atop the biome's tallest hoodoo.
- The Air Gem is not sold; it waits on a landing cloud at the top of the sky parkour course above town (see The town, under Setting, below).
- The Plant Gem is not sold; it waits atop one of the jungle biome's own trees (see Setting, below).
- The City Gem is not sold either; it's found somewhere among the outskirts city's own streets (see Setting, below).
- The Ice Gem and the Snow Gem are both found out in the Ice Kingdom (see Kingdoms, below) rather than sold.
- The Wood Gem is not sold; it's found only in the Wood Kingdom, the abandoned reveal tucked inside the Plant Kingdom (see Kingdoms, below).
- **Blorb Slime is blorb excretion.** Blorbs, only occasionally and rarely, excrete it: a wild blorb sometimes leaves a small glossy puddle behind that a forager can gather. The Tree of Life, which shares the blorbs' own nature, also produces it and can be harvested. No one makes, brews or otherwise harvests it by any other means, so every jar a vendor sells was foraged in the wild or taken from the Tree. It restores a blorb's body and wakes the Dinosaur's bones.
- **Type effectiveness**: Fire, Plant, and Water form a closed three-way advantage loop — Fire burns Plant, Plant drinks Water, Water douses Fire — and each pairing is handicapped in reverse (Plant is weak against Fire, Water against Plant, Fire against Water). Wood extends this: Wood beats any water-coded target (Fish Goblins included — see the Ocean Kingdom, below), and Plant in turn beats Wood, the same handicapped-in-reverse rule as the fire/plant/water loop. An attack of the stronger type in a matchup deals extra damage; the weaker side's attack deals reduced damage. Every other element (Electric, City, Rock, Ground, Air, Ice, Snow, and Psychic) is neutral against everything, including each other, for now.

## Tokoins (currency)

- The game's currency.
- Physical tokoins appear in the world as large coins (dinner-plate sized) standing up on their edge, found lying around for the player to collect.
- Collecting one physical tokoin adds **10** to the player's tokoin currency total.
- Spent on goods from town vendors, including elemental gems.

## Setting

- Across the wilds and elemental kingdoms, naturally transformed blorbs live alongside ordinary untransformed blorbs rather than replacing them. Roughly half of each biome's otherwise elemental wild population remains ordinary, making elemental blorbs distinctive discoveries while keeping plain blorbs part of every ecosystem.
- **Built circulation:** inhabited architecture throughout the world favors ramps over conventional staircases. Any usable upper storey has a real opening cut through the floor, a ramp broad and gentle enough for people and ordinary blorbs to traverse, adequate head clearance throughout its rise, a landing at each end, and guards wherever an exposed edge would otherwise be dangerous. A ramp never terminates against an unbroken ceiling or pass through a wall or structural beam. Large civic, royal, and lodging buildings may use switchback ramps around an open stairwell; compact houses may use one straight wall-side ramp or an exterior covered ramp where climate and culture make that more plausible. This is a normal architectural convention of the world, not a special accessibility retrofit, and it is why multi-storey interiors remain traversable to the hero's unusually shaped companions.

- **World orientation**: the sun rises in the **east** and sets in the **west**. Maps use that convention: the town lies toward the east of the world, while the canyon/mesa biome lies toward the west.
- **The wasteland**: the low-lying area surrounding the main plateau.
  - The wasteland north of the plateau is home to the sole known Size type blorb: **Humongous**, an enormous, slow-moving non-hopping giant.
  - East of the plateau, the wasteland opens into a vast lake. Its waterline sits below the surrounding desert, exposing a narrow natural beach along the upper basin slope. The plateau's eastern cliff falls into the submerged basin; beneath the water, the old wasteland slopes down through the bowl before rising again at the distant mountain foot.
  - Lake shells and freshwater clams collect around the shore and shallows, while harvestable lakeweed grows across the deeper basin floor. The fishing village's shops buy all three.
  - The lake's fishing village is a small working settlement on piles and floating decks, anchored among steep limestone islets that rise from submerged shoulders in the otherwise deep basin. Its primary built precedent is the Clan Jetties of George Town, Penang: a working access jetty and mooring spine from which stilt houses, sheds and side walks accumulate in a fishbone pattern. Its siting follows the stilt settlement around Ko Panyi in Phang Nga Bay: two steep limestone islets give shelter and fixed anchors, piles stand on their shallower shoulders, and the village spreads over the water around them, leaving open boat channels. The larger, flat-crowned **Anvil Rock** forms the village's back wall; the slender **Heron Rock** stands at its eastern end and carries the Vale household's fishing works and the Ocean Kingdom gate's landing. Rain filtering through Anvil Rock emerges as a clean seep at its foot, which the village gathers with roof runoff into a covered cistern rather than drinking lake water. A steady breeze usually blows from the west, down off the Ohio plateau, so the smokehouse and drying racks stand on the downwind eastern edge. It borrows the relationship between livelihood, water access, geology and settlement growth, not the real communities' clan, ethnic or religious organization. Like Ohio and the Snow Village, the fictional community is cosmopolitan. Its households arrived by different water routes and share a culture built from fishing, boat keeping, freshwater mussel and pearl cultivation, hospitality and mutual maintenance rather than a single ancestry. Family houses, work decks, drying and repair shelters, storage, a guest houseboat and a modest communal pavilion branch from the public jetty spine through shaded walks and flexible gangways. Small working boats belong at real moorings with clear routes to the lake. The settlement retains the working draft's lively terracotta, teal, ochre, violet, coral-red and green color range, arranged into coherent household and boat palettes rather than reduced to uniform brown timber. At least three broad public swim ramps are distributed around its occupied clusters; each runs flush from its deck down to about the human player's feet in the normal surface-floating pose, providing an immediate, unobstructed route out of the water for every playable body.
  - The fishing village has fifteen residents in five households. Dala's generation drove the first permanent piles beside the largest limestone islet. The Mor houseboat returned seasonally before taking a permanent mooring; the Vales began as seasonal fishers from the far shore; Mateo arrived aboard a damaged cargo boat and stayed; Asha and Rian stayed after working one storm season. **Nara Venn**, the lake diver, merchant and dockwarden, lives at the arrival landing with her husband **Mateo Venn**, the boatwright and pile carpenter, and their adopted sixteen-year-old **Lio**, who apprentices with them. **Mai Aran** tends the freshwater mussel and pearl beds (its pearls are ordinary cultivated valuables with no magical quality) with her fisherman and net-maker husband **Salim**, Salim's mother **Dala**, a retired captain and weather reader, and their eleven-year-old daughter **Pree**. **Jori Vale** captains the broad fishing boat; their husband **Osei** sorts and preserves the catch, and their nine-year-old son **Tavi** studies with the other children. **Leena Mor** keeps the communal pavilion and guest houseboat with her husband **Ivo**, the ferryman and Ohio cargo carrier, and their adult daughter **Sela**, a utility-boat pilot, diver and water rescuer. Siblings **Asha Sen** and **Rian Sen** share the house beside the pavilion: Asha teaches the children, keeps the village records and treats injuries, while Rian makes rope and shellwork and seals Mateo's Diving Helmets. The village has no hereditary chief; shared decisions bring one adult from every household to the pavilion, while specific safety authority belongs to the people doing the relevant work.
  - The Mor family's permanently moored houseboat is the fishing village's inn equivalent and its functional rest point. Its covered arrival deck opens into a common cabin and galley; a central party dormitory, two small guest cabins, the Mor family's private stern cabin, and a contained dry toilet occupy the roofed vessel behind it. **Leena Mor** charges the ordinary Crossroads rate of 10 Tokoins for a night. Resting there restores the party and melted blorbs, begins the next morning, and wakes the hero in the dormitory through the standard bed sequence while the party gathers in the common cabin and covered deck.
  - The wasteland floor is gently undulating desert scattered with loose rocks rather than a perfectly flat plain. The main plateau's sides are interrupted by occasional lower grassy shelves, with flat ledges capable of supporting bushes and other vegetation.
- **Starting field**: the grassy field where the player wakes up from their coma. The immediate magical clearing is a calm, level safe area, kept free of hostile encounters and dense wilderness clutter in the same spirit as the protected ground around towns and cities. Clusters of tiny single-shape stemless petals lie directly on the ground in an organically broken fairy ring halfway between the clearing's centre and perimeter. A small, shallow mound of mild brown prepared earth accepts the **Seed of Life** when it is thrown there: it takes root immediately, appears as a sprout on the next morning, and becomes the **Tree of Life** on the following morning. Once mature, it produces one harvest of rare **Blorb Slime** each day. Blorb Slime heals blorbs, is valuable enough to be stocked by only some merchants, and is also the substance that resurrects Dinosaur (see Rock and Ground Kingdom, below).
- **Ohio, the starting town**: a small, old farming and milling village near the starting field. A readable sign on the arrival road has a symbol-only carved face with no printed world lettering; examining it says, “Welcome to Ohio. Our village is awesome.” Ohio grew around a spring-fed fountain and the meeting place beside it rather than from a perfectly radial plan. The fountain stands on a two-step stone apron within a planted village green, with benches turned toward it, shade trees and low flower beds around the quieter edge, and a parish notice board where the market meets the meeting house. The arrival road passes the inn before widening into the green; the market faces it, quieter homes sit along two short lanes behind it, the windmill and grain yard occupy the exposed western rise, an east road runs past the smithy to a stone overlook on the plateau's rim where the village goes to watch the evening over the lake, and the village's water comes from a spring-fed tarn high in the plateau's northern hills, a long walk from the green and out of sight of it. The tarn is a genuine bowl in the hills, a body of water you can swim in with the hill itself for its banks, and only a low pair of curved stone cheeks marks the sill where its overflow leaves. Paths are light packed earth worn into the ground, never paved or laid as slabs. They form one continuous circulation network joining each public entrance, working yard, service door and garden gate, fading into ragged margins rather than radiating geometrically from the fountain. Buildings present their principal entrance and public windows to the green or a connected lane; private doors face gardens, and working openings face their actual service yards. The aqueduct is a stone-curbed channel on a masonry causeway holding one continuous body of water: it leaves the tarn's sill, drops down a chute past a timber sluice to the level bench where the sawmill stands apart from the village, turns east along the terrace past the mill's undershot wheel, and runs straight on to the plateau's sheer edge nearest the village, where the spent water goes over in one clean fall to the great lake far below. A long worn mill road climbs from the green's north lane over the hills to the mill door, which stands on the village side of the water, so no bridge is needed. Every substantial building has a known use and occupant; there are no anonymous filler houses or decorative mills and fences with no work to do. Ohio's ordinary buildings use continuous plaster and stone walls with timber-framed upper storeys; stacked-log construction belongs to the Snow Village and is not part of Ohio's architectural language.
  - **The Holt Inn** stands on the arrival road, close enough to the green to serve market visitors without occupying the market itself. A symmetrical two-post open timber porch frames a broad double entrance, leaving its central bay unobstructed. Because Ohio has a mild climate and the porch already shelters the threshold, that entrance opens directly into a generous arrival zone in the communal dining and fireside room rather than passing through a sealed vestibule. Its large windows look both toward the village and out to the countryside. Its broad, shallow masonry fireplace is bonded into the gable wall and rises as one chimney through the roof. A broad wall-clipped superellipse opening turns the fireside into a double-height hall, with a curved upper gallery carrying heat and light upstairs. Two separately locked rooms flank the chimney, with usable landings between their doors and the hearth rail. Only the south cache room has a parkour entrance around the open end of its wall. Its chest contains the key, visibly alongside Fire Gems and Blorb Slime, and each object must be picked up from the open chest. After returning to the gallery, the same key opens both distinct locks: first the cache-room door, then the sole door into the fully enclosed north room. Hollis waits there with a second chest containing boxing gloves, another Fire Gem and Blorb Slime, and rewards the player with 10 Tokoins after his door is unlocked. The bathroom and service storage occupy enclosed corners rather than dividing the public floor in half. The remaining upper rooms open normally from the landing; beds are not exposed in an undivided loft. A kitchen and service yard sit behind it. **Mira Holt** owns the inn and keeps watch over the entrance from the reception counter. She is an early riser who also sweeps the green nearest her door each morning, which is why she is forever complaining about dust returning to the paths. Her practical hospitality makes her the first person villagers seek when a stranger arrives, although she refuses to treat gossip as fact until she has heard it twice.

- Doors in inhabited procedural buildings are physical hinged objects, not permanently open decoration. Interacting with a door opens or shuts it; closed doors carry matching collision. Most Ohio doors begin open, while authored story doors may begin shut and locked. Chests use the same physical-interaction convention: their lids swing open and shut, and stable chest ids make loot collectible only once across travel.
  - **The meeting house and archive** is the village's principal civic building, facing the broad side of the green. It contains one large meeting room, a smaller records room, and the village bell hanging in a small open gablet on its front roof slope. It is dignified but not palatial: a shallow porch, timber columns, and a modest pediment distinguish it from a residence. **Halda Prewitt** is the elected reeve and market steward. Direct, observant, and difficult to impress, she settles boundary disputes, assigns market pitches, and keeps the village's common accounts here. Her familiarity with the antique dealer and his evasions comes from granting his licence and recording every unusual object he brings through town.
  - **Petra Voss** keeps the meeting house's little schoolroom and archive. She teaches the children of the village and nearby farmsteads in the morning, copies records for Halda in the afternoon, and privately wishes village history contained more excitement than roof repairs and grain tallies. Her fascination with the amnesiac stranger is therefore sincere, but she is also the person most likely to write down an accurate account of what follows. She occupies a compact teacher's room at the back of the meeting house rather than owning a separate unexplained cottage.
  - **Cob Ferris** is the village grain miller. He lives in the millhouse beside the large windmill on the western rise, where steady open wind reaches the sails. The windmill is a walk-in, two-stage smock mill in Ohio's stone-and-oak vernacular: the lower stage houses the stones and meal collection, while the timber upper stage carries the windshaft, brake wheel, wallower, upright shaft and spur gearing. The hopper feeds grain through its shoe to the driven runner stone, and finished meal falls through a chute into a bin and sacks. The adjacent millhouse is not a second mill. It is Cob's home and the grain-receiving, weighing, storage, bagging and cart-loading side of the same business. A fenced grain yard, loading apron, cart shelter, and granary complete the compound. Cob inherited both the mill and his weather lore from his grandmother. He dismisses her superstitions aloud while quietly planning his work around every one of them.
  - **Dorran Fask** is the carpenter, joiner, and practical builder responsible for most recent repairs in town. He operates Ohio's water-powered timber sawmill, which stands on a graded bench north of the village on the town side of the tarn's race, near the cliff where the water leaves the plateau. The race turns an undershot wheel at the north drive wall. Its axle enters the building, drives a faster crank gear, and an offset pin and connecting rod move the complete frame saw up and down over a rail-mounted log carriage. Unsawn logs enter from the yard; sawdust accumulates at the cut; squared boards leave on the output side. The hearth and chimney occupy a gable wall so neither can be mistaken for the wheel's drive. Timber racks, log piles, and a real work yard in front of the mill door make clear what enters and leaves the sawmill; Dorran and Pip walk the mill road each workday. Dorran built the upper storey of the home he shares with his spouse, Halda Prewitt, and has been dissatisfied with its roofline since shortly before their wedding. Halda has deliberately never told him which part is crooked.
  - **Oswin Cray** is a retired hedger, ditcher, and field hand who still tends the settlement's living boundaries when his joints permit. He lives in an old cottage at the edge of the residential lane with his grand-niece **Ivy Thorne**. A wild ordinary blorb occasionally chooses to sleep beneath the porch, but Oswin neither owns nor understands it and expects it to leave whenever it pleases. The cottage's enclosed kitchen garden and woven hedge are working boundaries, not decorative fence fragments. Oswin distrusts grand theories about elemental energy because he remembers blorbs as familiar but inscrutable creatures around the fields; his gruffness conceals considerable respect for their independence.
  - **Ivy Thorne** assists Oswin at home but spends much of her time observing the ordinary blorbs that congregate near the fountain. She is the village's self-taught blorb naturalist: not yet an expert, and sometimes too eager to repeat an unproven account, but the first local person to notice that gems change a blorb's whole nature rather than merely its color. She keeps sketches and attempted names in the loft room above Oswin's cottage and helps Petra teach natural lore when asked.
  - **Wren Sallow** serves as the village's boundary warden and herbal gatherer. Her small cottage stands nearest the hedge line, with a drying shed and a fenced physic garden rather than an ornamental yard. She walks the outer paths at dawn and dusk, repairs gaps Oswin can no longer reach, and knows which silences beyond the cultivated ground mean that ordinary wildlife has moved away. She is not needlessly mysterious; she withholds frightening guesses until she has evidence, which makes her warnings more serious when she gives them.
  - **Tam Ruskin** is the mason and keeper of the spring works. He maintains the fountain basin, drains, bridge abutments, millrace stonework, chimneys, and the stone lower storeys of the village buildings. His workshop and home stand at the north-east edge of the village where the mill road leaves the east road, so the road to the water works begins at his door, with stone samples and lime kept in a contained rear yard. Tam knows the fountain's carvings better than anyone alive but refuses to declare one inherited story authoritative when the physical evidence does not settle it. He and Wren are close friends whose different patrols of stonework and hedgerow make them the town's informal early-warning network.
  - The **antique dealer is Aldren Vey**, an outsider who settled in town years ago because old roads, abandoned farmsteads, and travellers from the surrounding wilds continually bring him inexplicable objects. His licensed stall faces the green; a narrow house and secure storehouse sit immediately behind it, so the wares do not imply an owner with nowhere to live. Aldren is charming, evasive, and genuinely knowledgeable about provenance, although he sometimes prefers a better story to an incomplete fact. He sells the Fire and Electric Gems but does not pretend to understand their origin.
  - The **armorer is Brinna Kest**, a former caravan guard who now repairs farm tools, tack, cookware, and modest travelling armour at a smithy on the noisier edge of the market. Her red-canopied display is the sales frontage of that working forge, not a disconnected outdoor table. She lives in a small upper room over the workshop and tests every weapon she is willing to sell; the dents and notches in her stock are disclosed, not disguised.
  - The **provisioner is Nell Barrow**, whose bakehouse and storeroom stand on the green's west edge, a short walk along the arrival road from the inn, its door turned to the green (her green-canopied stall stands on the market row by the green). She buys grain from Cob, fruit and herbs from nearby farms, and cheese from outlying herders, then sells durable road food beneath the green canopy on market days. The oven chimney, rear loading door, dry store, and handcart make the building's trade legible. Nell and Mira coordinate meals and lodging but maintain a friendly argument over whose bread visitors remember.
  - The remaining small dwellings belong to households already represented by these people rather than to invisible generic residents: Dorran and Halda's overhanging timber house, Oswin and Ivy's hedge cottage, Wren's warden cottage, and Tam's mason's house. Mira, Petra, Cob, Aldren, Brinna, and Nell live within or above their places of work. This keeps the physical village proportional to its actual population while still leaving room for the nearby farm families and children who visit for market or lessons without pretending that every one of them permanently lives inside the compact center.
  - **Ties between the villagers.** Mira and Nell keep a standing exchange: Nell's loaves arrive at dawn and Mira passes on spare broth for Nell's evening customers; they argue publicly about whose bread is remembered and cover for each other in private. Tam and Wren share a Thursday supper, alternating houses; Wren brings herbs for Tam's lime wash and Tam keeps her drying shed sound. Cob and Brinna trade in kind: she mends his mill irons and iron-shod sack carts, he pays her in flour. Petra and Ivy hold a Saturday "blorb watch" at the fountain with the pupils. Aldren lodges the odd travelling buyer at Mira's inn and never haggles in front of her. Halda settles which of Dorran's saw and Cob's wheel takes the millrace first, at the green, and every party abides by it.
  - **Trade in Ohio is traceable.** Nell Barrow bakes at the Barrow Bakehouse from Cob Ferris's flour and sells the loaves at the green stall; Tess carries the loaves out and brings cheese in from the north farm, and Dorran Fask carries dried berries in from the east-field farms. Ivy Thorne forages Blorb Slime where wild blorbs gather by the fountain and leaves jars on commission at the end of Nell's board; Nell keeps a cut for the space. Brinna Kest sells what she forges at the smithy, with pitch from Dorran's sawmill in her torches. Aldren Vey sells road finds and traded curios from the Vey storehouse.
  - **Pip, Tess and Wick** are the village's named young people, none of them a stranger and none of them a new household. **Pip** (about eleven) is Dorran's apprentice from an east-field farm; he sleeps in the sawmill loft on workdays and goes home on market day. **Tess** (about nine) is Petra's pupil and Nell's bread runner, from a north farm connected to Nell's late husband's family; she walks the lane to the bakehouse each morning. **Wick** (about twelve) is Ivy's fellow blorb-watcher at the fountain and carries grain sacks for Cob. Other farm children still come only for market and lessons.
  - **A day in Ohio.** Mira sweeps the green at dawn. Nell has baked before it and sells from her stall mid-morning. Cob grinds from the first wind. Wren walks the hedge at dawn and dusk. Petra teaches in the morning and copies records for Halda in the afternoon. Brinna's forge runs from mid-morning until dusk. Halda works the hall and then the market. Dorran works the saw with Pip. Tam checks the channel and the fountain. Oswin tends his hedge and garden. Ivy sits at the fountain watching blorbs. Aldren opens late. Yogi rounds the warm places. On market day farm families and pupils fill the green, and at harvest a long supper is laid out on it under lanterns.
  - **Yogi**, the white-and-orange cat, is nominally Mira's inn cat, although she sleeps in Cob's grain sacks, Brinna's warm smithy, and Oswin's garden often enough that nobody seriously claims exclusive ownership.
  - Scattered around the open ground between buildings are a handful of small "floating" crate staircases -- optional parkour, not required to reach anything. Set apart from these, a single spiraling staircase of cloud platforms climbs all the way up into the sky, continuing as a hand-placed chain of large walkable clouds and ending on one wide landing cloud that holds the **Air Gem** -- see The Sky Kingdom, below, for why this entire climb is invisible and untouchable until the Bird Helm is worn.
  - **False Hero**: introduces himself as "the Hero of the Village, Savior of Earth" the moment he's met, and accuses the player of reeking of the Demon King's own mirror world -- an act; he's secretly working against the player, not for the village. Tall and heavily built, every segment of his own body plain skin-toned except an off-white brief at the hips -- what's left once his "heroic" look, entirely a worn blorb suit (two Water blorbs on his legs, two Fire blorbs on his arms, and a Psychic blorb named **Blorbaka** on his head), is stripped away. Blorbaka carries the **Bird Helm** bound into her own core (see The Sky Kingdom, below) from the start, which is why the false hero's own head reads as helmed rather than a plain hat. He only appears once the player could field a full blorb suit of their own (see the Blorb Suit, above) -- before that, and after he's dealt with, the town's villagers have nothing to do with him at all. Confronting him starts a real fight: he cycles between his own flamethrower and a hovering retreat on alternating Water legs or Fire arms, the same suit-power mechanics the player's own worn blorbs grant, but he never leaves the town itself to give chase. Defeated, his entire suit falls away and his five worn blorbs -- Blorbaka among them -- land nearby as real, wild, joinable blorbs the player can then befriend like any other; acquiring Blorbaka this way is also how the player acquires the Bird Helm itself, since it's never sold or found loose. He himself flees on foot and doesn't get far -- see Aggros, below, for what catches him.
- **The outskirts city**: a separate, larger settlement elsewhere in the wilderness from the town's own small village — a full-scale avenue/cross-street grid lined with houses, distinct from the town's plaza-and-market layout. It once had a matching City Kingdom gate nearby; that kingdom has since been removed, but the city biome itself, unaffected, has no gate of its own.
  - A handful of wild **City blorbs** live scattered through its streets, found already naturally elemental the same way the canyon's Rock blorbs and the jungle's Plant blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
  - A **City Gem** is hidden somewhere among those same streets.
- **The canyon biome** (also called the **Mesa Biome**, same landmark): a large banded-sandstone rock formation out in the wilderness, distinct from the grassy starting field and the smaller rocky outcrops scattered nearer town. Striped orange/rust/tan/white rock, a slab-paved canyon floor, towering stacked-slab hoodoos, and a natural rock arch. Its paving and shelves are true physical ground for the hero, NMEs, and blorbs alike rather than scenery resting above a separate movement surface. A dramatic climbable landmark, not tied to any plot event yet. The warp to the **Rock and Ground Kingdom** (see Kingdoms, below) stands within the biome, off to one side of the Mesa Tower.
  - Its tallest hoodoo — the **Mesa Tower** — sits at the biome's own center, notably taller and more tiered than the others around it. Reaching the **Ground Gem** waiting on top means climbing it tier by tier, real jump-to-jump parkour rather than a guaranteed path up.
  - A **Rock Gem** rests on the canyon floor elsewhere in the same biome — a shorter find for a player exploring on foot, not gated behind the tower climb.
  - A handful of wild **Rock blorbs** live scattered across the canyon floor, found already naturally elemental the same way the jungle's Plant blorbs and the volcano's Fire blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
- **The jungle biome**: a separate, smaller plateau of its own, southwest of the main plateau — a lush, thick, varied landscape distinct from every other biome. Its sloping sides are broken up by small, flat, grass-capped shelves at varying elevations, with bushes and grass growing on the ledges. Its trees run larger and taller than the ones found elsewhere, and they grow to genuinely varied heights rather than to one shared ceiling: within a single species some stand at ordinary canopy height while others push well above their neighbours, and scattered through the whole biome are true emergents whose crowns clear the canopy altogether. An emergent carries heavy limbs spiralling up its bare upper trunk, each reaching out and slightly up with leaf clusters at its end, solid enough to stand on and solid enough for a thrown vine to catch. This layering is what makes the jungle swingable anywhere in it rather than only along a route. The species below: palm trees (trunks ranging in height with a natural lean/curve), shorter banana trees bearing banana fruit, durian trees bearing large, naturally spiky durian fruit, sprawling banyan trees, stout baobab trees, flowering trees, and hanging vines strung between the canopy.
  - Its resident is a small monkey named **Xiao Hou Zi**, of the **stuffed animal monkey** class (see Plant Kingdom's own jungle villagers, below, for the other member of that class, and the taxonomy note there for how it differs from the world's other primates). He roams the plateau on his own until a player wanders close, at which point he leaves off wandering to come find them specifically. Stick around him long enough and he'll join the party — following the player the way a bonded blorb does, though he is his own companion rather than a blorb himself. Once recruited, the player can also take direct control of him the same way they can with Blorbus. He'll talk if approached, with a loose, chatty handful of lines about the jungle, its fruit, and the vague things "the older vines whisper."
  - Xiao Hou Zi is a beloved stuffed animal who has come to life, and that is why he is invincible: he takes no damage and never runs out of breath, so he can stay underwater indefinitely.
  - A handful of wild **Plant blorbs** live within the plateau, found already naturally elemental the same way the electric and rock blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
  - A **Plant Gem** waits on top of one of the plateau's own trees, for a player willing to climb up after it.
- **The volcano**: a landform out in the open wasteland, well clear of the main plateau, the lake, and the jungle plateau alike. Larger in footprint than the jungle plateau, deliberately so -- a walkable volcano cone needs more room to climb gently than a flat-topped plateau does. Its outer flank climbs gradually from the wasteland floor up to a broad, organically round crater rim, walkable the whole way; a shorter, steeper (but still climbable) inner wall then drops from the rim down into the crater itself, opening onto a level, walkable ring of solid ground that circles a large, round pool of lava at the very center, glowing and textured like a fire blorb's own molten body. A handful of wild **Fire blorbs** live on the outer flank and in the crater itself, found already naturally elemental the same way the electric, rock, and plant blorbs are (see Blorbs, above). The warp to the **Fire Kingdom** stands on a broad basalt outcropping emerging from within the crater's lava pool rather than on the shore (see Kingdoms, below).
- **The Ice Kingdom's gate**: stands on its own naturally raised snow plateau in the northwestern wasteland. The plateau is formed by the same continuous terrain as the surrounding world, with a white snow-coloured surface and a broad walkable slope rather than white shapes laid over the ground. Snow and Ice blorbs live around the gate among frost-tinted pines and rocks.
- **The Chinese village**: a large settlement out in the northeast wasteland, built across many compact floating islands above the **Abyss of Impending Doom**. Each island has a broad, genuinely level grass crown for village life, rocky cliff sides, and an irregular tapered underside like a torn-away floating plateau. Long plank bridges cross meaningful gaps between the islands, including a level approach from the surrounding terrain. Each smaller residential island holds only one or two enclosed houses with a single doorway; the large central imperial island holds a spacious four-level golden castle whose successively smaller storeys form a stepped pyramid. Broad perimeter terraces and alternating side stairs connect real landings without crossing the palace walls. Inside, lacquer-red columns, benches, a central ceremonial carpet, raised golden throne and dais, and rows of hanging *deng long* give every level the visual hierarchy of an imperial hall. A formal flowering garden and stone paths frame its entrance. The palace's fully enclosed rear annex is the Royal Kitchen, connected directly to the throne hall by an open doorway; this is where the Emperor initially sends the blorbs after mistaking them for meat buns, watched over by the Royal Chef. Trees grow naturally around the village and bamboo gathers in loose, irregular groves. Its ordinary-human-scale buildings have tiered red-and-gold pagoda roofs and lanterns, and its villagers speak Chinese. Unlike the town, it has no portal gate and belongs to no kingdom; it is simply a place in the world.
  - **The children.** A handful of kids play outside, apart from the adult villagers, and have built their own small stick play hut. One of them wears her hair in pigtails.
  - **History and households.** The islands are the pieces of one plateau that broke away over the Abyss. Families settled them three generations ago and bridged them, ruled from the largest piece by the Emperor's hereditary line. The palace's yellow glaze is the old shipped stock that nobody nearby can fire any more, which is why ordinary homes use local grey and green tile and why the palace roofs cannot be mended. By the old bargain the palace takes a modest share of beans, flour and bamboo shoots and keeps the bridges and lamp posts in repair. The Royal Chef arrived a few harvests ago with a single recipe and rose to the Emperor's ear; since then the Emperor's hunt for the perfect *rou bao* has raised the share every year, and the land taken falls first on the bamboo island. Nobody rebels: the palace still keeps the bridges, the Emperor is distracted rather than cruel, and no ordinary resident knows the Chef is behind it. Ohio, due south across the wasteland, is the one settlement within reach. The carrier Bo Xiang walks there for flour and salt and brings lanterns, beans and bamboo goods back to sell.
  - **The five children and their families.** Ting (pigtails; daughter of Hua Chen, the village's bao maker), Xiu (daughter of Jin Wei, granddaughter of the lantern maker Mei Lian), Pei (granddaughter of the waymarker Shu Mei), Rong (son of the carrier Bo Xiang) and Bao (child of the tiler Yun Tao, who wants to be a roofer like Wen Zhao). Tian Bo farms the bamboo island with Pandy. 林静 keeps the island's inn, a family business of three generations, and Chen the peddler lodges there. Everyone in the village speaks Chinese. Each adult works a trade that supplies the palace or the stall: lanterns and lamp paper, tile and the last of the glaze, buns, carrying, the entry-bridge lamps, roofs, beans, the inn.

After the quest, the palace ceases to be a private imperial residence. Tian Bo opens its halls, gardens and grounds as the village's civic centre, and Liang Zhen uses the former Royal Kitchen as a community kitchen.

He is slightly taller than the hero and wears a formal continuous red robe with gold trim, dark boots extending up beneath the robe, a high court bun, a long beard emerging from his chin, and a red square ceremonial crown with ten hanging gold cords at both its front and back. The Royal Chef is secretly an agent of the Demon Lord who has been influencing Liang Zhen into unjust rule, including Tian Bo's grievance over escalating demands on his land. Liang Zhen discovers a genuine love of cooking and becomes the community chef, using the former Royal Kitchen to prepare meals for everyone. Every quest stage, the palace's changed use, the developing relationship, and Pandy's place in the party persist across world travel.

- **The Sky Kingdom**: a place in the world rather than a gated kingdom (see Kingdoms, below, for how those differ) -- a small cluster of cloud islands sitting among the world's own ordinary sky clouds (the same clouds visible from the ground every day, not a separate higher layer), each with its own quartz-and-gold pavilion. Ordinarily invisible and untouchable, not merely hidden behind distance or a locked door: it is "totally turned off" for anyone not wearing the **Bird Helm**, a core-bound item permanently attached to Blorbaka's own core (see False Hero, above, for how she's acquired) rather than ever sold or found loose. Only while a blorb wearing the Bird Helm is worn on the hero's own head does the Sky Kingdom become visible, solid, and reachable at all -- there is no other route in. Its own gateway is the town's existing spiraling cloud staircase (see The town, above, for the crate-and-cloud climb that otherwise leads to the Air Gem): that whole climb, the Air Gem included, is invisible and untouchable the same "totally turned off" way without the Bird Helm, so putting it on for the first time is what actually reveals the staircase up out of the ordinary sky clouds. The Sky Kingdom's own islands begin right where that climb tops out and extend onward from there, away from the village. It is populated by the **Tempestars**, a peaceful people with an ordinary humanoid upper body but no legs: below the waist their bodies are living cloud drawn into a slow, swirling vortex, on which they float freely from cloud to cloud, the way merfolk continue into a fish tail. They drift and idle around their own cloud in the same unhurried, independently-timed way merfolk do. They are not gods but a people of the sky, as the merfolk are of the sea and the lava people of the volcano, and they live invisibly in the clouds, slightly out of phase with ordinary sight. They age and have children, but their lives are very long and children are rare. Their culture echoes the human world's images of a heaven above the clouds -- most strongly Greek Olympus and the Roman heavens, with the Chinese Celestial Court of Journey to the West a substantial second influence -- without any worship or religion attached. Their food comes with almost no labor, gathered from sun, dew and cloud in the manner of the feasts of myth, so they have little need of work and spend their long lives on leisure and intellectual pursuits: music, verse, games, gardens, conversation, stargazing, weather-shaping and fine craft. That same freedom is their weakness: nobody needs anybody, status replaces livelihood, and every small court considers itself the centre. They build with cloud, gold and quartz in ways that defy ordinary physics, using cloud itself as a material for floating furniture, rooms and props. Rather than one central monarch, many small Tempestar rulers each preside over just their own single cloud island -- comparable in spirit to the Chinese Emperor, but scaled down to "the little ruler of a tiny kingdom of one cloud," and it shows: each ruler treats their own cloud as a point of pride against the others rather than as part of one shared realm, and more than one privately admits the arrangement reads more like a string of small exiles than an actual kingdom. The largest island, **Aethra**, is ruled by **Aristeon** and keeps two attendants, **Mnesia** and **Alkis**; three smaller satellite clouds -- **Chrysa**, **Lyria** and **Koinon** -- are ruled respectively by **Aurelia**, **Elegon**, and **Sophrona**.
  - **Working court register and culture (proposed, in review).** The second design pass (`docs/architecture/sky_kingdom.md`) proposes twenty-one Tempestars in six courts, each tied to an office of the mythic heavenly courts. **Aethra**, the court of state, adds the cupbearer **Drosia** to Aristeon, Mnesia (chamberlain and keeper of ledgers) and Alkis (herald, the only Tempestar who still carries invitations between clouds). The **Chrysa**, the court of craft and weather and the kingdom's only goldsmiths, adds the apprentice **Kallix** and the weather-shaper **Keraunia** to Aurelia. The **Lyria**, the court of music and verse, adds the musician **Aulia** and the keeper of songs **Rhapsos** to Elegon. The **Koinon**, the court of learning, adds the stargazer **Astrion** and the archivist **Archeia** to Sophrona; its "hollow" is the empty **Hall of Assembly** at its centre, where all the courts once met and none has convened in living memory. Two new courts are proposed: the **Pantao**, the court of the feast, under the hostess **Xiangyun**, with the master of the table **Tianlu** and the orchard keeper **Chunlu**; and the **Dromos**, the court of games, under the judge **Nikandra**, with the racer **Tachys** and the course keeper **Stephane**. Xiangyun's daughter **Mingyue** and Nikandra's son **Dromeus** are the kingdom's only children. Their cuisine: **nectar** condenses as golden dew only on gold bowls set out at dawn; **ambrosia** is bread of noon cumulus kneaded with nectar and baked in sunlight; **golden peaches** ripen a few at a time on cloud-rooted trees, and the season's first is a matter of fierce precedence; small self-moving **golden tripods** carry dishes between tables. Construction follows one rule: cloud gives volume, gold holds it, and quartz carries weight. Cloud is used as natural bank cloud (the ground), woven cloud (awnings, curtains, sails, robes) and set cloud (floating couches, steps and small moored rooms that hold their shape only where gold binds them); roofs float above their colonnades, columns hang short of the floor and stairs drift as separate slabs. The kingdom's arrival is the open **Dawn Gate** at the top of the town's cloud staircase. Through it Aethra stands straight ahead and highest, its Hall of Mist on the axis; the other courts float in a loose ring around Koinon, which sits low in the middle with its empty hall open to the world below. Pantao floats level with Aethra's foot, close enough to be seen competing; Chrysa is lowest, under its own flickering storm crown; Lyria floats high and apart; Dromos is a flat disc ringed by its racecourse. The courts stand 70 to 110 m apart in open sky (proposed layout: `docs/architecture/sky_kingdom_layout.md`). Plants root directly in cloud and grow from its essence, their trunks, branches and leaves made of the same cloud material as the islands, with colour only in fruit and blossom: cloud peaches, weeping cloud willows, tiered pines, clipped puff shrubs, cloud wisteria, vines hanging beneath the islands, and sky lilies on pools of set cloud. Nobody is a merchant and Tokoins mean nothing there; the courts part with nectar, ambrosia, peaches, gold work and woven cloud only as swaps for novel curiosities from the ground world, as contest prizes, from Pantao's open leftover table, or as gifts of favour.
  - **Sun Wu Kong and the courts (proposed, in review).** Long ago Sun Wu Kong was king of the monkeys of the Primate Kingdom's jungle. He rode the Jindouyun up into the courts uninvited and demanded to be received as a king among kings. The cloud rulers would not treat a jungle monkey as their equal: they named him **Great Sage Equal to Aethra**, a title with no cloud, court or duties, and set him to guard Pantao's peaches to keep him busy. Left off the guest list of the great **Peach Banquet**, he ate the season's golden peaches, drank the nectar, wrecked the banquet and Dromos's games, and fought the courts' champions to a standstill with the Jingu Bang. All six courts then met in the **Hall of Assembly** on Koinon -- the only time in living memory they acted together -- and sealed him beneath a rock on the crown of the Emperor's castle below, binding the seal so that only one of his own monkeys could lift it, which is why Xiao Hou Zi can free him. The gold sealing decree is the last decision on the Hall's walls, and the courts have not met since. That was long ago; the Air blorbs vanished only recently. The Air blorbs' disappearance has nothing to do with him. The courts are simply furious with him, as heaven is in his legend, and have never forgiven the insult, the peaches or the banquet; when he turns up beside the hero, freed, every court is outraged to have him loose in their sky again, and he is not sorry. The Primate Kingdom's monkeys have had no king since he left, and their villagers still tell of the Monkey King who went up into the sky and never came back.
  - **Restored harmony.** The return of the Air blorbs restores harmony among the courts. Seeing the flocks drift between the clouds again makes the Tempestars realise how distant and frivolous they had become, and the courts meet in the Hall of Assembly again for the first time since Sun Wu Kong's sealing, this time for something better.
  - **The missing air blorbs.** Wild Air blorbs (see Blorbs, above) are conspicuously absent from the Sky Kingdom itself, where they would naturally belong -- several Tempestars remark on it unprompted, in their own words, without any of them naming a cause. The actual reason is the Demon King's own doing (see The Demon King's mirror world, above): he has vaporized the Sky Kingdom's own Air blorb population directly into the surrounding clouds, another instance of him feeding on a kingdom's elemental life the way he already does to the Plant Kingdom's monkeys. Restoring them is intended to be the Sky Kingdom's own central plot quest, not yet designed -- see Open threads, below.

## Kingdoms

Beyond the outskirts — the starting field, town, wasteland, and biomes described under Setting above — lie separate **kingdoms**, each reached through its own portal gate rather than by walking there directly.

- Blorbus's psychic power is what makes the trip possible: a gate only opens for a player who is directly piloting Blorbus (or Humongous while merged with him after Humongous has previously entered Blorbus's core), never for the player's own body or while riding along as a companion. Walking a piloted Blorbus through an open gate triggers the crossing.
- Arriving in a kingdom sets the player down near a twin gate that leads back to the outskirts, so the same psychic crossing works in reverse.
- The party (bonded blorbs and Xiao Hou Zi) and the player's inventory both travel with the player across a crossing.
- Five gates are confirmed so far. Plant, Ocean, Fire, and Ice each stand in an outskirts biome or plateau that thematically matches them; the Rock and Ground Kingdom's gate stands inside the canyon biome. The former **City Kingdom** gate (a repeat of the outskirts city's own avenue-grid layout, no named districts/NPCs/plot content) has since been removed — the outskirts City biome itself, its wild City blorbs, and its City Gem (see Setting, above) are untouched by that removal.
  - **Plant Kingdom** — gate stands in the jungle biome, Xiao Hou Zi's home. First-pass content: undulating jungle ground spanning out to the mountains ringing it, under a thick jungle canopy as dense as the crossroads jungle biome's own, broken up by a handful of open clearings, rocky outcrops, and a winding river carved into the ground (mirroring how the crossroads lake sits pushed down below the surrounding land). The kingdom's first village is a handful of giant emergent trees (a new towering tree species with climbable branch-ramps spiraling up their trunks, each landing sprouting its own small leaf-tipped twigs) with small treehouses mounted on every third landing, up and down each trunk. These treehouses are open platforming structures rather than enclosed ground houses: each uses a single central timber pillar, exposed sides, wood construction, and a broad green leaf roof so the climbing route remains open. Has its own day/night cycle. Populated by named jungle villagers, apes, and "primate template" monkeys (see below) standing on treehouse decks or wandering the village clearing, by wild blorbs of several types (including a scatter of naturally elemental Plant blorbs) roaming the jungle floor, and by a single giant gorilla living out in the wider kingdom (see below), well clear of the village itself. Named for its primate residents' own home terrain rather than the primates themselves — see Primate taxonomy, below, for why the residents keep their original species names regardless.
    - **Jungle villagers**: a second primate species distinct from Xiao Hou Zi in build, but the same **stuffed animal monkey** class as him — larger and taller, with no yellow cheek markings and no heart-shaped facial marking, hips set a bit closer together, proportionally longer limbs that taper less toward the hands/feet, no foot pads, and a torso that reads less sharply pear-shaped than Xiao Hou Zi's own. Individuals vary in height, limb length, tail length, and fur color, which ranges from dark charcoal through grey and light grey to shades of brown. Ambient wildlife: each individual now has their own name and their own two lines of dialogue on "Talk," the same way the outskirts town's villagers do, but still no join/recruit mechanic.
    - **Apes and "primate template" monkeys**: the village also has a smaller population of the world's other two primate classes (see Primate taxonomy, just below) roaming the same ground clearing as the jungle villagers, each with their own name and two lines of dialogue the same way. Ordinary village size range for both (not the giant gorilla's own landmark scale, below).
    - **Primate taxonomy.** The world's primates split into three classes: **monkeys** (Xiao Hou Zi, the jungle villagers, and the village's own "primate template" monkeys, all the same tailed body plan at different scales/species) always have tails and stay comparatively small; **apes** never have tails and run noticeably larger, with essentially no size overlap between the two size classes — an ape's smallest is still bigger than a monkey's biggest. Apes and "primate template" monkeys share one underlying rig (a build-time flag toggles the tail and the size/proportion range), while the jungle villagers/Xiao Hou Zi are their own separate "stuffed animal monkey" rig.
    - **Kova Kong**, a single male giant gorilla living out in the wider kingdom, away from the village — comparable in scale to Humongous (see Blorbs, above), and, per the same design idea, moves in a proportionally slowed, lumbering way rather than covering ground quickly despite his long strides. His true name is **Da Hou Zi**, and he will be revealed as Xiao Hou Zi's big brother. He begins with unnaturally purple eyes because the Demon Lord is controlling his mind; the story beat that frees him is not yet established. His articulated body is physical terrain the hero can climb and stand upon, turning the route up him into creature-scale parkour. Carries a **Special Banana** conspicuously at the top of his head — see Rewards and local quests, just below, for what it's for.
    - **The curse.** The kingdom is under a curse: whenever a new baby monkey is born, the kingdom's monkeys are reduced to ashes. Their shadows survive the ashing, since shadows can't burn. This is how the Demon King feeds off the Plant Kingdom (see The Demon King's mirror world, above). Lifting the curse is the kingdom's central plot quest, tied to the player's overall fight against the Demon King.
    - **Rewards and local quests.** Once the curse is lifted, the monkeys thank the player by confirming Xiao Hou Zi's place in the party for the rest of the game, on top of the informal recruit that already happens back in the outskirts jungle biome (see Blorbs, above, and Xiao Hou Zi's roam/approach/recruit behavior). A separate local quest begins with **Ossian Redbrow**, a reddish-furred ape encountered riding the horse **Manchego** (idling in the village, not following the player, and not yet rideable by the player himself) around the primate village. Per direct instruction, the quest itself is a deliberate placeholder pending real design: bring Ossian the Special Banana from the top of Kova Kong's head (see just above), and he dismounts, hands Manchego over as a real party mount, and rejoins the village as an ordinary roaming NPC. The quest's real content/story (why the banana, why Manchego, what it means) is still unestablished — see Open threads.
    - **Training dummy.** One of the kingdom's side quests unlocks a new Aggro type, the training dummy (see Aggros, below).
    - **The Wood Kingdom.** An abandoned reveal tucked inside the Plant Kingdom itself, rather than a separate gate — a cluster of weathered, long-emptied treehouses, greyed and dulled rather than the rest of the village's lived-in color, populated by wild **Wood blorbs** rather than any primate residents. The abandoned houses retain the same traversable open architecture as the living primate village: exposed sides around one weathered central wood pillar, with muted green leaf roofs rather than ordinary enclosed house walls. It doesn't exist on a first visit to the Plant Kingdom; it only appears once the player has already visited the Ice Kingdom (see below), then returned. Why visiting the Ice Kingdom specifically is what uncovers it, and who abandoned it, is still unestablished — see Open threads.
  - **Rock and Ground Kingdom** — gate stands within the canyon biome (see Setting, above), off to one side of the Mesa Tower. One kingdom spanning two existing elements at once, the same way the Ice Kingdom (below) spans two of its own. Its eastern Ground half remains a broad dirt landscape containing Dinosaur's sunken fossil basin and hardy scrub. Its western Rock half grows into layered, climbable banded-sandstone terrain—terraces, steep cliff bands, winding dry canyons, hoodoos, arches, slab fields, boulders and a broken mountain rim—using the same natural language as the Crossroads canyon biome. Wild **Rock blorbs**, **Ground blorbs**, and interspersed Normal blorbs inhabit the realm. A protected town occupies a broad level shelf, with enclosed earth-and-stone homes, individually named residents, a communal stone well, and a working inn. In the fossil basin lie the immense scattered, collapsed skeletal remnants of **Dinosaur**, a titanic dinosaur unique to this world (not a real-world species) at the same titan scale as Humongous and Da Hou Zi, standing and walking on all four legs when alive. Throwing Blorb Slime onto the remains rapidly draws the scattered bones back into their complete quadruped anatomy before the slime spreads across them and restores Dinosaur as a living, walking, physical titan; that resurrection persists across travel. The cliffs and canyon walls exist for exactly the climbing traversal a paired Ground-leg suit (see The Blorb Suit, above) is built for — Rock blorb-suit powers grant no comparable climbing ability of their own.
- **Ice Kingdom** — one kingdom split deliberately between Ice and Snow. Its Ice half holds a vast irregular frozen lake with water beneath a solid, visibly thick surface, an exact round fishing hole farther out on the ice beside Ivar's hut, and large glacier peaks rising as continuous terrain rather than placed rock props. Its Snow half contains the protected village and a great resort mountain set well inside the kingdom, with a complete alpine back shoulder and basin beyond its summit rather than an empty world edge. A working two-lane chair lift climbs a quieter shoulder from the village's base plaza without the ground being artificially raised into a ridge or carved into a trench beneath it. Three groomed snowboard pistes fan down separately from the summit: a broad, gently winding bunny route, a narrower turning intermediate route, and a steep advanced fall line. Natural pines, exposed cold-rock bands, shrubs and winter growth gather in irregular pockets between the clear pistes. Soft snow yields slightly beneath a traveler's feet without changing ordinary movement, while lake ice carries momentum. Pale ice-aspected skeleton NMEs rise in the wilderness but neither appear inside nor pursue anyone into the village. Named human villagers dress in long sleeves, long trousers or skirts, and gloves. **Solveig Woolcap** keeps the village's winter-goods stall, selling provisions and the **Toboggan**, a thick knitted head item with a folded band, a crown tapering to a pinched top, and a soft pom-pom. A blorb that binds it takes on that same winter-hat silhouette when worn, with its living eyes situated on the crown just above the folded band. Wild **Ice blorbs**, **Snow blorbs**, and interspersed Normal blorbs favor their corresponding halves. Ice blorbs share Rock blorbs' power pattern, raising temporary solid ice formations instead of stone; Rock blorbs cannot draw stone through the frozen lake surface. Beneath the frozen lake, near the fishing hole, lies a sunken ice-fisher's skiff with a carved penguin figurehead on its prow. A frost-rimed chest rests on its floorboards, glowing faintly cold, and holds the **Penguin Helm**: swim down through the hole to find it. A great alpine cedar fell from the bank on the village side of the lake long ago and broke through the ice there, leaving a gap of open water ringed with broken ice slabs. Its trunk runs from its root ball on the shore down into the water and under the ice, so a swimmer who surfaces in the gap climbs out along the trunk. Whose skiff it was, and why the helm was sealed in it, is unestablished. Visiting here for the first time also uncovers the Wood Kingdom back in the Plant Kingdom (see above) — why is still unestablished.
    - **The snow village** is a compact working mountain settlement clustered in the lee of the lower slope, close to the chair-lift station but clear of the descending snowboard run. It is laid out like a Norse farm-hamlet grown into a small modern mountain town: an irregular ring of houses round an open common snow yard (the tun), with a carved symbol stone on a low turf mound where Elin speaks for the village, a fire ring with log seats and a stone-curbed cistern at its heart, the inn as the long hall on the yard's north side, a raised stabbur storehouse on stone piers, and a boat and net house at the foot of the lake road. The chair lift's lower station and the end of the bunny run sit on the village's north-west corner, in a base plaza between Niko's workshop and Elin's hall, clear of every wall. It is organized around that sheltered common snow yard rather than a formal paved square. Narrow, gently curving lanes of light trodden earth, worn into the snow and never paved, connect the inn, rescue hall, lift workshop, homes, textile shop, wood yard, bathhouse, and lake trail; buildings turn their smallest walls toward the prevailing wind, with covered porches and entrance vestibules preventing every opened door from emptying heat into the street. Steep snow-bearing gables, stone foundations, deep eaves, enclosed wood stores, and chimneys make the climate legible. Every building has a function and occupant rather than being one identical cabin assigned to one wandering NPC.
    - **Astrid Snowrest** keeps the large village inn beside the lift approach. It serves as lodging, public dining room, and the warm gathering place for travellers returning from the mountain. A covered porch enters a mudroom and equipment-drying room before the common room. The common room is vaulted and gathered around a masonry hearth centered on a gable-end wall; its chimney breast rises continuously with that end wall and the flue clears the ridge. A service counter opens toward a real kitchen and pantry instead of dividing the building arbitrarily. A quieter sleeping wing contains several believable guest beds, the party dormitory, and Astrid's own room beneath a flat insulated ceiling with storage loft above. A short rear corridor reaches an indoor dry-toilet room: an insulated seat over a sealed masonry compost vault, ventilated by its own stack above the roof, avoiding freeze-prone water plumbing and avoiding an exposed midnight walk to an outhouse.
    - **Elin** is the elected village steward and head of mountain rescue. She keeps the rescue and meeting hall beside the lift base, where weather reports, missing-traveller searches, slope closures, and communal decisions are coordinated. Its bell and broad equipment porch distinguish it from a house. Elin's remark about snow softening every sound comes from listening for distant calls and avalanches during searches, not simple sentimentality. She occupies a small steward's room within the hall.
    - **Niko** is the village carpenter, roofer, and chair-lift mechanic. His workshop stands directly beside the lower lift machinery with covered space for timber, replacement chairs, cable tools, and roof repairs. He built much of the village's steep roof framing and is the person who jokes that the roofs wear winter better than their owners. His compact living room occupies the sheltered rear of the same building rather than requiring an unrelated residence.
    - **Anja** is the forester and trail keeper. She manages selective cutting in the surrounding pine woods, marks safe winter trails, clears stormfall, and supplies seasoned firewood to the communal stacks. Her home and wood yard sit at the village's outer edge nearest the forest, enclosed by a practical windbreak fence. The pine needles she finds carried across the mountain help her read the high wind before it reaches the village.

Their stove, worktables, wool storage, fitting space, and private sleeping rooms explain the building's larger footprint. Freya sets lines, cleans and smokes the catch, and is more willing than Ivar to admit when something beneath the fishing hole is watching them. A smokehouse and net shed complete their yard.
    - The **communal bathhouse and washhouse** occupies the remaining public building near the inn and wood supply. A hot room, washing room, changing vestibule, floor drain, and gable-end stove give the village a credible place for regular bathing without pretending every small cabin contains extensive plumbing. Ordinary homes use compact enclosed rear service lean-tos with ventilated dry privies or covered night vessels; detached outhouses exist only for work yards used by day. This keeps sanitation believable in severe winter while reserving the inn's larger indoor toilet room for guests and accessibility.
    - **Log architecture** uses whole-wall construction rather than a visible grid of prefabricated square cells. Each horizontal course runs continuously from corner to corner wherever it meets solid wall. At a window or doorway, a course stops cleanly at one side of the framed opening and resumes on the other; courses above the lintel continue uninterrupted. The triangular gable ends use the same logs, each higher course shortening naturally to meet the roof pitch. Alternating courses extend into interlocked corner joints. Windows are sparse, glazed, deeply framed, and fitted with appropriately sized shutters. Doorways are framed structural gaps in the courses, with real plank doors shown open or closed rather than slabs pasted over an unbroken wall.
    - Hearths are Scandinavian and sit against a gable-end wall, each one mass with one flue clearing the ridge: the inn and rescue hall have a broad open **peis** under a corbelled masonry hood; homes and bedrooms have a tiled **kakelugn** stove with an iron door and a dark cornice; the bathhouse has a **kiuas**, an iron sauna stove under a cage of heated stones. The inn has a second tiled range for its kitchen at the far gable. Beds have legs, side rails, headboards, recessed mattresses, pillows, and blankets; they read as furniture made for sleeping rather than two rounded slabs stacked like confectionery.
    - Two-storey lodges, workshops, and homes follow the world's ramp convention: a broad interior timber ramp rises through a correctly sized opening in the upper floor, with sheltered landings, useful space beneath where clearance permits, and rails around the well above. The snow village's ramps receive transverse grip battens and shallow side curbs so wet boots and sliding blorbs remain controllable. Every occupied floor sits behind full-height log walls with real windows and the attics above are unoccupied, so the village has no dormers. Small cabins, sheds, icehouses, and smokehouses remain simple and do not receive decorative dormers without an interior room to justify them.
  - **Ocean Kingdom** — gate stands at the lake. ## Aggros

Hostile creatures — "NME"s — that oppose the player and their blorbs, as opposed to the peaceful wildlife and townsfolk found elsewhere.

- **Skeletons**: the first Aggro, and the first minions manifesting from the Demon King's mirror world (see above). They rise straight up out of the ground out in the open wasteland — never in the starting clearing, town, city, village, or lake. Built on the same base figure as a person or NPC, but bone-white all over, with noticeably skinnier arms and legs to read as bare bone, and no abdomen — in its place, a column of small spine segments runs from hip to chest, tilted back a little the way a real spine would sit.
- Skeletons are aggressive: they close in on the player and any of the party's free-roaming blorbs and attack with punches. Blorbs are what actually fight them off — jumping in for a melee bounce-attack, or, for the water and fire blorbs, streaming their element at the skeleton straight from their core. A skeleton that's fought off sinks back into the ground it rose from and awards 24 XP divided among the party blorbs that damaged it.
- A blorb that takes enough damage to fully deplete its HP loses its mass entirely, leaving only its core behind until the core regenerates it a full blorb again.
- **Ape Skeletons**: the Plant Kingdom's own counterpart to the ordinary skeleton above, manifesting the same way but on a primate's own frame instead of a person's — bone-white all over, no ears, and (like the ordinary skeleton) no abdomen, replaced by the same tilted-back column of spine segments. They fight and sink the same way an ordinary skeleton does, but hit harder, have more HP, and award more XP when defeated. They rise anywhere in the kingdom's wilds except in or near the primate village.
- **Training dummies**: a second Aggro type, unlocked through one of the Plant Kingdom's side quests (see Kingdoms, above). A training dummy starts as inert training equipment, harmless to walk past. Left unobserved for three in-game nights, it comes to life and behaves like a skeleton, but is noticeably harder to defeat.
- **False Hero** (see The town, under Setting, above) is the game's first named boss fight, with far more HP and a far larger XP reward than any ordinary skeleton. Defeated and fleeing, he doesn't make it out of town: a skeleton catches him at the edge of the plaza and drags him under, having failed the Demon King he secretly served. Word spreads fast afterward — the village names the player its true savior.
