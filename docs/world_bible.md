# Eleblorbs — World Bible

A living reference for the game's story, characters, and world-building facts, as they've been established during development. This is lore/narrative content only — implementation details (how something is built in Godot) belong in code, not here.

## Premise

The player wakes from a coma in a grass field with amnesia. A small town sits nearby, including an antique shop the player is meant to visit. The overarching plot conflict is a **Demon King** who wants to exterminate all elemental energy in the world.

### The Demon King's mirror world

The Demon King's domain is a mirror world: a reverse reflection of ours, inverted and mirrored beneath it. Skeletons are the first minions known to manifest from it, rising directly up out of the ground into the wasteland above.

The Demon King feeds off living things elsewhere in the world too. In the Plant Kingdom, that feeding takes the form of a curse on the monkeys living there (see Kingdoms, below).

## Protagonist

- An amnesiac player character (no name established yet).
- Has a sentient companion named **Blorbus** — in fact, Blorbus *is* one of the player's own three starting blorbs (see Blorbs, below), not a separate character. Specifically: whichever of the three is still ungemmed once the player has merged elemental gems into the other two. The second elemental transformation finishes first; then the untouched third blorb unexpectedly begins transforming too, turns a dull, greyish pink — "brain pink," not a bright bubblegum tone — and starts talking, unprompted, taking the name Blorbus for itself. Blorbus's awakening unlocks the player's ability to befriend additional wild blorbs: before the sentient Psychic blorb is present to coordinate them, no other blorb can join the starting party. Once awake, Blorbus can never be merged with a gem itself. Why it specifically wakes up this way, and what (if anything) it remembers or knows, is still unexplained — Blorbus's own account of it amounts to "I don't know why either."
- Blorbus is a **Psychic-type** blorb. His core opens into a pocket-universe storage space that functions as the player's inventory: he psychically manages the items kept there and coordinates the player's other blorbs. The inventory interface is therefore a manifestation of Blorbus himself rather than an ordinary abstract menu.
- The player's awareness can switch psychically between their own body and Blorbus whenever he is not being worn as part of the blorb suit. While inhabiting Blorbus, the player's body follows him like a companion blorb. Humongous begins as a free resident of his Crossroads home, with no automatic connection to Blorbus's core. Blorbus must first speak with him, establishing their trust and unlocking the choice to mind-merge or deliberately shrink and carry him within the pocket dimension of Blorbus's infinite core. Humongous can cross into another kingdom only after he has actually entered that core; merely meeting or mind-merging with him at home does not silently pack him for travel. A carried Humongous can later be released or mind-merged from within the core. Ending a mind merge returns awareness to Blorbus while leaving Humongous physically present.
- The hero's health does not return on its own. Food restores it in amounts reflecting the food's rarity, while an inn restores the whole party. If the hero's own HP is exhausted—even while awareness is inhabiting Blorbus, Xiao Hou Zi, Manchego, or another controllable companion—the world goes black and the hero faints. They lose half their carried tokoins but keep every item, achievement, blorb, and party companion, then awaken as they did at the beginning: eyes closed, opening them before rising. They wake at the last inn used within that kingdom, or at that kingdom's entrance if they have not rested there; the outskirts instead returns them to the magical clearing. Xiao Hou Zi remains invulnerable (see the jungle biome, below, for why). If a directly controlled Blorbus melts, awareness returns immediately to the living hero rather than remaining with his melted core.

## Blorbs

Creatures the player finds and befriends, apart from the one settled giant
that remains in the wasteland.

- **Normal type** blorbs: a classic slime silhouette — a smooth, continuously-curved dome/blob body (no flat-cut geometry), sagging low and wide like a water balloon settled under its own weight rather than a symmetric dome. Light, neutral off-white in color, with individual blorbs varying in subtle shade. Rendered with a viscous, gooey, slightly translucent material. Two large, round eyes.
- **Size type blorb**: a single immense resident farther out in the northern wasteland beyond the main plateau, named **Humongous**. It is at least fifty times the size of a normal blorb, broadly flattened and deeply settled into the ground as though gliding across its surface. Its enormous mass makes every movement exceptionally slow, and on its own it glides without ever hopping. While Blorbus is psychically merged with it, however, deliberate control can make the entire giant jump with the same squash-and-stretch motion as a smaller blorb. Once more than half of a person's body has entered its viscous mass, they are slowly lifted to its surface, where they can walk across the giant's goo and make a superjump three times higher than a normal blorb jump; it never gives the ordinary blorb trampoline bounce. Blorbus can carry Humongous between kingdoms inside his core, but that relationship is unlocked by their first conversation and travel requires Humongous to have physically entered the core first. If he has never been carried, he remains free at home while the party visits a spoke kingdom and is still free when they return. A Humongous deliberately carried into and then released within a spoke kingdom is recalled into the core when the party returns to the Crossroads, avoiding a persistent trail of giant bodies across worlds; an eligible active mind merge can continue through a gate crossing.
- Every blorb carries a **core**: an apple-sized, many-faceted object suspended inside its body, visible through the translucent flesh. Pale and opalescent in a normal blorb.
- Giving a blorb an elemental gem transforms it into an elemental blorb of that type: the gem is thrown into the blorb, and it merges into the core through some pocket-dimension property of the core itself — no matter how large the merged object actually is, the core simply takes it in. The world stills during the brief transformation: the blorb shines white until the light fills everything, then the glare clears to reveal its new form. The merge changes both the core's glow and the blorb's whole body: a water merge turns the body a deep, viscous blue; a fire merge turns it molten orange-red, like slow-moving lava.
- Fire and Electric blorbs specifically give off real light from their core, not just a color change — a small, steady glow bright enough to notice in the dark. The other elements don't share this trait.
- The player starts the game with three companion blorbs, which stay nearby and follow once the player wanders far enough away. Whichever of the three is still ungemmed once the other two have taken an elemental gem turns pink and wakes up as **Blorbus** — see Protagonist, above.
- **Wild blorbs**, found out in the world, cannot join the party before Blorbus awakens; his sentient Psychic presence is what makes befriending and coordinating additional blorbs possible. After that awakening, a wild blorb that the player approaches notices them and starts tagging along after the party's own blorbs rather than the player directly. After it's spent enough time actually traveling with the party (not necessarily all at once — falling behind and catching back up doesn't undo the progress), it asks whether it can join. Blorbs love being bounced on, so landing on one quickly strengthens this bond. Once the player owns the corroded pocket compass (see the antique shop, below), it points them toward the nearest not-yet-partied wild blorb so they can go find it — the compass doing the elemental-tracking job its own lore already describes, rather than a glow on the blorb itself. Finding every wild blorb currently out in the field doesn't end the hunt: a fresh few appear elsewhere shortly after, so there's always another one for the compass to point toward.
- Most wild blorbs found out in the world are plain, unmerged Normal blorbs. A smaller number are found already naturally elemental — encountered already merged, rather than merged by hand with a thrown gem (see below). The electric blorb (found anywhere in the open field), the canyon biome's rock blorbs, the jungle biome's plant blorbs, and the volcano's fire blorbs (see Setting, below, for all three) were all met this way.
- **Shiny blorbs**: a rare wild variant, cosmetic only and not tied to any element — a shiny blorb carries no elemental power and can never accept a gem. It's a plain blorb with a distinctly bright, glossy, pearlescent finish rather than the ordinary off-white body most wild blorbs have. Only a handful exist in the field at once, against many plain wild blorbs, so encountering one is meant to read as a genuine rare find.
- **Levels and experience**: every blorb begins at Level 1 with its own natural variation in Strength, Defense, HP, and MP. Dealing even one damaging attack to an NME marks that blorb as a participant in the encounter, and a blorb worn on the player's own body counts as participating too, for as long as its own power is actively drawn on (streaming, or hovering) during the fight — not merely for being worn. When the NME is defeated, its XP reward is divided among every participant. Melting resets all of that blorb's current combat contributions, so it receives no XP from an earlier hit even if it reforms before the NME falls; landing a fresh hit after reforming can establish a new contribution. Harder NME types carry larger XP rewards. Each new level raises Strength and Defense and increases maximum HP and MP — HP and MP grow with the blorb's level itself now, while Strength governs how hard that blorb's own attacks land and Defense how much incoming damage it shrugs off (see Elemental Gems, below, for how an attack's own element factors in too). The Blorbs menu shows the current level and an XP bar filled toward the next one, and a passive message announces each level-up without interrupting play.

### The Blorb Suit

- A called trampoline companion can answer beneath either the hero or a directly controlled Blorbus. Fire blorbs can make that crossing over lava as well as ordinary ground.
- Free Water blorbs are true three-dimensional swimmers and can follow companions throughout the water column. Air blorbs cannot fly underwater and remain buoyant at the surface; an Air chest likewise grants flight only after its wearer leaves the water, never diving.
- A sealed Diving Helmet combined with two Rock blorbs worn as legs lets the hero walk directly along the ocean floor at a slightly reduced pace instead of swimming freely.
- A blorb's Speed stat never changes the hero's ordinary walking or running speed or animation cadence, nor a free blorb's ordinary movement. It contributes only while worn for special traversal: both equipped legs power skating, swimming draws from the worn head and leg blorbs leading the swimmer, chest flight draws from its Air blorb, and limb-powered flight draws from its leg blorbs. The contribution is deliberately exaggerated during map testing and will be reduced for progression balance later.
- Armor can be thrown into a blorb and bound into the matching equipment slot in its core. The blorb then reshapes itself into an equivalent living armor form when worn: the Dented Breastplate becomes a broad medieval blorb cuirass designed in the same visual language as the Knight's Helm. Bound armor adds to that blorb's own Defense while it is free, and the hero inherits the same complete effective Defense while wearing it. Protection varies with the object's rarity and power: practical equipment such as the Diving Helmet gives a modest boon, ordinary steel armor and the Knight's Helm give stronger protection, and unique relics such as the Nautilus Crown and especially the Lava Helm are stronger still. A blorb can bind only one head modification and one body-armor modification at a time. The **Bird Helm** is a further such head item -- permanently bound to Blorbaka's own core rather than ever thrown or found loose (see False Hero, under The town, below) -- whose worn shape is a small hemisphere capping just the top half of the head, with a hooked hawk's beak at the nose and small wing decorations where the ears sit. Wearing it grants ordinary head-armor protection like the others, but its real purpose is unique to it: it's the only way to see or reach the Sky Kingdom at all (see The Sky Kingdom, below).

The player can call their whole party onto their own body at once, and dismiss them again just as physically — each blorb visibly hops across the ground and up onto its spot rather than appearing there, and hops back off to land scattered around the player when dismissed. The most recently bounced-on available companion can also be called directly beneath the hero as a moving trampoline aid, even during a fall. Landed, a blorb flattens into one flexible covering over a body part: a full arm from shoulder to hand, a full leg from hip to boot, an oval covering the torso, or, on the head, an inflated spherical blorb helmet enclosing the whole head like a deep-sea diver's air supply. Every covering keeps its own blorb's elemental color and core, the core still sitting just behind its own eyes, except that a worn Rock blorb visibly hardens into the same rough stone substance as the world's natural rocks while retaining its living face and core. A worn head blorb allows underwater swimming; paired Water leg blorbs remain part of the swimmer's suit, and their separately controlled downward water streams lift the hero into a level hover while both fire together. A single Fire hand projects its flamethrower forward, while two active Fire hands lower beside the hips and fire downward to hover; adding two simultaneously firing Fire feet turns that hover into free directional flight. Fire feet otherwise provide downward jets without lift of their own, but each compounds the speed of chest-Air-blorb flight while firing. Paired Air feet passively hover and retain the normal walk/run movement while traversing in the camera's full direction, including elevation. Air limb powers also form silent temporary cloud platforms: an arm makes one ahead at shoulder height, while a foot makes one immediately beneath the wearer. Paired Fire leg blorbs also protect the hero well enough to walk across lava; removing either Fire leg while over lava immediately returns the hero to the pool's edge. Free Fire blorbs can travel directly over molten lava, while every other free blorb—including Blorbus under direct control—refuses bare lava but can safely occupy rock or another solid surface rising from within it. Continuous elemental jets draw on the individual blorb's power reserve and stop when it is depleted. A worn Electric hand crackles with a continuous, erratic shock rather than a directional jet — a hand-to-hand arc more than a beam — and anything it actually hits freezes in place for a moment, unable to move. A worn City hand crackles with that same kind of arc, tinted a cooler blue rather than Electric's own yellow, and instead of a freeze it leaves whatever it hits hastened but weakened — faster on its feet, but its own attacks land softer, for a few seconds after. A worn Rock hand calls up solid crags that can carry the player and companions, strike NMEs as they emerge, and grow in number, size, damage, and persistence with the blorb's level; a Rock leg raises one directly beneath the hero as a platforming skill. Rock and Ice slabs recognize existing temporary slabs as valid foundations, allowing repeated powers to form towers. A worn Plant hand fires individual seed pellets one after another at a steady, unhurried pace, rather than any continuous stream. Free-roaming Electric, City, Rock, and Plant blorbs fight with these same four powers on their own when defending themselves, exactly as free Water and Fire blorbs already stream their own elements. Paired Ground leg blorbs grow a single broad wheel between the feet, its axle spanning heel to heel, lifting the hero to ride it rather than walk — the wheel scales even the steepest climbable ground without hesitation, and rolling off a rise or a canyon lip launches the rider into a true fall, momentum intact, until gravity brings them back down. Adding a paired set of Ground arm blorbs on top does nothing by itself; only while the hero also holds down both arm powers together, with the leg wheel already active, do the raised arms grow a second wheel between the hands, and the whole body leans forward into a hunched riding crouch until that front wheel meets the ground alongside the rear one. Releasing either arm power drops the front wheel and stands the hero back upright at once.

- Any equipment or traversal form created by reshaping a blorb's own body—such as Ground wheels or a Snow snowboard—keeps that blorb element's exact body hue. This rule does not apply when a power summons separate environmental matter: Air platforms are actual clouds and therefore retain the world's natural cloud color rather than the Air blorb's body color.

### Checkpoint portals

- A **checkpoint portal** is an upright ring of piping in a super-egg outline, tall enough for the hero to walk through, its opening filled with a swirling, space-like membrane tinted the body colour of one element's blorbs (off-white for Normal blorbs). Its rim is not solid; it is a threshold rather than an obstacle.
- Portals are one-way and stand in back-to-back pairs at the border between two biomes, facing apart. Each biome's portal stands on the side you enter that biome from, its face turned toward you, and its blorbs wait there, idling and wandering close by as if expecting the hero rather than following him.
- Walking, riding, jumping, flying or swimming through a portal's face with most of the body swaps the hero into that portal's suit: the blorbs he is wearing hop off, and the waiting blorbs join the party and hop on. Nobody is left behind: the blorbs that hop off stay in the party and keep following, so the party grows with every portal, and passing back through an earlier portal simply puts its blorbs back on. Passing through a portal from behind never does anything, so crossing a border changes suits exactly once, in either direction.
- Where a suit has a helm, its head blorb carries it: the Diving Helmet for Water, the Lava Helm for Fire, the Toboggan for both Ice and Snow, and the Bird Helm for Air.

## Demo World

- A standalone world outside the story's geography: one long valley that runs through a biome for every blorb suit with a clear traversal power, divided by checkpoint portal borders, under the Crossroads' own sky, light and fog. From west to east: an arrival clearing exactly like the Crossroads' own, where the hero wakes with his five Normal blorbs, Xiao Hou Zi, Manchego and Pandy in the party (the companions stay with him throughout); forest plains of the Crossroads' own trees, undergrowth and flower meadows, where wild shiny blorbs roam and can be recruited (still the Normal suit's ground); a patch of the Primate Kingdom's own jungle (Plant); a long natural lake with a narrow beach in bare wasteland, as at the Crossroads lake (Water); a long frozen lake of solid ice over water, set in a snowfield (Ice); the Ice Kingdom's own snowboard mountain and the ranges around it, climbed from the west and ridden down its run to the east (Snow); a stretch of the Rock/Ground Kingdom's terraces, washes and halfpipe canyon that climbs steadily to cloud height (Ground); a cliff edge high among the clouds above a deep chasm, flown across to the far side (Air); and a volcanic lowland with a full-size Fire Kingdom volcano, its mouth brimming with lava (Fire). The valley floor rolls throughout, and its lakes lie deep in basins punched well below it. It exists for demonstrating and testing each suit, switching between characters and riding, and will grow as suits and biomes are added.

## Elemental Gems

- Used to turn a normal blorb into an elemental blorb (thrown into it; see the merge mechanic above). Some blorbs are also found already naturally elemental out in the world, without ever needing a gem thrown at them — see Blorbs, above.
- Elemental types confirmed so far: **Water** (blue), **Fire** (orange/red), **Electric** (yellow, crackling), **City** (a saturated, glassy azure — a cooler cousin of Electric's own crackle, deliberately more saturated than Air's own pale, wispy sky-blue so the two don't read as the same thing), **Rock** (warm brown-grey, shared exactly by natural rocks, hardened Rock-blorb armour, and Rock constructs), **Ground** (a distinct, darker loamy brown — its own element, not a synonym for Rock), **Air** (a naturally hovering blorb with wings), **Plant** (leafy green), **Ice** (pale glassy blue-white), **Snow** (near-white, matte), and **Wood** (warm cedar brown).
- Physically, gems are small (apple-sized), faceted, crystalline objects.
- The Fire Gem and the Electric Gem are both bought from the antique dealer in town, using tokoins.
- The Water Gem isn't sold anywhere; it rests in the fountain at the center of the plaza, free for anyone who finds it.
- The Rock Gem and the Ground Gem are both found out in the canyon biome (see Setting, below) rather than sold — the Rock Gem lying on the canyon floor, the Ground Gem waiting atop the biome's tallest hoodoo.
- The Air Gem is not sold; it waits on a landing cloud at the top of the sky parkour course above town (see The town, under Setting, below).
- The Plant Gem is not sold; it waits atop one of the jungle biome's own trees (see Setting, below).
- The City Gem is not sold either; it's found somewhere among the outskirts city's own streets (see Setting, below).
- The Ice Gem and the Snow Gem are both found out in the Ice Kingdom (see Kingdoms, below) rather than sold.
- The Wood Gem is not sold; it's found only in the Wood Kingdom, the abandoned reveal tucked inside the Plant Kingdom (see Kingdoms, below).
- **Type effectiveness**: Fire, Plant, and Water form a closed three-way advantage loop — Fire burns Plant, Plant drinks Water, Water douses Fire — and each pairing is handicapped in reverse (Plant is weak against Fire, Water against Plant, Fire against Water). Wood extends this: Wood beats any water-coded target (Fish Goblins included — see the Ocean Kingdom, below), and Plant in turn beats Wood, the same handicapped-in-reverse rule as the fire/plant/water loop. An attack of the stronger type in a matchup deals extra damage; the weaker side's attack deals reduced damage. Every other element (Electric, City, Rock, Ground, Air, Ice, Snow, and Psychic) is neutral against everything, including each other, for now.

## Tokoins (currency)

- The game's currency.
- Physical tokoins appear in the world as large coins (dinner-plate sized) standing up on their edge, found lying around for the player to collect.
- Collecting one physical tokoin adds **10** to the player's tokoin currency total.
- Spent on goods from town vendors, including elemental gems.

## Setting

- Across the wilds and elemental kingdoms, naturally transformed blorbs live alongside ordinary untransformed blorbs rather than replacing them. Roughly half of each biome's otherwise elemental wild population remains ordinary, making elemental blorbs distinctive discoveries while keeping plain blorbs part of every ecosystem.

- **World orientation**: the sun rises in the **east** and sets in the **west**. Maps use that convention: the town lies toward the east of the world, while the canyon/mesa biome lies toward the west.
- **The wasteland**: the low-lying area surrounding the main plateau.
  - The wasteland north of the plateau is home to the sole known Size type blorb: **Humongous**, an enormous, slow-moving non-hopping giant.
  - East of the plateau, the wasteland opens into a vast lake. Its waterline sits below the surrounding desert, exposing a narrow natural beach along the upper basin slope. The plateau's eastern cliff falls into the submerged basin; beneath the water, the old wasteland slopes down through the bowl before rising again at the distant mountain foot.
  - Lake shells and freshwater clams collect around the shore and shallows, while harvestable lakeweed grows across the deeper basin floor. The fishing village's shops buy all three.
  - The wasteland floor is gently undulating desert scattered with loose rocks rather than a perfectly flat plain. The main plateau's sides are interrupted by occasional lower grassy shelves, with flat ledges capable of supporting bushes and other vegetation.
- **Starting field**: the grassy field where the player wakes up from their coma. The immediate magical clearing is a calm, level safe area, kept free of hostile encounters and dense wilderness clutter in the same spirit as the protected ground around towns and cities. Clusters of tiny single-shape stemless petals lie directly on the ground in an organically broken fairy ring halfway between the clearing's centre and perimeter. A small, shallow mound of mild brown prepared earth accepts the **Seed of Life** when it is thrown there: it takes root immediately, appears as a sprout on the next morning, and becomes the **Tree of Life** on the following morning. Once mature, it produces one harvest of rare **Blorb Slime** each day. Blorb Slime heals blorbs, is valuable enough to be stocked by only some merchants, and is also the substance that resurrects Dinosaur (see Rock and Ground Kingdom, below).
- **The town**: a small fantasy village near the starting field, with a central plaza/fountain, market stalls, houses, and NPC villagers going about their day.
  - Scattered around the open ground between buildings are a handful of small "floating" crate staircases -- optional parkour, not required to reach anything. Set apart from these, a single spiraling staircase of cloud platforms climbs all the way up into the sky, continuing as a hand-placed chain of large walkable clouds and ending on one wide landing cloud that holds the **Air Gem** -- see The Sky Kingdom, below, for why this entire climb is invisible and untouchable until the Bird Helm is worn.
  - **False Hero**: introduces himself as "the Hero of the Village, Savior of Earth" the moment he's met, and accuses the player of reeking of the Demon King's own mirror world -- an act; he's secretly working against the player, not for the village. Tall and heavily built, every segment of his own body plain skin-toned except an off-white brief at the hips -- what's left once his "heroic" look, entirely a worn blorb suit (two Water blorbs on his legs, two Fire blorbs on his arms, and a Psychic blorb named **Blorbaka** on his head), is stripped away. Blorbaka carries the **Bird Helm** bound into her own core (see The Sky Kingdom, below) from the start, which is why the false hero's own head reads as helmed rather than a plain hat. He only appears once the player could field a full blorb suit of their own (see the Blorb Suit, above) -- before that, and after he's dealt with, the town's villagers have nothing to do with him at all. Confronting him starts a real fight: he cycles between his own flamethrower and a hovering retreat on alternating Water legs or Fire arms, the same suit-power mechanics the player's own worn blorbs grant, but he never leaves the town itself to give chase. Defeated, his entire suit falls away and his five worn blorbs -- Blorbaka among them -- land nearby as real, wild, joinable blorbs the player can then befriend like any other; acquiring Blorbaka this way is also how the player acquires the Bird Helm itself, since it's never sold or found loose. He himself flees on foot and doesn't get far -- see Aggros, below, for what catches him.
- **The outskirts city**: a separate, larger settlement elsewhere in the wilderness from the town's own small village — a full-scale avenue/cross-street grid lined with houses, distinct from the town's plaza-and-market layout. It once had a matching City Kingdom gate nearby; that kingdom has since been removed, but the city biome itself, unaffected, has no gate of its own.
  - A handful of wild **City blorbs** live scattered through its streets, found already naturally elemental the same way the canyon's Rock blorbs and the jungle's Plant blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
  - A **City Gem** is hidden somewhere among those same streets.
- **The canyon biome** (also called the **Mesa Biome**, same landmark): a large banded-sandstone rock formation out in the wilderness, distinct from the grassy starting field and the smaller rocky outcrops scattered nearer town. Striped orange/rust/tan/white rock, a slab-paved canyon floor, towering stacked-slab hoodoos, and a natural rock arch. Its paving and shelves are true physical ground for the hero, NMEs, and blorbs alike rather than scenery resting above a separate movement surface. A dramatic climbable landmark, not tied to any plot event yet. The warp to the **Rock and Ground Kingdom** (see Kingdoms, below) stands within the biome, off to one side of the Mesa Tower.
  - Its tallest hoodoo — the **Mesa Tower** — sits at the biome's own center, notably taller and more tiered than the others around it. Reaching the **Ground Gem** waiting on top means climbing it tier by tier, real jump-to-jump parkour rather than a guaranteed path up.
  - A **Rock Gem** rests on the canyon floor elsewhere in the same biome — a shorter find for a player exploring on foot, not gated behind the tower climb.
  - A handful of wild **Rock blorbs** live scattered across the canyon floor, found already naturally elemental the same way the jungle's Plant blorbs and the volcano's Fire blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
- **The jungle biome**: a separate, smaller plateau of its own, southwest of the main plateau — a lush, thick, varied landscape distinct from every other biome. Its sloping sides are broken up by small, flat, grass-capped shelves at varying elevations, with bushes and grass growing on the ledges. Its trees run larger and taller than the ones found elsewhere: palm trees (trunks ranging in height with a natural lean/curve), shorter banana trees bearing banana fruit, durian trees bearing large, naturally spiky durian fruit, sprawling banyan trees, stout baobab trees, flowering trees, and hanging vines strung between the canopy.
  - Its resident is a small monkey named **Xiao Hou Zi**, of the **stuffed animal monkey** class (see Plant Kingdom's own jungle villagers, below, for the other member of that class, and the taxonomy note there for how it differs from the world's other primates). He roams the plateau on his own until a player wanders close, at which point he leaves off wandering to come find them specifically. Stick around him long enough and he'll join the party — following the player the way a bonded blorb does, though he is his own companion rather than a blorb himself. Once recruited, the player can also take direct control of him the same way they can with Blorbus. He'll talk if approached, with a loose, chatty handful of lines about the jungle, its fruit, and the vague things "the older vines whisper."
  - Xiao Hou Zi is a beloved stuffed animal who has come to life, and that is why he is invincible: he takes no damage and never runs out of breath, so he can stay underwater indefinitely.
  - A handful of wild **Plant blorbs** live within the plateau, found already naturally elemental the same way the electric and rock blorbs are (see Blorbs, above) rather than merged by hand.
  - A **Plant Gem** waits on top of one of the plateau's own trees, for a player willing to climb up after it.
- **The volcano**: a landform out in the open wasteland, well clear of the main plateau, the lake, and the jungle plateau alike. Larger in footprint than the jungle plateau, deliberately so -- a walkable volcano cone needs more room to climb gently than a flat-topped plateau does. Its outer flank climbs gradually from the wasteland floor up to a broad, organically round crater rim, walkable the whole way; a shorter, steeper (but still climbable) inner wall then drops from the rim down into the crater itself, opening onto a level, walkable ring of solid ground that circles a large, round pool of lava at the very center, glowing and textured like a fire blorb's own molten body. A handful of wild **Fire blorbs** live on the outer flank and in the crater itself, found already naturally elemental the same way the electric, rock, and plant blorbs are (see Blorbs, above). The warp to the **Fire Kingdom** stands on a broad basalt outcropping emerging from within the crater's lava pool rather than on the shore (see Kingdoms, below).
- **The Ice Kingdom's gate**: stands on its own naturally raised snow plateau in the northwestern wasteland. The plateau is formed by the same continuous terrain as the surrounding world, with a white snow-coloured surface and a broad walkable slope rather than white shapes laid over the ground. Snow and Ice blorbs live around the gate among frost-tinted pines and rocks.
- **The Chinese village**: a large settlement out in the northeast wasteland, built across many compact floating islands above the **Abyss of Impending Doom**. Each island has a broad, genuinely level grass crown for village life, rocky cliff sides, and an irregular tapered underside like a torn-away floating plateau. Long plank bridges cross meaningful gaps between the islands, including a level approach from the surrounding terrain. Each smaller residential island holds only one or two enclosed houses with a single doorway; the large central imperial island holds a spacious four-level golden castle whose successively smaller storeys form a stepped pyramid. Broad perimeter terraces and alternating side stairs connect real landings without crossing the palace walls. Inside, lacquer-red columns, benches, a central ceremonial carpet, raised golden throne and dais, and rows of hanging *deng long* give every level the visual hierarchy of an imperial hall. A formal flowering garden and stone paths frame its entrance. The palace's fully enclosed rear annex is the Royal Kitchen, connected directly to the throne hall by an open doorway; this is where the Emperor initially sends the blorbs after mistaking them for meat buns, watched over by the Royal Chef. Trees grow naturally around the village and bamboo gathers in loose, irregular groves. Its ordinary-human-scale buildings have tiered red-and-gold pagoda roofs and lanterns, and its villagers speak Chinese. Unlike the town, it has no portal gate and belongs to no kingdom; it is simply a place in the world.
  - **The children.** A handful of kids play outside, apart from the adult villagers, and have built their own small stick play hut. One of them wears her hair in pigtails.
  - **The Jingu Bang.** Sun Wu Kong's legendary staff lies hidden among the ordinary loose sticks scattered at the play hut's base, unnoticed by the children who built it. Xiao Hou Zi can free Sun Wu Kong from beneath the sealing rock atop the Emperor's castle; returning the staff completes that meeting. Sun Wu Kong remains in the village rather than joining the traveling party, but tells Xiao Hou Zi that the little monkey can summon him in a fight. From then on, while the player is controlling Xiao Hou Zi, nearby combat calls Sun Wu Kong into the world in a puff of smoke; he fights until the encounter ends and then disappears in smoke again.
  - **The Emperor, the farmer, the Royal Chef, and Pandy.** The Emperor is found inside his four-level golden castle. He is slightly taller than the hero and wears a formal continuous red robe with gold trim, dark boots extending up beneath the robe, a high court bun, a long beard emerging from his chin, and a red square ceremonial crown with ten hanging gold cords at both its front and back. The Royal Chef is secretly an agent of the Demon Lord who has been influencing the Emperor into unjust rule, including the farmer's grievance over escalating demands on his land. He initially appears to be an ordinary human royal cook in a clean pale uniform and white toque, offering guarded but plausible remarks around his working hearths, bronze pots, bamboo steamers, shelves and preparation counters without exposing himself. Only when confronted does his disguise fall away, revealing red demonic skin and a blackened uniform and hat. Hearing the farmer's story changes the hero's next audience with the Emperor. The Emperor disregards the appeal because he is obsessed with finding the perfect *rou bao*, mistakes the party's blorbs for the buns, and sends them to the Royal Kitchen. There even a large party's blorbs sit magically shrunken to true hand-sized bun scale in neat rows on the long counter. The Chef reveals his allegiance when confronted and attacks with a heavy **Royal Meat Cleaver**; the hero must defeat him without any blorbs, relying on the hero's own movement, throwable items, and any non-blorb companions already traveling with the party. The defeated Chef drops the cleaver for the player to keep as a weapon, then retreats to the underworld in dark smoke, breaking his spell over the Emperor. The restored Emperor thanks the hero by transferring control of the village to them. The hero then appoints the farmer to govern in their stead, and the farmer entrusts his giant panda companion **Pandy** to the party in gratitude. Pandy has outward-splayed black patches around dark-brown, naturally blinking eyes, a small black puff tail, quiet idle and resting motions, and watches the hero when nearby. Every quest stage and Pandy's place in the party persist across world travel.
  - **The outdoor merchant**, Chen, sells a small selection of wares including the rare **Seed of Life**. It can only take root in the prepared soil of the hero's initial magical clearing (see Setting, above).
- **The Sky Kingdom**: a place in the world rather than a gated kingdom (see Kingdoms, below, for how those differ) -- a small cluster of cloud islands sitting among the world's own ordinary sky clouds (the same clouds visible from the ground every day, not a separate higher layer), each with its own quartz-and-gold pavilion. Ordinarily invisible and untouchable, not merely hidden behind distance or a locked door: it is "totally turned off" for anyone not wearing the **Bird Helm**, a core-bound item permanently attached to Blorbaka's own core (see False Hero, above, for how she's acquired) rather than ever sold or found loose. Only while a blorb wearing the Bird Helm is worn on the hero's own head does the Sky Kingdom become visible, solid, and reachable at all -- there is no other route in. Its own gateway is the town's existing spiraling cloud staircase (see The town, above, for the crate-and-cloud climb that otherwise leads to the Air Gem): that whole climb, the Air Gem included, is invisible and untouchable the same "totally turned off" way without the Bird Helm, so putting it on for the first time is what actually reveals the staircase up out of the ordinary sky clouds. The Sky Kingdom's own islands begin right where that climb tops out and extend onward from there, away from the village. It is populated by the **Tempestars**, a peaceful people with an ordinary humanoid upper body but no legs, continuing instead into a swirling tornado lower half the way merfolk continue into a fish tail; they drift and idle around their own cloud in the same unhurried, independently-timed way merfolk do. Rather than one central monarch, many small Tempestar rulers each preside over just their own single cloud island -- comparable in spirit to the Chinese Emperor, but scaled down to "the little ruler of a tiny kingdom of one cloud," and it shows: each ruler treats their own cloud as a point of pride against the others rather than as part of one shared realm, and more than one privately admits the arrangement reads more like a string of small exiles than an actual kingdom. The largest island, **Highcloud**, is ruled by **Cumulus Highvane** and keeps two attendants, **Squall Windrider** and **Breeze Suncrest**; three smaller satellite clouds -- the Anvil Cloud, the Drift Cloud, and the Hollow Cloud -- are ruled respectively by **Nimbus Greywisp**, **Gale Stormwick**, and **Cirro Ashveil**.
  - **The missing air blorbs.** Wild Air blorbs (see Blorbs, above) are conspicuously absent from the Sky Kingdom itself, where they would naturally belong -- several Tempestars remark on it unprompted, in their own words, without any of them naming a cause. The actual reason is the Demon King's own doing (see The Demon King's mirror world, above): he has vaporized the Sky Kingdom's own Air blorb population directly into the surrounding clouds, another instance of him feeding on a kingdom's elemental life the way he already does to the Plant Kingdom's monkeys. Restoring them is intended to be the Sky Kingdom's own central plot quest, not yet designed -- see Open threads, below.
  - First pass, prototyped specifically to get the Bird Helm's own design right through live testing -- see Open threads, below.
- **The antique shop**: the market stall the player is meant to visit. Run by an antique dealer who deals mostly in old, unexplained curiosities, plus a couple of elemental gems on the side (currently the Fire Gem and the Electric Gem; the Water, Rock, and Ground Gems are not for sale here, see Elemental Gems above). Confirmed stock:
  - **A corroded pocket compass.** Its needle never settles. Older residents say it was built to point toward elemental energy rather than north, back when someone still needed to track it.
  - **A sealed reliquary locket.** Doesn't open, no matter what's tried on it. Etched with symbols nobody in town can read, though a couple of the older carvings around the fountain use a similar hand. Just an antique curiosity, with no deeper mystery or connection to the plot.
  - **A cracked hourglass**, sand stopped mid-fall. No particular story attached to this one yet. Just a curiosity.
- The fountain in the plaza holds a Water Gem, resting in the water at the bottom of the basin rather than sold anywhere.
- **Fruit trees**: scattered through the wilds alongside the ordinary round-canopy and pine trees, sometimes standing alone and sometimes grouped into a small orchard of the same species planted in tidy rows. Confirmed varieties so far: apple, orange, lemon, and plum. Fruit collects on the ground beneath a tree, already fallen, free for anyone who finds it.
- **Domestic cats** have varied coat colors and can settle into a fully grounded, curled sleeping/resting pose.
  - **Yogi**, the world's one canonical named cat so far, lives in the first town. She is white-coated with light, orange-tinged brown markings: one ear, both front paws, an uneven pair of hind legs (one marked all the way up, the other just the paw and the segment right above it), and a few rings near the tip of her tail capped by a solid patch of the same color.
- **The armorer's stall**: a red-canopied market stall selling basic adventuring gear for anyone heading out of town. Run by an armorer (no name established yet). Confirmed stock:
  - **A notched shortsword.** Seen some use, still holds an edge.
  - **A dented breastplate.** Whoever wore it last, it stopped whatever hit it.
  - **A pitch torch.** Burns steady without ever needing relighting or fuel — it just stays lit.
- **The provisioner's stall**: a green-canopied market stall selling food for the road. Run by a provisioner (no name established yet). Confirmed stock:
  - **A rye loaf.** Dense enough to survive the bottom of a pack.
  - **A wedge of cheese.** Waxed rind, sharp inside.
  - **Dried berries.** Shriveled, but they'll keep for months.

## Kingdoms

Beyond the outskirts — the starting field, town, wasteland, and biomes described under Setting above — lie separate **kingdoms**, each reached through its own portal gate rather than by walking there directly.

- Blorbus's psychic power is what makes the trip possible: a gate only opens for a player who is directly piloting Blorbus (or Humongous while merged with him after Humongous has previously entered Blorbus's core), never for the player's own body or while riding along as a companion. Walking a piloted Blorbus through an open gate triggers the crossing.
- Arriving in a kingdom sets the player down near a twin gate that leads back to the outskirts, so the same psychic crossing works in reverse.
- The party (bonded blorbs and Xiao Hou Zi) and the player's inventory both travel with the player across a crossing.
- Five gates are confirmed so far. Plant, Ocean, Fire, and Ice each stand in an outskirts biome or plateau that thematically matches them; the Rock and Ground Kingdom's gate stands inside the canyon biome. The former **City Kingdom** gate (a repeat of the outskirts city's own avenue-grid layout, no named districts/NPCs/plot content) has since been removed — the outskirts City biome itself, its wild City blorbs, and its City Gem (see Setting, above) are untouched by that removal.
  - **Plant Kingdom** — gate stands in the jungle biome, Xiao Hou Zi's home. First-pass content: undulating jungle ground spanning out to the mountains ringing it, under a thick jungle canopy as dense as the crossroads jungle biome's own, broken up by a handful of open clearings, rocky outcrops, and a winding river carved into the ground (mirroring how the crossroads lake sits pushed down below the surrounding land). The kingdom's first village is a handful of giant emergent trees (a new towering tree species with climbable branch-ramps spiraling up their trunks, each landing sprouting its own small leaf-tipped twigs) with small treehouses mounted on every third landing, up and down each trunk. These treehouses are open platforming structures rather than enclosed ground houses: each uses a single central timber pillar, exposed sides, wood construction, and a broad green leaf roof so the climbing route remains open. Has its own day/night cycle. Populated by named jungle villagers, apes, and "primate template" monkeys (see below) standing on treehouse decks or wandering the village clearing, by wild blorbs of several types (including a scatter of naturally elemental Plant blorbs) roaming the jungle floor, and by a single giant gorilla living out in the wider kingdom (see below), well clear of the village itself. Named for its primate residents' own home terrain rather than the primates themselves — see Primate taxonomy, below, for why the residents keep their original species names regardless.
    - **Jungle villagers**: a second primate species distinct from Xiao Hou Zi in build, but the same **stuffed animal monkey** class as him — larger and taller, with no yellow cheek markings and no heart-shaped facial marking, hips set a bit closer together, proportionally longer limbs that taper less toward the hands/feet, no foot pads, and a torso that reads less sharply pear-shaped than Xiao Hou Zi's own. Individuals vary in height, limb length, tail length, and fur color, which ranges from dark charcoal through grey and light grey to shades of brown. Ambient wildlife: each individual now has their own name and their own two lines of dialogue on "Talk," the same way the outskirts town's villagers do, but still no join/recruit mechanic.
    - **Apes and "primate template" monkeys**: the village also has a smaller population of the world's other two primate classes (see Primate taxonomy, just below) roaming the same ground clearing as the jungle villagers, each with their own name and two lines of dialogue the same way. Ordinary village size range for both (not the giant gorilla's own landmark scale, below).
    - **Primate taxonomy.** The world's primates split into three classes: **monkeys** (Xiao Hou Zi, the jungle villagers, and the village's own "primate template" monkeys, all the same tailed body plan at different scales/species) always have tails and stay comparatively small; **apes** never have tails and run noticeably larger, with essentially no size overlap between the two size classes — an ape's smallest is still bigger than a monkey's biggest. Apes and "primate template" monkeys share one underlying rig (a build-time flag toggles the tail and the size/proportion range), while the jungle villagers/Xiao Hou Zi are their own separate "stuffed animal monkey" rig.
    - **Kova Kong**, a single male giant gorilla living out in the wider kingdom, away from the village — comparable in scale to Humongous (see Blorbs, above), and, per the same design idea, moves in a proportionally slowed, lumbering way rather than covering ground quickly despite his long strides. His true name is **Da Hou Zi**, and he will be revealed as Xiao Hou Zi's big brother. He begins with unnaturally purple eyes because the Demon Lord is controlling his mind; the story beat that frees him is not yet established. His articulated body is physical terrain the hero can climb and stand upon, turning the route up him into creature-scale parkour. Carries a **Special Banana** conspicuously at the top of his head — see Rewards and local quests, just below, for what it's for.
    - **The curse.** The kingdom is under a curse: whenever a new baby monkey is born, the kingdom's monkeys are reduced to ashes. Their shadows survive the ashing, since shadows can't burn. This is how the Demon King feeds off the Plant Kingdom (see The Demon King's mirror world, above). Lifting the curse is the kingdom's central plot quest, tied to the player's overall fight against the Demon King.
    - **Rewards and local quests.** Once the curse is lifted, the monkeys thank the player by confirming Xiao Hou Zi's place in the party for the rest of the game, on top of the informal recruit that already happens back in the outskirts jungle biome (see Blorbs, above, and Xiao Hou Zi's roam/approach/recruit behavior). A separate local quest begins with **Ossian Redbrow**, a reddish-furred ape encountered riding the horse **Manchego** (idling in the village, not following the player, and not yet rideable by the player himself) around the primate village. Per direct instruction, the quest itself is a deliberate placeholder pending real design: bring Ossian the Special Banana from the top of Kova Kong's head (see just above), and he dismounts, hands Manchego over as a real party mount, and rejoins the village as an ordinary roaming NPC. The quest's real content/story (why the banana, why Manchego, what it means) is still unestablished — see Open threads.
    - **Training dummy.** One of the kingdom's side quests unlocks a new Aggro type, the training dummy (see Aggros, below).
    - **The Wood Kingdom.** An abandoned reveal tucked inside the Plant Kingdom itself, rather than a separate gate — a cluster of weathered, long-emptied treehouses, greyed and dulled rather than the rest of the village's lived-in color, populated by wild **Wood blorbs** rather than any primate residents. The abandoned houses retain the same traversable open architecture as the living primate village: exposed sides around one weathered central wood pillar, with muted green leaf roofs rather than ordinary enclosed house walls. It doesn't exist on a first visit to the Plant Kingdom; it only appears once the player has already visited the Ice Kingdom (see below), then returned. Why visiting the Ice Kingdom specifically is what uncovers it, and who abandoned it, is still unestablished — see Open threads.
  - **Rock and Ground Kingdom** — gate stands within the canyon biome (see Setting, above), off to one side of the Mesa Tower. One kingdom spanning two existing elements at once, the same way the Ice Kingdom (below) spans two of its own. Its eastern Ground half remains a broad dirt landscape containing Dinosaur's sunken fossil basin and hardy scrub. Its western Rock half grows into layered, climbable banded-sandstone terrain—terraces, steep cliff bands, winding dry canyons, hoodoos, arches, slab fields, boulders and a broken mountain rim—using the same natural language as the Crossroads canyon biome. Wild **Rock blorbs**, **Ground blorbs**, and interspersed Normal blorbs inhabit the realm. A protected town occupies a broad level shelf, with enclosed earth-and-stone homes, individually named residents, a communal stone well, and a working inn. In the fossil basin lie the immense scattered, collapsed skeletal remnants of **Dinosaur**, a titanic dinosaur unique to this world (not a real-world species) at the same titan scale as Humongous and Da Hou Zi, standing and walking on all four legs when alive. Throwing Blorb Slime onto the remains rapidly draws the scattered bones back into their complete quadruped anatomy before the slime spreads across them and restores Dinosaur as a living, walking, physical titan; that resurrection persists across travel. The cliffs and canyon walls exist for exactly the climbing traversal a paired Ground-leg suit (see The Blorb Suit, above) is built for — Rock blorb-suit powers grant no comparable climbing ability of their own.
  - **Ice Kingdom** — one kingdom split deliberately between Ice and Snow. Its Ice half holds a vast irregular frozen lake with water beneath a solid, visibly thick surface, an exact round fishing hole farther out on the ice beside Ivar's hut, and large glacier peaks rising as continuous terrain rather than placed rock props. Its Snow half contains the protected village and a giant mountain whose peak approaches the world's rim. A working two-lane chair lift runs from near the village to the summit on tall pylons; a wide, smooth, organically winding snowboard route descends separately through alternating snow and exposed cold-rock bands, scattered pines, rocks and winter growth. Soft snow yields slightly beneath a traveler's feet without changing ordinary movement, while lake ice carries momentum. Pale ice-aspected skeleton NMEs rise in the wilderness but neither appear inside nor pursue anyone into the village. Named human villagers dress in long sleeves, long trousers or skirts, and gloves. **Solveig Woolcap** keeps the village's winter-goods stall, selling provisions and the **Toboggan**, a thick knitted head item with a folded band, a crown tapering to a pinched top, and a soft pom-pom. A blorb that binds it takes on that same winter-hat silhouette when worn, with its living eyes situated on the crown just above the folded band. Wild **Ice blorbs**, **Snow blorbs**, and interspersed Normal blorbs favor their corresponding halves. Ice blorbs share Rock blorbs' power pattern, raising temporary solid ice formations instead of stone; Rock blorbs cannot draw stone through the frozen lake surface. Visiting here for the first time also uncovers the Wood Kingdom back in the Plant Kingdom (see above) — why is still unestablished.
  - **Ocean Kingdom** — gate stands at the lake. The kingdom is one continuous ocean terrain: a shallow central shelf rises naturally through beach shorelines into an organically scattered tropical archipelago, sinks below the water between the islands, then descends into deep ocean toward the perimeter. One broad main island provides substantial ground to explore and supports denser, Plant-Kingdom-like vegetation; loose chains of smaller satellite islands carry lighter palm and tropical undergrowth. Beneath them, the ocean floor is a varied natural landscape of tall loose kelp, fan-shaped seaweed, rocks, luminous deposits, and colorful coral reefs growing along selected shallow shelves rather than an empty plain. The water remains visibly present as a bright ceiling when viewed from below; underwater distance falls into blue-green haze beneath a darker blue sky. A wooden arrival dock on stilts holds the return gate and the player's landing spot, with climbable ramps on both sides.
    - A giant reddish-orange, teal-splotched Kraken swims through and emerges from the sea; its solid mantle and twelve enormous articulated tentacles are traversal surfaces. Its mantle leans crown-first into its direction of travel while the closely joined tentacle chains hang beneath it. A flattened square SuperEgg rotated onto one corner forms its squid-like diamond crown. It has one large blinking eye with a heavy skin-coloured upper lid, and a ring of sharp teeth in the mouth hidden among the tentacle roots; entering that mouth is immediately incapacitating and the Kraken ejects the hero.
    - **Fish Goblins** serve the Kraken and are equivalent to ape skeletons in combat strength and XP. Their humanoid upper bodies are salmon-pink with deeper red areas, restrained head fins, and scale/sea growth clothing; below the torso, with no separate humanoid hip segment, their bodies continue directly into the same articulated dark-blue mer-tail body plan used by merfolk. Always water-coded for type-effectiveness purposes, which is what makes Wood's own advantage against them real (see Elemental Gems, above).
    - Two large three-masted pirate ships continuously sail the ocean with roaming pirate crews, wind-filled sails set forward of their yards, and exterior stairs rising from swimming level so their decks, masts, and structures form moving parkour landmarks. Warm oil lanterns along their main and raised decks illuminate at dusk and extinguish after sunrise. The black-bearded and orange-bearded captains work their respective wheels wearing distinct skull-marked hats: one broad, low bicorne with a red band and tails, the other a taller angular tricorn with contrasting trim. Each captain has replaced one hand with a metal hook. Some sailors wear cropped trousers with bare lower legs or have a wooden peg replacing one lower leg and foot, and some carry visible beard stubble.
    - Far into the deep sea stands the actual kingdom itself: a luminous teal and prism-green castle with an open, traversable audience hall, surrounded by a full village of enterable homes, illuminated paths, and kelp gardens. The whole settlement rests on a broad, level seabed shelf that blends gradually into the naturally undulating ocean floor beyond it. The underwater society lights its streets and thresholds with living pearl-and-shell lamps in aqua, blue, and violet, while softly luminous mineral stepping stones trace routes through the village; the castle is the brightest landmark. It is populated by peaceful **merfolk**, both male and female, who alternate between swimming local routes and independently timed floating rests. Every resident is an individually named villager with their own dialogue, following the same rule intended for every populated village rather than using generic filler NPCs. Merfolk have marine-teal humanoid upper bodies with restrained head fins; hair in varied sea-teal, ocean-blue, coral-red, and deep red shades, including tied ponytails, buns, pigtail buns, afros, flattops, and buzzcuts, with some women wearing small shells or sea flowers nestled into their hair; shiny scale clothing in silver, dark blue, or brown that varies between sleeveless shirts, short-sleeved shirts, and cropped tops; and no separate legs or humanoid hip segment—their torsos continue without a gap into compact articulated fish tails in natural teal, blue, red, and mauve variations. Their ruler, **Tidekeeper Nerion**, wears a nautilus crown. Merfolk merchants sell luminous **Nautilus Shells** and **Watering Cans**, the latter associated with tending the Tree of Life after the Seed of Life has taken root.
    - Defeating the Tidekeeper is intended to yield the **Nautilus Crown**, a core-bound head item. A head-worn blorb carrying it reshapes its own color into a pirate-style hat with a nautilus spiral. The exact battle premise and resolution are not yet established.
    - Wild Water and Air blorbs inhabit the open water. The kingdom has its own day/night presentation while sharing the persistent world clock.
  - **Fire Kingdom** — gate stands on a basalt outcropping within the lava pool inside the outskirts volcano's own crater (see Setting, above). The kingdom is an undulating volcanic landscape with scattered, genuinely deep lava bowls filled nearly to their volcanic mouths, many smaller cones, and a mountainous perimeter shaped like an immense chain of volcanoes. Curving tributaries of lava follow the lowlands around volcanoes and the gate clearing, with intermittent natural flame eruptions rising from exposed molten surfaces. Dark ember-aspected skeleton NMEs rise throughout the solid volcanic wilds, remaining outside the protected settlement and exposed lava. Its largest volcano opens into a massive crater whose inner slope descends to a broad, level caldera floor. A protected village occupies that floor, lit by standing braziers and wall-mounted fire fixtures around a central lava fountain. Its named inhabitants are **lava people** built on the ordinary articulated human body plan, including varied hair and skirt silhouettes, but animated molten texture covers every surface except their high-contrast living eyes. Wild Fire blorbs live throughout the wider volcanic country. **Lava Slide** wanders the rocky country among the volcanoes. His head has a living cashew-like sweep ending in twin backward horns, crowned by flame; his broad blinking eyes, horned spaulders, fire-wrapped arms, hexagonal belt buckle, flared greaves and sabatons, and living fire cape make him unmistakable at a distance. When first spoken to, he entrusts the hero with the Lava Helm. The kingdom has its own day/night cycle. A volcanic relic called the **Lava Helm** is one continuous glassy-black shell, broad around the face before arching upward and backward over the head in an organic boomerang-like sweep. A Fire blorb that absorbs it can become that enclosing helm while worn, retaining its eyes and growing a burning flame mohawk. Wearing it as the head of a complete Fire-blorb suit seals the whole body in living lava armour and permits the wearer to dive and swim through deep lava with the same freedom of movement used underwater; below the molten surface the world remains visible through a fiery, muffled analogue of underwater vision and sound. This supersedes the ordinary two-Fire-leg ability to remain on lava's surface.
- Each kingdom's deeper plot content remains to be established — see Open threads.

## Aggros

Hostile creatures — "NME"s — that oppose the player and their blorbs, as opposed to the peaceful wildlife and townsfolk found elsewhere.

- **Skeletons**: the first Aggro, and the first minions manifesting from the Demon King's mirror world (see above). They rise straight up out of the ground out in the open wasteland — never in the starting clearing, town, city, village, or lake. Built on the same base figure as a person or NPC, but bone-white all over, with noticeably skinnier arms and legs to read as bare bone, and no abdomen — in its place, a column of small spine segments runs from hip to chest, tilted back a little the way a real spine would sit.
- Skeletons are aggressive: they close in on the player and any of the party's free-roaming blorbs and attack with punches. Blorbs are what actually fight them off — jumping in for a melee bounce-attack, or, for the water and fire blorbs, streaming their element at the skeleton straight from their core. A skeleton that's fought off sinks back into the ground it rose from and awards 24 XP divided among the party blorbs that damaged it.
- A blorb that takes enough damage to fully deplete its HP loses its mass entirely, leaving only its core behind until the core regenerates it a full blorb again.
- **Ape Skeletons**: the Plant Kingdom's own counterpart to the ordinary skeleton above, manifesting the same way but on a primate's own frame instead of a person's — bone-white all over, no ears, and (like the ordinary skeleton) no abdomen, replaced by the same tilted-back column of spine segments. They fight and sink the same way an ordinary skeleton does, but hit harder, have more HP, and award more XP when defeated. They rise anywhere in the kingdom's wilds except in or near the primate village.
- **Training dummies**: a second Aggro type, unlocked through one of the Plant Kingdom's side quests (see Kingdoms, above). A training dummy starts as inert training equipment, harmless to walk past. Left unobserved for three in-game nights, it comes to life and behaves like a skeleton, but is noticeably harder to defeat.
- **False Hero** (see The town, under Setting, above) is the game's first named boss fight, with far more HP and a far larger XP reward than any ordinary skeleton. Defeated and fleeing, he doesn't make it out of town: a skeleton catches him at the edge of the plaza and drags him under, having failed the Demon King he secretly served. Word spreads fast afterward — the village names the player its true savior.
