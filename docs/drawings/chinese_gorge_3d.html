The Broken Plateau

The village's islands as the pieces of one plateau that broke apart over the Abyss. The palace's island is its biggest remnant. The rest of the rim shattered round it along jagged fracture lines. Each piece then drifted out on its own heading and turned a little, all swinging slightly the same way as they parted. So no two gaps are alike, and neighbouring broken edges still roughly match. One piece shattered, and its shards float bare at their own heights. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom, and pick a place to visit.

What changes

Today

Round grass discs over hanging cones, every one a circle, set evenly round the palace (switch to Built today to see it).

Proposed
  • Torn shapes. Each island's outline is a piece of the plateau. The old rim edge is rounded and weathered. The broken edges are jagged, with long bends and smaller teeth, and they face their neighbours. "Where they broke" shows the pieces fitted back together.
  • Torn undersides. Each island hangs a rock keel of its own depth, tapering unevenly to a point off its centre, banded by its strata.
  • Asymmetric drift. Each piece moved out from the palace by its own distance (26 to 52 m) and turned by its own angle (up to 18°), all swinging slightly the same way. The gaps run from 19 m to 59 m, and no two are alike.
  • Shards. The north piece, behind the palace, shattered. Four bare shards float there at their own heights, from 26 m below the village to 7 m above it.
Kept
  • Every household and role, and every island at least the land its program needs. The palace's piece holds its north–south axis.
  • The one shared surface height for every lived-on island, which the village code relies on.
  • The ring and the processional axis. Every span is within its type: chain 34 to 68 m, covered timber 19 to 37 m. The longest bridge drops from 77 m to 68 m.
  • The circular Abyss, with every island inside its full-depth radius.
