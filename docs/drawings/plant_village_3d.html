What building the model turned up

The village layout is now data with checks (tools/plant_village_layout.py), and the model is drawn from it. Placing everything at true size exposed these.

Brief error

The commons cannot be 520 m². The triangle between the three trunk centres is already 432 m². A deck that wraps the trunks with 3.5 m to spare is about 800 m², 630 m² once the trunks and their spirals are set aside. That is still more room than the program needs, so the amenities fit with routes between them.

Brief error

The rope bridges span 15.6 to 17.9 m, not 30 to 32 m. The trunks stand 30 to 34 m apart, but each household ring reaches about 7.5 m out from its trunk's centre, so the bridges only cross the gap between rings.

Review 2

The review found the village standing on stilts: eleven timber posts 11 m tall under the commons and the inn, and seven spiral ramps, 366 m of them. Neither came from the trees. Now the trees carry everything, and each tree has one ramp:

  • No stilts. Every deck bears on its trunk through a bracket collar just off the bark, the strongest support a tree offers. The commons' middle still hangs from six limbs. The inn moved 0.4 m east and lost 1.5 m of depth so that it sits in the East Tree's arms, on three saddle limbs grown just under the deck. Posts: 11 before, 0 now.
  • One ramp per tree. A 32° spiral hugs each trunk from the roots to its highest deck, hung on its own brackets, with a landing halfway up every storey and on every deck. It replaces the climbers' spiral and the public spiral on every tree, and the grand spiral round its post. Ramps: 7 before (366 m), 3 now (215 m).
  • No climbing wells. The 2 m well round every trunk existed only for the climbers' spiral. With it gone, every deck reaches the bark, and each spiral comes up through one opening beside the trunk.
  • Climbers climb. The canopy monkeys use the bark and the lianas hanging from each ring. That is their quick way, and it needs nothing built.
  • The ground legs stay on all three trees. They are the only optional part of the spirals. Each serves its own side of the floor: the gardens and the floor hut by the Elder Tree, the tailed monkeys and the stable by the North Tree, the apes by the East Tree. And each tree then reads as one ribbon from root to crown.
Placed

The brief gave no positions for the amenities on the commons. They now stand along the deck's edges with 1.5 m routes between them:

  • the market on the south edge;
  • the kitchen and dining terrace on the north-west edge;
  • the store, cistern and Jati's workshop on the north-east edge, with the public privy hung just outside the rail beside the store;
  • the gathering circle, 7.2 m across, at the middle;
  • the inn on a rounded lobe of deck east of the East Tree, on three of its limbs.
Review 3

The branches were too regular and some were bald, and the ropes all hung plumb. Now:

  • Each tree grows its own way. Limbs come mostly one at a time, now and then a pair, 2.4 to 5.2 m apart up the trunk. Each is turned roughly, never exactly, by the golden angle, with its own length, pitch, arch and sideways bend. The crowns' top lobes are uneven too. There are fewer limbs than before: 11 crown limbs and 31 that carry decks.
  • No limb is bald. A limb is grown only if it comes into leaf. A limb under a deck leafs out level, or hangs its foliage below the deck. The limbs under the bridge landings turn aside, out from under the bridge, and up into leaf.
  • Raked rope stays. Every hung part hangs in V-pairs from points along its limb, raked out to the beams it carries, so each hang is braced both ways.
Review 1

What holds it up. The first model had no limbs, and the inn's deck hung out 16 m past the East Tree on nothing. Since then:

  • The rings and the crown sit on limbs. Each deck's edges are curved ring beams of laminated bamboo, with the joists hidden under the planks. The beams are carried no more than 12 m apart: by a living limb under every pavilion and every bridge landing, and a few timber bearers between. Where a spiral rises under a deck, nothing may cross it, so that bay hangs from a limb grown above it.
  • The commons' middle hangs. It is over the gate plaza. Each tree grows two limbs at about 19 m toward the middle, and their six tips carry a hung ring beam round the gathering circle.
  • The trees are in leaf. The crowns carry two limbs a level, every 2.4 m. Every limb, the structural ones too, puts out side branches that fork once, with leaves at every tip. A limb that carries a pavilion runs on past it into leaf. Nothing grows into a deck, a bridge or a walk.
  • Checked. The plan tool checks every bearer and limb against the spiral's headroom, and that no part of any deck is left without something under or over it.
Built today

The game still has the prototype village:

  • the three giant trees with their steep branch ramps;
  • a 3.6 m treehouse deck on every fourth landing;
  • the inn on the ground at (27, 22).

None of the commons, rings, bridges, ground camp or switchbacks exist yet. The trees' branch ramps climb at 46°, and the model draws their chain from the same rule as nature_props.gd. Their starting directions are random in the game, so the model's are approximate.
