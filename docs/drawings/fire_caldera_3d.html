How the stewardship works

  1. Crown annulus. The former broad lava mouth becomes a settling reservoir around a 34 m opening. Gates meter flow instead of allowing an uncontrolled sheet.
  2. Three falls. The civic Heartfall, Forge Fall and gentler Renewal Fall descend through separate sectors, leaving clear views and swimming routes between them.
  3. Terraced sphere. Public, scientific and industrial rooms occupy rings following the inner shell. No arbitrary stack of flat basement floors hides the spherical volume.
  4. Collection and separation. The lowest basin receives the falls, separates gas and heavy mineral load, and divides useful flow from an emergency bypass.
  5. Return. Three enclosed risers exploit pressure and thermal convection, assisted by valve stations, to return lava to the crown and the city's service spine.
  6. Relief. Excess pressure travels outward and uphill to the existing safe western and far shelves—not toward the arrival route or glass fronts.

Questions this model is meant to answer

  • Does the 84 m sphere feel monumental without making the fourteen-person city absurdly large?
  • Should the Heartfall stay on the Civic axis, or move off-axis like Jewel's Rain Vortex?
  • Are three falls enough to make stewardship legible, or does the system need one quieter overflow veil?
  • Should visitors see the bottom collection basin from the crown gallery, or discover it only after the Lava Helm?
  • Does the Crown Gallery need one dry lift back to Civic, or should every deeper route remain lava-only?

Jewel Changi supplies the organizational precedent: one falling element seen from many levels, terraced circulation around a deep void, and a visible recirculation system. Fire Kingdom keeps its own basalt, steel, glass, heat, hazard and civic logic.
