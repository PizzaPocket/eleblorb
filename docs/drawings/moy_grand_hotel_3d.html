The Grand Hotel in 3D

Moy's first tall building, redesigned in the manner of John Portman's atrium hotels: a white concrete block of twelve levels round an atrium 44 m high, and a ribbed cylinder tower whose glass lifts ride in slots let into its face, from the atrium floor to the revolving Windrose in the drum. True size, on its block on Civic Plaza, with its neighbours as plain blocks at their planned heights. Turn on the cut-away to look into the atrium.

What building the model turned up

The hotel's dimensions are data in the city's plan tool (HOTEL in tools/city_layout.py), with checks, and this model and the plan drawings are drawn from it. The brief is docs/architecture/city.md, section 8.

Resolved

The old plan and the old brief disagreed: the plan file had the guest tower at 25 floors and 97 m, the brief at 21 floors and 91 m to a 100 m lantern. Both now read from one set of levels: ground 6.5 m, mezzanine 5 m, then 3.25 m a level, so level 12 tops out at 44 m (the atrium block's roof) and level 28 at 96 m (the tower's roof), with the drum to 108 m. The plan tool fails if they drift apart.

Resolved

The roof, the lifts, the chandelier and the rain, designed together. Three earlier attempts fixed one fault at a time and made another: capsules running up through the skylight's glass, a chandelier hung off a slim glazing truss, a solid roof collar across the lifts' path. The requirements, taken together:

  • daylight the 44 m void through shaded bronze glass;
  • let the stack's warm air out at the atrium's highest point;
  • shed every rain, including a desert cloudburst, without any water running against a joint round something that pierces the roof, and keep every drop (Moy uses its water twice);
  • hang an 8 t chandelier over the rock garden, lower it 10 m once a year, service its winch from inside, and lower it by hand if the power fails;
  • let four glass capsules ride from the atrium floor up the outside of the tower to the Windrose, in view of the plaza, with nothing across their path;
  • keep clear of the galleries, the tower and each other.

The answer:

  • The lifts ride in the tower, not past the roof. Four glazed slots are let into the tower's south face, from the atrium floor to the drum. Each keeps the tower's rhythm: it is two of the ribs' bays wide, centred where one rib is left out and framed by the ribs either side, its glass in the plane of the windows. Across the face onto the atrium the bays run glass, slot, glass, glass, slot, glass either side of the rib at due south, the one the chandelier's arm leaves by, and the drum's tank ribs and the Windrose's mullions carry the same 40 ribs on up. The capsules ride inside the tower's outline, their faces showing through glass flush with the facade, their doors opening into the tower's lobbies at every level. No roof crosses their path, because the roof stops at the tower's face.
  • The skylight is one pitch, falling away from the tower. It is high (+47 m) along the tower and low (+44.6 m) on the leaning front, 7.6° at the corners and 11° in the middle, so the rain always runs away from the tower. Nothing pierces it.
  • Air and rain at the top. Along the tower, a band of louvres from +47 to +48 m lets the warm air out at the atrium's highest point. Above it, the tower gutter catches the rain off the tower's face and runs it east and west round the tower to the side wings' roofs.
  • Rain at the bottom. The glass sheds into a box gutter on the front, falling 1:100 to an outlet at each end, inside the east and west wings. Every pipe runs down inside a pier to the rain cistern under the forecourt. The cistern holds 307 m³, more than a 50 mm cloudburst on the whole roof. It feeds the runnel, and overflows to the street drains and the park's reed pond.
  • The chandelier hangs from the tower. A steel box arm is cantilevered from the tower's core. It runs out between the two middle lift slots, 0.6 m or more under the glass, to a pulley block 9 m out over the rock garden. The winch room is inside the tower on level 13, behind the arm, reached from the service core, with a handwheel to lower the chandelier if the power fails. It hangs on four ropes, two to hoist and two for safety.

tools/city_layout.py checks each of these.

Resolved

The chandelier now hangs 16 m down a void 32 m across, so it is 12 m or more from any gallery. The old brief's perch, "reached from level 4's gallery by a jump", cannot work any more. Its crown becomes a Leaf Hat swing anchor instead, 24.5 m above the mezzanine, for swinging gallery to gallery; it is still a perch for a hero who swings onto it. The plan tool checks that it clears every gallery by 2 m, the tower by 4 m and the glass lifts by 1.5 m, and that lowered 10 m for its yearly cleaning it clears the pavilion's roof.

Resolved

The leaning front narrows the void as it rises: 26 m north to south at the floor, 18 m at the top (with the tower's curve, 12 m at its narrowest). That is as far as it can lean, 0.8 m a level, before the chandelier and the capsule lifts crowd each other. The double ramp, curving round the rock garden, rises 6.5 m over about 12.9 m at 27°, inside the world's 32°.

Decide
  • The Deck's route (revised 2026-10-17). Civic Plaza now runs from the park to City Hall with nothing in front of the hotel but lawn and water, so the Deck stays off the square: a short glazed skybridge crosses the Park Ring west from the Park Deck into the mezzanine (Portman's skybridges).
  • The party's suite moves from floor 20 to level 27, the top guest floor under Lucinda's flat, so the ride up the outside of the tower is long enough to be the hotel's moment.
  • The library and city hall. The brief had the hotel and the library as an art deco pair from the same boom. The hotel is still of that boom, but the library and city hall are now the boom's stone pair, and the hotel its daring exception. Is that the story you want?
Open
  • The rest price.
  • Whether the Windrose, the revolving lounge, is realised inside (proposed: faux, its ring of light turning).
  • Whether the capsules' roofs can be ridden as moving ledges up the tower.
Approximate

The model is a sketch in simple solids: boxes, cylinders and SuperEgg shapes for the capsules, rocks, trees and pavilion roof. Rooms behind the glass are not modelled; the neighbours are plain blocks at their planned heights.
