Sea Folk City Floor Plans

The whole city now stands inside walls with a roof: an octagon of stone from the seabed to 24 m above the sea, with a fountain on top and the way down hidden in it. The classical city stays on the seabed, and its heritage is kept, used and in places brought into the dry under new glass. The modern city is whole SuperEgg bubbles with floors, held in the water by their own ballast, and joined by glass tunnels into one network you can walk from the floor up to the crown ring 30 m above it. No cables, no legs, no bird-cage ribs. Every drawing here is generated from tools/sea_folk_third_pass.py, which checks the fits, clearances and circulation.

The guest hall

The walls and the roof

The first city's sea wall, in a dark sturdy stone, rises to just above the water. On it the city built upper walls and a roof in a lighter stone, in the classical manner, when it closed itself. Twelve piers carry a coffered vault. Eight glass wells and the fountain's glass floor let daylight down. Four sea gates stand shut on the avenues.

A fortress outside, a covered harbour within; the oldest stone at the bottom.

Tunnels and trunks

The floor-level tunnels lie on the old ring arcade, its bridges over the Ring Current, and the avenue colonnade, so swimmers still pass beneath through the arches. The four glass trunks rise from the stumps of the old ring towers.

New glass carried on old masonry, kept distinct from it and never cutting into it.
  • The old is never altered. New glass stands at least 1.5 m clear of a heritage wall and touches old work only at a nacre collar that could be taken off again. Old and new stay easy to tell apart.
  • Tidehall is kept as it is, under glass: the palace, now dry, inside the civic hall. Over the mound nothing modern stands but the civic hall and the central trunk that drops into it.
  • The first city stays where it fell. Its spires are never restored and never taken down, and nothing modern touches them.
  • The new structure language: whole SuperEgg bubbles; frameless curved glass; a few broad nacre bands at floor lines; a nacre ballast keel under each bubble in the water. That keel holds it steady, so nothing hangs on cables or stands on legs.
  • One network at one pressure (proposed, 2026-10-14). All the glass you can walk in is one body of air, so the trunks' ramps and the tunnels need no doors. Its pressure is the sea's at the datum, the walking floor at the network's foot, 4.4 m up. Open moon pools work only there: a swimmer rises into the pool and steps out. Above the datum the air is at a higher pressure than the sea, so every way in up there is a lock chamber with two hatches, which the hall's own air blows dry. Floors below the datum get their pool on a nacre drum whose rim is at the datum.

Circulation

Plato's rings, built twice. On the floor, the civic ring tunnel runs on the old arcade round the open column and threads the Exchange and the guest hall. It has four spokes out over the Ring Current to the trunks, and the avenue tunnel runs out to the Glassworks. Thirty metres up, the crown ring joins the four trunks. You can walk up any trunk, round the crown ring and down another without touching water. Swimmers come and go through an open moon pool in every building on the floor and at every trunk foot, all at the datum, and through a lock chamber into every hall above it and from each hall into its homes. The civic link runs in from the ring to the civic hall, and the central trunk climbs from there through the column to the shaft under the fountain: the secret way in from the roof.
