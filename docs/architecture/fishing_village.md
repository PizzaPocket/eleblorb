# Crossroads Fishing Village

Status: design brief in review. This is the next settlement planning pass after
the remaining Snow Village visual and walked checks. Chinese Village
implementation is held until this pass has an approved population, plan and
style charter.

## Design premise

The settlement is a small, cosmopolitan lake community with a long maritime
memory. Its families did not all arrive together or share one ancestry. Some
descend from fishers and boat dwellers, some from craftspeople and traders who
stayed after a season at the Crossroads, and some joined more recently. Their
shared culture comes from living and working on water, maintaining one another's
platforms and boats, and adapting several inherited practices to a freshwater
lake.

The primary built reference is the Clan Jetties along Weld Quay in George Town,
Penang. These began as working landing jetties used by Chinese Hokkien immigrant
communities engaged in cargo handling, boat work, fishing and waterfront trade;
shelters and communal houses accumulated along the working spines. Their useful
lesson is therefore not a Peranakan shophouse style. It is the growth of a
waterfront neighborhood from work, mooring and access: a principal plank jetty,
side branches, stilt houses, sheds and water approaches forming a fishbone plan.

The game must translate that spatial intelligence rather than reproduce one
real clan, ethnicity, religion, costume or decorative vocabulary. The village's
people remain internationally varied, as in Ohio and the Snow Village. Its
families are organized by mutual work and chosen community rather than shared
surnames. Watercraft knowledge is a lived occupation, not an exotic costume.

Because the Crossroads lake is fresh water, its pearl work centers on cultivated
freshwater mussels and clams rather than unexplained marine oyster beds. Ocean
shells may later enter through ordinary trade, but the Ocean Kingdom portal is
not used as a shortcut to explain the local economy.

## What exists now

`scripts/floating_village.gd` currently builds:

- one 46 metre straight main dock and one perpendicular cross-dock;
- seven anonymous, sealed box huts in two rows;
- six decorative boats with no owners, routes or boarding use;
- one generic Lake Diver and a merchandise counter;
- three swim ramps, four lanterns, and a separate Ocean portal deck.

The lake shop, safe routes out of the water, the Ocean portal, and the current
draft's lively varied color palette are anchors to preserve. The strip plan,
anonymous huts, uniform spacing and decorative-only boat population are not.
The existing terracotta, teal, ochre, violet, coral-red and green family should
remain visible in the rebuilt settlement rather than being standardized into a
mostly brown fishing village.

## Settlement structure

The recommended plan adapts the Clan Jetties' fishbone logic to a lake
settlement anchored by two modest natural rock islets. One principal public
jetty joins the arrival islet to the communal heart. Short side branches serve
homes and work decks; secondary gangways bend with the sheltered water rather
than forcing the entire settlement into a rigid rectangle. The result can form
a loose crescent in the lake while every branch still has a legible structural
and social origin.

1. **Arrival landing and market deck.** A broad public landing receives the
   route from shore. The lake shop, covered waiting space and community notice
   objects face this deck. It is large enough for the player, Blorbus, a mount
   and several party members to turn without entering a household work area.
2. **Communal pavilion.** A shaded, open-sided meeting and meal pavilion sits at
   the hinge of the boardwalk network. It is the settlement's social center,
   storm muster point and shared repair space, not a ceremonial building copied
   from a real culture.
3. **Household cluster.** Four or five family homes occupy short spurs off the
   public loop. Front verandas face the shared water court; kitchens, washing
   platforms and working edges face outward. No public route passes through a
   private wet-work deck.
4. **Pearl and shell yard.** Clean sorting tables, submerged mussel baskets,
   grading trays, shell storage and a small craft bench form one legible work
   sequence. Cultivation lines stay outside swimming and boat lanes.
5. **Fishing and processing edge.** Net mending, catch sorting, a screened
   drying shelter and a compact smokehouse sit downwind on the outer edge. This
   area has direct boat access and does not occupy the village's main walkway.
6. **Boatwright slip.** A sloped haul-out, timber rack, tool shelter and one boat
   under repair explain who keeps the fleet working.
7. **Mor guest houseboat.** A broad, permanently moored travelling houseboat is
   the village's equivalent of an inn and its functional rest point. It serves
   travellers and preserves the community's mobile lineage without making every
   building a floating prop.
8. **Ocean portal landing.** The portal keeps its own generous, collidable
   platform and return clearance, connected to the public loop but clear of
   homes, cultivation lines and boat approaches.

The main circulation is a working jetty spine with one secondary return route,
not the present cross. Flexible gangways join floating structures to
pile-supported platforms. Household spurs end at homes. Work spurs end at a
named task. Open water lanes remain wide and visibly lead to the lake. Every
route has a destination.

## Population and economy

The village has fifteen residents in five households. The households formed at
different times and are tied together through marriage, adoption, work and the
shared upkeep of the jetties. Nobody is an anonymous population marker.

Dala's generation established the first permanent piles beside the largest
limestone islet. The Mor houseboat returned seasonally for years before taking a
permanent mooring. Nara grew up on the lake; Mateo arrived aboard a damaged
cargo boat and stayed after repairing more than his own hull. The Vales began as
seasonal fishers from the far shore. Asha and Rian came through as travelling
healer and sail-mender, then remained after one storm season made clear how much
the village needed both. This history gives the community several lineages
without assigning the village itself one ethnicity.

### Venn household: landing house, shop and boatyard

- **Nara Venn**, 38, is the existing Lake Diver given a name and a life. She
  free-dives, keeps the lake shop, buys shells, clams and lakeweed, and serves as
  dockwarden when visiting boats arrive. She knows the basin well enough to
  recognize when a current or water level has changed.
- **Mateo Venn**, 42, Nara's husband, is the village boatwright and pile
  carpenter. He maintains hulls, platforms, flexible gangways and moorings. He
  builds the metal-and-timber body of the Diving Helmet from imported fittings;
  Rian Sen seals and finishes it.
- **Lio Venn**, 16, their adopted child, apprentices with both parents. Lio
  carries orders between households, tends the landing lights, rows the shop
  skiff and would rather learn the deep channels than spend another day sorting
  nails.

Their home and shop face the arrival landing. The boatyard and haul-out lie
along the outer side, so sawing and hull work do not block customers. Their
narrow dive skiff has the closest public berth.

### Aran household: pearl house and mussel yard

- **Mai Aran**, 45, manages the freshwater mussel beds. She sets and rotates the
  submerged baskets, grades shell, records water conditions and selects the few
  pearls worth keeping.
- **Salim Aran**, 47, Mai's husband, fishes the shallower edges and makes and
  repairs most of the village's nets. He and Jori Vale share heavy gear but fish
  different water.
- **Dala Aran**, 72, Salim's mother, is a retired boat captain and the village's
  most trusted weather reader. She calls the storm muster, teaches the children
  the old channel marks and refuses to let experience become an excuse for
  ignoring what the lake is doing now.
- **Pree Aran**, 11, Mai and Salim's daughter, attends Asha's lessons and tends
  the household's herb floats. She has begun learning pearl grading but is not
  treated as village labor.

The Aran house stands beside the clean pearl and shell yard. Its wet stair
reaches the cultivation baskets without crossing the public jetty. A broad,
stable work punt carries baskets but does not enter the swimming channel.

### Vale household: fishing house and processing edge

- **Jori Vale**, 34, captains the village's broad working fishing boat. They set
  deeper lines and traps, coordinate the shared fishing grounds and take the
  first rescue watch when weather turns.
- **Osei Vale**, 37, Jori's husband, receives and sorts the catch. He runs the
  screened drying shelter and smokehouse and cooks there on workdays, while
  Leena Mor handles communal meals and guests.
- **Tavi Vale**, 9, their son, studies with Asha. He makes cork floats and knows
  every boat by its sound, but adults keep him off the outer work deck when a
  catch comes in.

Their house and drying shelter occupy the downwind outer branch. The working
boat has a straight route from its berth into open water; catches never travel
through the communal pavilion or pearl yard.

### Mor household: guest houseboat and cargo landing

- **Leena Mor**, 56, keeps the communal pavilion and the moored guest houseboat.
  She organizes shared meals, receives travellers and knows which household can
  spare a bed, a tool or an hour of work.
- **Ivo Mor**, 61, Leena's husband, is the village ferryman and cargo carrier.
  His regular run exchanges fish, smoked catch and shellwork for Ohio bread,
  berries, pitch, metal fittings and other supplies the lake cannot produce.
- **Sela Mor**, 25, their daughter, pilots the fast utility boat, assists Nara on
  difficult dives and leads water rescues. She intends to take over the ferry
  route eventually, but not while Ivo still pretends he never misses a marker.

The Mor houseboat is permanently moored in the sheltered cove but remains
visibly capable of travel. Its shoreward deck serves guests; cargo transfers at
the opposite end so luggage and crates do not obstruct the sleeping cabin.

The boat is an approximately 18 by 7 metre shallow-draft timber vessel with a
long weather roof and deep side eaves. A covered arrival deck enters a common
cabin where Leena receives guests and serves meals. The galley and dry pantry
sit beside that room with their own service door to the cargo deck. A central
party dormitory is large enough for the player and a large travelling party;
two smaller guest cabins open from the same passage. The Mor family's private
cabin occupies the stern behind a closing door. A compact contained dry toilet
and wash space stand against the outer service wall, never discharging into the
lake.

Leena offers a night's rest for **10 Tokoins**, the ordinary Crossroads rate.
The transaction shows the price and Tokoin HUD through the shared transaction
interface. Resting fades to black, restores the party and melted blorbs, advances
the world to morning, and plays the established waking sequence in the dormitory
bed. The player stands beside that bed afterward; party members gather in the
common cabin and covered arrival deck rather than spawning in the water or
inside furniture. This becomes the last rest point for the Crossroads world.

### Sen household: clinic, school room and shell workshop

- **Asha Sen**, 32, is the village teacher, healer and record keeper. She teaches
  Lio, Pree and Tavi together in the morning, keeps medical supplies and weather
  records, and treats diving and boatyard injuries. Her work gives the village a
  necessary occupation unrelated to harvesting the lake.
- **Rian Sen**, 29, Asha's younger brother, makes rope, net floats, shell buttons
  and pearl settings from the usable by-products of the catch. He seals Mateo's
  Diving Helmets and repairs canvas and waterproof gear.

Their compact house and workroom stand beside the communal pavilion. The clinic
door faces the public route; Rian's dustier cutting and sealing bench opens onto
a separate rear deck.

### Community organization

The village has no hereditary chief. One adult from each household meets at the
communal pavilion when shared decisions are needed. Nara controls arriving
traffic, Dala calls weather closures, Mateo can close an unsafe structure, Asha
keeps the written decisions, and Leena coordinates food and shelter during a
storm. These are responsibilities, not ranks.

The three children are friends without being interchangeable. Lio is nearly an
adult and works an apprenticeship; Pree is absorbed by living things and water
conditions; Tavi is mechanically attentive and young enough to be kept clear of
dangerous work. Their routes connect the households to the school room and make
the village feel inhabited beyond work shifts.

The trade ledger must explain every display item. Fish move from boat to sorting
deck to kitchen, smoker or trader. Mussels move from submerged baskets to the
grading table; edible catch and usable shell separate there. Lakeweed is washed
and dried away from fish processing. Timber and repair supplies arrive by cargo
boat or from Ohio trade. Nothing appears on a stall because it is generically
"nautical."

The current lake assortment has these sources:

- **Diving Helmet:** Mateo builds the housing from imported fittings and Rian
  seals it; Nara tests and sells it.
- **Corroded Pocket Compass:** Ivo acquires damaged instruments through cargo
  trade; Rian cleans them, though their unreliable needles remain part of what
  they are.
- **Pitch Torch:** Ivo carries pitch and torch fittings from Ohio; Mateo assembles
  and Nara sells them.
- **Rye Loaf and Dried Berries:** Ivo brings these on his Ohio run. Leena keeps
  the food stock and supplies Nara's sale shelf.
- **Blorb Slime:** Nara receives the rare item by commission from travelling
  merchants. It is not harvested or manufactured by the fishing village.
- **Lake Shell, Freshwater Clam and Lakeweed:** Nara buys these from the player
  and other gatherers. Mai grades shell and clams, while Rian uses suitable
  shell and Leena or Asha takes usable lakeweed.

## Architectural charter

### Foundations and movement

- Permanent public decks and larger homes use timber piles in the shallows or
  tie into rock islets. Floating pontoons are reserved for houseboats, small
  work decks and elements that plausibly need to move with water level.
- Flexible gangways visibly mediate between fixed and floating levels. They use
  broad, gentle pitches and have enough width and head clearance for the game's
  traversal bodies.
- Deck boards run in structurally intelligible directions over beams. Piles,
  braces and mooring posts meet the structures they support.
- Retain at least three broad public swim ramps, distributed around the village
  so every major public cluster has a convenient way out of the lake. Their
  upper ends finish flush with the deck, with no lip or submerged collision to
  catch a swimmer. Their lower ends descend to approximately the height of the
  human player's feet in the normal surface-floating pose, slightly below that
  point where needed to guarantee contact. Determine this from the shared water
  level and playable-character swim pose rather than copying one absolute world
  height. The complete slope remains collidable and usable by the human,
  Blorbus and Xiao Hou Zi. Swim ramps open onto clear water and never collide
  with huts, boats, moorings or cultivation gear. Low boarding landings serve
  working boats separately and do not replace the swim exits.

### Houses and shared buildings

- Broad, high roofs, deep eaves, shaded verandas and generous cross-ventilation
  answer heat and rain. Roofs may be hipped, gabled or combine a main mass with
  a lower veranda, but each form follows drainage and structure.
- Walls are light infill within a visible timber frame. Open panels, woven
  screens, shutters and half-height doors create privacy while keeping air
  moving. Buildings are not sealed boxes.
- Covered walkways and verandas are both weather protection and social space.
  They should form useful thresholds, not decorative awnings.
- The project design language remains soft-edged: posts, beams, hulls and trim
  use appropriately squarish SuperEgg forms. This does not justify swollen
  structural members or hiding bad junctions behind rounded shapes.
- The current working draft's varied palette is a cultural and visual
  non-regression requirement. Preserve its recognizable terracotta, teal,
  ochre, violet, coral-red and green range, along with natural timber. Apply
  those colors coherently by household, boat and work area, using sun-faded
  finishes and a few repeated accents rather than random color on every panel.
  The rebuild may replace the shared Ohio roof implementation, but it must not
  erase the color variety that gives this settlement its identity.
- No sacred roof form, ethnic emblem or historic ornament is copied merely to
  signal Southeast Asia. Original rope, shell, wave and basket patterns may
  emerge from the village's own work, used sparingly.

### Water, sanitation and weather

- Roofs visibly collect rainwater into household jars or shared tanks. Drinking
  water does not come straight from the working lake edge.
- Toilets use sealed or serviced containment. Nothing visually discharges into
  the lake beside homes, swimmers or mussel beds.
- Buildings show storm logic: shutters close, roofs tie down, loose gear has
  storage, boats have secure moorings, and the communal pavilion has a clear
  muster function.
- Reeds and bank planting belong at the shore connection. Floating planters may
  carry herbs and flowers near homes. Tiny decks do not receive miniature land
  gardens that could not function there.

## Boats

The fleet begins with three useful types, built at correct game scale and owned
in the settlement plan:

- narrow paddle skiffs for one or two fishers;
- broad working boats carrying nets, baskets and catch;
- one roofed family or guest houseboat with a real interior and mooring.

A cargo launch may be added only when its Ohio or lake trade route is authored.
Boats at rest use ropes, fenders and clear berths. Moving boats need explicit
water routes and boarding behavior before they are animated. Decorative boats
must not block swim exits or the Ocean portal.

## Landscape and water use

- Preserve open swimming water through and around the settlement.
- Group mussel baskets and fish pens by current, depth and access rather than in
  a decorative grid.
- Use the two rock islets for limited shade vegetation, repair anchors and
  pauses in the deck network. They should remain natural rocks, not disguised
  rectangular foundations.
- Add ropes, floats, drying lines, traps and baskets only at the work stage that
  uses them. Empty water is an important part of the composition.
- Night lighting hangs beneath eaves or from working posts at junctions. Avoid
  freestanding "lights on sticks."

## Gameplay requirements

- Preserve the lake shop's buy and sell behavior, underwater helmet guidance,
  swim exits and Ocean Kingdom portal.
- Preserve a minimum of three unobstructed public swim exits. Validate each one
  from an in-water approach: the lower ramp must meet a surface-floating human
  at about foot height, the upper ramp must meet its deck flush, and the full
  route must let the human, Blorbus and Xiao Hou Zi climb out without jumping.
- Keep a continuous public route usable by the human, Blorbus and mounts. Audit
  low rails, roof eaves, gangway changes in height, household thresholds and
  boat slips at all three scales.
- Interaction volumes for residents, chests, doors and merchandise must be
  floor-aware so a character cannot operate them through another deck.
- Water uses the shared liquid rendering and underwater tint system.
- Boat collision must match visible hulls. A mounted character may not pass
  through piles, walls, boats or work racks.
- The settlement is protected from hostile encounters, but working water and
  open deck edges remain physically honest.

## Implementation sequence

1. Survey lake depth, shoreline, portal return location and current terrain
   collisions. Choose the sheltered crescent and rock-islet locations.
2. Author `FishingVillagePlan` as the only source for households, structures,
   routes, work areas, boats, trade and schedules.
3. Build the approved fifteen-person census and household relationships into
   the plan, schedules and world bible.
4. Build and validate public circulation, at least three correctly submerged
   swim exits, boat lanes and portal clearance before any house shell.
5. Build the architectural kit and one representative home, one work deck and
   one boat as isolated proofs.
6. Construct the village, then interiors, water work and landscape in that
   order.
7. Add resident schedules and dialogue only after the plan is stable. Dialogue
   receives a final `no-ai-slop` pass.
8. Run an aerial, eye-level, water-level and underwater audit, followed by
   walked tests with the human, Blorbus and a mounted character.

## Decisions to approve before construction

- Whether freshwater pearls are ordinary cultivated valuables or carry a small
  magical quality. The architectural plan works either way.
- Whether working boats become rideable in this pass or remain scheduled
  environmental traffic until vehicle boarding is generalized.

## Research principles

The reference research is used for spatial and working-water reasoning:

- George Town World Heritage Incorporated records the Clan Jetties as rows of
  timber houses on stilts connected by plank walkways. The jetties grew from
  cargo landings and moorings into places of work and residence.
- George Town's conservation material describes the characteristic fishbone
  organization: the timber jetty is the principal access and communication
  spine, with premises branching from it.
- The historic communities were surname clans, including Chew, Tan, Lee, Lim,
  Yeoh and others. That history should be understood accurately but not copied
  into this cosmopolitan fictional settlement.
- Broader Gulf of Thailand fishing and boat-community references may still
  inform working boats, diving and water livelihoods. They do not supply a
  generic architectural costume.
