# Fire Caldera Heartworks

Status: **proposed for spatial review, 2026-10-15**. The surface city is built;
the Heartworks is not yet part of the playable world. Its dimensioned proposal
lives in `FireCalderaPlan.HEARTWORKS`, `HEART_LEVELS`, `HEART_FALLS` and
`HEART_RISERS`. The explorable cutaway is
`docs/drawings/fire_caldera_3d.html`.

## 1. Premise

The caldera's lava mouth is no longer only a broad filled basin. It is the
upper opening of a great spherical structure inside the volcano: the civic and
thermal **Heartworks**. A crown reservoir gathers lava around the opening;
three controlled falls descend through the hollow sphere; six inhabited,
working and inspection rings follow the inner shell; a deep collection basin
separates useful flow, gas and mineral load; and three enclosed return risers
bring molten material back to the crown and the city's buried service spine.

This makes the Fire people active stewards of the volcano. Their settlement is
not merely protected from volcanic power and does not passively consume it.
They read, divide, cool, return and relieve its flows as their central civic
work. The city is visibly one large instrument maintained over generations.

## 2. The Jewel Changi precedent

Jewel Changi supplies four spatial ideas, not an appearance or a program:

1. one falling element is visible from many levels and organizes a very large
   interior;
2. terraces and crossings turn a deep void into public space rather than an
   obstacle;
3. the falling medium changes the chamber's light, air and sound;
4. collection and recirculation are part of the building's environmental
   system.

The Fire translation is not a glass airport, indoor forest, mall or literal
copy of the Rain Vortex. It uses the approved volcanic-modern charter:
cast-basalt shell, exposed blackened steel, large protected glass, mineral
lenses, controlled molten light and Gaudí-informed branching metal only at
named supports, rails, screens and gates. No plants survive in the chamber.

## 3. Principal section

All depths below are measured from the crown lava datum.

| Element | Dimension | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Crown reservoir | about 52 m outer mean radius | retains the approved surface city's bank and promenade relationship |
| Oculus | 17 m radius / 34 m diameter | large enough to reveal the sphere and divide the falls, small enough to leave a useful crown annulus |
| Hollow sphere | 42 m inner radius / 84 m diameter | holds six distinct rings without making them flat basement storeys |
| Sphere centre | 38.4 m below crown | makes the 34 m oculus the true cut through the sphere's upper cap |
| Structural shell | 2.4 m conceptual thickness | allows a continuous refractory shell, embedded steel ribs and service chases |
| Collection basin | 12 m radius, 69 m below crown | receives every gravity fall above the lower return gallery while leaving a structural bottom below it |
| Public/service ring width | 4.2 m | carries swimmers, stewards, equipment and protected stopping bays |

The sphere is not an unsupported perfect bubble inserted into loose rock. Its
refractory inner shell is tied into the volcano by broad radial basalt webs at
the occupied rings and by denser structure around the crown and collection
basin. Those webs create pressure compartments and equipment bays without
breaking the perception of one room. The natural volcanic mass outside the
shell remains visible at selected service cuts and instrument sockets.

## 4. Surface crown and reveal

The existing 52 m mean reservoir becomes a **crown annulus**. It still reads
from the surface as the city's source of heat and light, but its central 34 m
is open. The annulus performs four jobs:

- settles gas and heavy inclusions before lava reaches a public fall;
- stores enough flow to keep individual falls steady while one gate is closed;
- supplies the household thermal spine without routing the hottest material
  beneath the guest house;
- forms a visible safety interval between the oculus and the existing bank.

The surface promenade, Highline, Tempering Gate, plots and arrival-to-Civic
sightline remain. The first view through the opening shows the broad Heartfall
on the far Civic side and glimpses of several rings. It does not expose every
room or the bottom basin at once.

The **Crown Gallery**, 4 m below the lava datum, is the one dry interior ring.
It is reached from protected surface overlooks and lets a visitor understand
the chamber before the Lava Helm. Its floor is structural glass and cooled
basalt in alternating bays, with opaque refuge pockets between views. It has
real guards, fall-control stations and two directions back to the surface.

## 5. The six rings

The rings follow the inner spherical section. They are interrupted where a
fall, return riser, structural web or room requires a bay; none is a generic
continuous balcony pasted onto the wall.

| Ring | Depth | Access | Program |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `H0` Crown Gallery | 4 m | dry, before Lava Helm | oculus overlook, public fall controls, first chamber view |
| `H1` Steward Ring | 14 m | Lava Helm | Renewal chamber `L0`, primary manifold `L1`, daily flow balancing |
| `H2` Civic Ring | 26 m | Lava Helm | deep observatory `L2`, fired archive `L3`, broad public crossings |
| `H3` Works Ring | 38 m | Lava Helm | high-temperature forge `L4`, materials bay `L5`, protected lifts to Kel and Vara |
| `H4` Deep Inspection Ring | 52 m | Lava Helm | fall inspection, sampling, isolation stations, pressure compartments |
| `H5` Return Gallery | 66 m | Lava Helm | collection gates, return-riser intakes, emergency bypass |

Each ring has at least two routes onward. Curved passages connect neighboring
programs around the void, never through a fall. The approved surface immersion
entries remain the three public approaches: Renewal to `H1`, Civic to `H2`,
and Kel to `H3`. The service lifts remain Kel–`L4`, Vara–`L5`, and
Civic–`L3`; they move material and do not replace public circulation.

## 6. The lava cycle

### 6.1 Intake and settling

Lava arrives at the crown through natural pressure and the city's existing
thermal branches. The broad annulus slows it. Refractory baffles let gas leave
through instrumented vents and let heavy mineral load settle where Pela and
the stewards can sample it. Three gate houses meter the falls. Their machinery
is embedded in the crown and read as glass-fronted valve rooms, not decorative
exposed pipes.

### 6.2 Controlled falls

- **`F0` Heartfall**, 9 m wide, stands on the far Civic bearing. It is the
  chamber's public and ceremonial fall and descends to the central collection
  basin.
- **`F1` Forge Fall**, 5 m wide, occupies the east works sector. A splitter at
  the Works Ring diverts controlled high-temperature flow to Kel and Vara.
- **`F2` Renewal Fall**, 5.5 m wide, occupies the west sector. Its shallower
  ledges and tempered side flow support communal immersion and care.

The falls are separated sheets or braided curtains, never one solid lava
cylinder. Clear sectors between them preserve sightlines, swimming routes,
ventilation and emergency crossing. Each fall can stop independently while the
other two continue.

### 6.3 Collection and separation

The basin 69 m below the crown is a receiving and sorting work, not a lake on
the sphere's bottom. Concentric basalt lips calm the impact zone. Radial gates
divide:

- reusable flow for the three return risers;
- heavy mineral sediment for controlled recovery;
- gas and pressure for the outward relief system;
- emergency overflow to a sacrificial natural tube outside the inhabited
  shell.

No occupied route crosses directly above the impact center. The Return Gallery
looks into it from protected side bays.

### 6.4 Return and relief

Three enclosed risers follow the shell: the Aro civic return, Kel industrial
return and Civic relief return. Pressure differential and thermal convection
do most of the work; mechanical valve stations at `H1` and `H5` trim and
isolate the flow. Their presence is expressed by glowing seams, instrument
bays and occasional protected glass sections. They are not open pipes beside
public paths.

Excess pressure still travels outward and uphill toward the west and far
emergency shelves fixed in the surface plan. It never exhausts toward the
Tempering Gate, guest house or large glass fronts.

## 7. Experience and access

### Before the Lava Helm

- the Crown Gallery and surface overlooks reveal one inhabited interior;
- moving residents, control lights and the upper two rings are visible;
- every deeper entry is physically a lava route, not a locked human door or an
  instructional sign;
- unsuitable companions gather on authored dry pads.

### After the Lava Helm

- Renewal, Civic and Kel become three different depths of entry;
- the player can circle the sphere, cross between rings and swim beside—not
  blindly through—the separated falls;
- every room and ring has two escape directions;
- the deepest reward is understanding and operating the return system, not
  merely reaching the bottom.

## 8. Light, sound and environmental character

The oculus supplies a narrow changing cone of daylight. The falls light the
lower chamber orange from within; cobalt and teal mineral lenses distinguish
science, cooling and return systems. The upper rings receive reflections from
the crown, the middle rings see moving light across the curved shell, and the
bottom remains dark except for impact glow and return seams.

Sound changes by sector: a broad low fall roar in the open void, quieter
resonant galleries behind pressure doors, valve pulses at the steward ring and
deep impacts at the collection basin. Steam and gas appear only at engineered
separators and relief points. They do not fill every room with generic fog.

## 9. Review decisions before implementation

1. Keep the sphere at 84 m diameter, reduce it, or let its bottom extend still
   deeper?
2. Keep the Heartfall on the arrival-to-Civic axis, or shift it off-axis as
   Jewel's waterfall was shifted by its site constraints?
3. Keep three main falls, or add one thin overflow veil used only during high
   pressure?
4. Let the Crown Gallery see the collection basin, or conceal the bottom until
   the Lava Helm descent?
5. Give the Crown Gallery a dry lift to Civic, or keep every route below it
   explicitly lava-only?
6. Is **Caldera Heartworks** useful as a working name, or should the Fire
   people use another civic name?

No live terrain, collision, hazard or story gating should be replaced until
these decisions are reviewed in the model and the resulting section is fixed.
