# P11 architectural development — the inhabited railway wall

2026-10-23. **Design study for review, not a builder handoff.** The initial
P11 model and its readiness claim are withdrawn. This document is the active
architectural brief for a27m/three-bay wall study and one20×10m bastion.

## Purpose and people

The city-side ground circuit is a public everyday walk: people pass, sit in
shade, meet a neighbour and look into the city. The upper promenade gives
long views beside a working railway. A bastion is a public viewing place
shared with the Watch's searchlight equipment, reached without crossing rails.
Maintenance remains behind a closed bay door; no new business or staff member.

The architecture must explain the wall's conversion from railway viaduct:
heavy retained masonry below, a continuous load-bearing upper band, a newer
ceramic protective face and a deliberately finished parapet. Planting and
seating belong to the construction, rather than obscuring an unresolved box.

## Architectural grammar — proposed charter, awaiting design review

Primary: modern adaptation of masonry railway infrastructure, roughly75%:
load-bearing vaulted bays, exposed pier rhythm, deep reveals, simple copings,
recessed infill panels and framed openings. Secondary: Moy's modern civic
ceramic/steel vocabulary,20%, confined to upper infill, guards, shade and
service doors. Accent5%: warm timber and restrained bronze/mauve garden detail.
High Line is a landscape/occupation reference, not a third facade style.

A shared palette, arch geometry, base/coping profile, joint rhythm and thin
steel details make wall and bastion siblings. Existing gate silhouettes remain
locked; their detailed adaptation is a later design step, not a claim that
this representative section resolves City Hall or every gate.

No battlements, mock-classical entablature, timber pagoda roofs, false vendor
fronts, arbitrary graphic bands, planted green rods or printed environmental
text. The roofed bastion is a proposed detail, not approved new lore.

Left: shaded garden seat. A wall-backed timber bench, planted back corner and
supported climber make the deep vault an occupied place. Clear central entry

Middle: maintenance enclosure, with a recessed2.4×2.7m double service door

Right: open garden belvedere niche, with matching bench and more open sightline.
Same arch/base/trim as the left, different planting/occupation. Variation is

## Ground landscape: design the beds and path together

Ground allocation remains the6m Rim annulus. The representative local strip
is v−12.5..−6.5. A3.2m path curves through it, with long planted fingers rather
than two evenly spaced lines of tufts. Paving fingers extend into planted
edges and the three threshold aprons. Benches are in the architecture's shade,
not obstructing the through-path. Draw path, bed edges and aprons from the same
geometry; no lawn sliver between doorway and paving.

Plant in overlapping masses: fine desert grasses behind low silver-green
agave/aloe rosettes, small mauve flowering/climber patches at protected niches.
Use ground cover to close soil between clumps. Heights step from low path edge
to fuller sheltered pockets; no isolated uniform sticks or random plant confetti.
This uses the existing palette; no new tree locations or deep soil on narrow caps.
Maintenance belongs to existing city works/park stewardship, no new gardener NPC.

## Bastion: a viewing room carved from the wall

The20×10m outward footprint and14m terrace remain. The base continues the
wall plinth; corner piers and recessed ceramic fields carry its cap. An
outer setback/shadow groove breaks the tall face into base, field and crown.
It must read as related infrastructure, not an unarticulated rectangular box.

The ramp is part of the building section: two10m1:10 flights12→13→14,
2.4m wide, paired within the inner roof notch, with1.8m end landings. The
open notch and lower throat share exact boundaries with deck and structure;
no slab or mass above a walkable ramp. Guards follow ramps/landings and leave
throats open. Ground public routes do not pass through maintenance rooms.

At14, a continuous2.4m clear terrace route remains v12..14.4. Beds and a bench
occupy the outer strip in deliberate pockets, not random planters across the
path. One corner has a small sheltered viewing bay: a5.8×3.8m flat steel/timber
shade canopy, underside16.7, top17.05. Two stout steel supports in the outer
bed zone anchor into the bastion; cantilever beams shade the clear route.
This is an open-bladed sunshade, not a watertight roof.
The canopy is a **new proposed architectural element** for review; the14m
structural terrace remains unchanged. It is not repeated at all seven posts
until the study is accepted. Show the support and roof section, not a floating
wafer on tiny posts. No deep rooted tree on this roof.

Searchlight housing remains at the opposite outer corner, on an anchored
pedestal clear of the path. Housing, lens and outward night sweep form one
assembly. It is subordinate to the architecture; the public view is not a gun
emplacement. Gate bells and Watch scheduling are outside this representative
study.

## Material, joints and weather

Warm grey stone plinth/rings/coping; muted reddish-brown retained masonry;
cream ceramic upper infill; dark grey-green steel; warm timber seats/shade.
Approximately60% light structural/ceramic surfaces,30% retained masonry and
stone depth,10% metal/timber/plant colour. No flat brown wall covering all parts.
Masonry joints follow arch/load direction; upper joints follow panel module.
Recesses and coping throats cast real shadows. Timber shows separate planks;
roof beams bear on columns; posts terminate on real bases. Rain falls away
from thresholds, through unobtrusive drains into existing service systems.
Night light is warm at occupied niches and low on routes, not uniform glowing
bands over every arch. No new water feature or maintenance simulation.

## Drawing set and review standard

The revised model must show coordinated city elevation, desert elevation,
ground plan, upper plan, bastion plan/roof, wall section and bastion section,
plus eye-level approach, seated niche, upper promenade and viewing terrace.
All views use the same geometry. Actual exported game hero gives scale.
Technical labels belong in the viewer, never on the architecture.

Review form and occupation **before** rollout: can one read the railway
history, solid/void depth, base/middle/cap, related bastion, useful shade,
connected paths, massed planting and a view worth stopping for? A passed
syntax/containment check is not visual approval. Record specific remaining
weaknesses; do not call the whole package ready based on the representative.

Full gate adaptation, continuous circuit/terrain placement, exact train sweep,
station transitions, production collision and final rollout schedule remain
later P11 tasks. Keep existing P10/P11 coordinate risks documented until
independently reconciled against the actual implementation; don't present
an inferred midpoint convention as new runtime acceptance evidence.
