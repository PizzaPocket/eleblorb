story details remain working proposals;
no terrain, population, or architecture should be rebuilt until the layout is

The current dimensioned proposal is `docs/architecture/fire_caldera_layout.md`.
This document owns the culture and programs; the layout document owns their

## Design premise

The settlement belongs to people whose bodies are living lava. It must not be
a human village made from dark stone. Their physiology determines its plan,
buildings, economy, customs, and relationship with the volcano.

The city occupies the level floor of the kingdom's largest caldera. Its center
is a cultivated opening into the volcano's magma system: a broad, deep,
irregular lava reservoir rather than the present small ornamental fountain.
The city grows in terraces around that reservoir and continues beneath its
surface. The volcano is not scenery behind the settlement. It is the source of
the inhabitants' heat, the subject of their science, the material of their
craft, and the place through which their community is physically connected.

## Physiology and daily life

The following model is recommended because it resolves the needs raised in the
concept without making the lava people behave like renamed humans.

- **Heat supplies bodily energy.** Direct contact with lava replenishes them
  most quickly. Radiant heat, thermal channels, and hot rock sustain them more
  slowly. Prolonged cooling makes them sluggish and causes their surface to
  crust; reheating restores their normal movement.
- **Minerals supply matter and sensation.** They can ingest selected rock,
  crystal, ore, and refined metal as trace material for repairing or altering
  their bodies. Temperature, grain, mineral content, and the way a substance
  melts provide an analogue to flavor. Minerals do not replace heat as their
  principal energy source.
- **Their visible dress is self-shaped.** Hair, skirts, and other silhouettes
  are stable expressions formed from their own molten bodies rather than
  textile clothing. Forged metal, obsidian, and crystal may be worn as tools,
  jewelry, armor, or ceremonial additions.
- **They do not require human kitchens, farms, or ordinary beds.** A home needs
  a private thermal alcove or immersion bath, a cooler resting niche, mineral
  storage, shaping tools, safe gas ventilation, and places for personal
  objects. Some homes have a shallow lava well or an entire rejuvenation room
  whose floor is molten. The current copied beds and fireplaces do not fit
  these residents.
- **Youths exist and need care and education.** They learn to hold a stable
  shape, read the mountain's heat and vibration, handle minerals safely, and
  understand eruption routes before taking on adult work.

This model leaves room for individual preference. One resident may enjoy long
immersion while another prefers a radiant slab; mineral mixtures can become a
real local cuisine without requiring agriculture.

The volcano also supplies a civic thermal utility. An engineered network of
lava ducts carries controlled molten material upward from the reservoir to
homes, baths, laboratories, and workshops. Most of that network can remain
inside walls, floors, and the caldera rock. The architecture only needs to show
where it affects use: service manifolds, inspection hatches, glowing seams,
temperature controls, and the wells or molten floors it feeds. This is the
lava people's equivalent of plumbing, heating, and an electrical grid, not a
maze of exposed decorative pipes.

## City plan

The caldera is organized as three connected bands. These are social and
thermal zones, not perfect concentric circles.

### Arrival sequence and city threshold

The kingdom gate and the city entrance must form one legible approach. Draw an
arrival axis from the kingdom gate toward the center of the caldera. Where that
axis first reaches the near bank of the central reservoir, widen the dry route
into a generous arrival forecourt. This is the city's public threshold and the
point from which a visitor first understands the reservoir, the terraces, and
the principal civic route.

The threshold is not a defensive wall or a freestanding fantasy arch. Two
sculptural pylons rise from the basalt on either side of the route. Their
blackened-metal branches curve inward and frame a tall superellipse of open air
without reducing the route's mount and party clearance. Fused clear and colored
glass sits selectively between the branches. It catches daylight from above the
caldera and volcanic light from below. The two sides are related but not
perfectly mirrored, consistent with the city's organic modernism.

Each pylon contains one narrow, controlled flame behind heat-resistant glass.
The flames draw from the city's thermal service network and function as welcome
beacons, not giant bonfires. A low glowing seam crosses the paving between the
pylons to mark the transition without becoming a barrier. From this point the
visitor route divides visibly toward the guest inn and market on one side and
the council, school, and public reservoir walk on the other. No sign needs to
explain the city.

The forecourt and approach must provide:

- at least 5 metres of clear width through the pylons;
- a continuous dry route from the kingdom gate;
- unobstructed turning and gathering space for the player, Blorbus, mounts,
  party members, and visiting residents;
- an immediate sightline across the reservoir to one civic landmark;
- no molten channel, fixture, sculpture, or vendor counter in the travel lane;
- controlled banks and rails only where the drop or reservoir edge requires
  them.

### 1. Outer visitor and exchange ring

This is the coolest and driest part of the city. The arrival route, small guest
house, armor and weapon shop, elemental-gem and mineral dealer, receiving point,
and safe gathering space belong here. Non-lava visitors can use it before
obtaining the Lava Helm. It also provides stable ground for companions who
cannot enter lava. Ordinary edible provisions are conspicuously absent;
outside visitors depend mainly on what they brought, with a small reserve kept
for the occasional guest.

The existing inn remains a functional rest point with its 25 Tokoin price, but
it becomes a minor guest house rather than a major economic or social anchor.
It serves the rare researcher, trader, or lost traveller who can tolerate the
kingdom. One broad party sleeping room, a compact receiving room, a washroom,
and a protected imported-provisions cabinet are sufficient. Its caretaker has
another primary civic role and opens the house when someone arrives. The
building still needs insulated basalt walls and clear separation from open lava
channels. Its caretaker must be an actual named resident rather than the
building name being used as a person.

### 2. Habitation and working terraces

Homes, communal recharge baths, schools, studios, laboratories, foundries, and
the council hall step around the reservoir and into the lower caldera wall.
Radial paths connect them to a broad annular route. The lava-duct network feeds
small household immersion wells, molten-floor rejuvenation rooms, communal
baths, and industrial equipment. These feeds remain largely embedded in the
architecture, with visible channels used only where access, inspection, or
public spectacle gives them a reason to be seen.

The civic hall sits on a broad terrace beside the reservoir, with one edge open
to its heat and a dry public entrance from the annular route. It is not placed
on a small island that would diminish the reservoir. Emergency routes lead from
every district toward the outer ring and the caldera ascent.

### 3. Reservoir and submerged district

The reservoir is a shaped natural vent, not a round masonry tank. Its visible
surface should occupy roughly a 28 to 32 metre radius within the current
72-metre caldera floor, subject to a traversal mock-up. Its banks are organic
volcanic shelves, reinforced only where bridges, workshops, or public landings
need a controlled edge. The floor descends far enough to make immersion and
vertical exploration meaningful.

Before the hero has the Lava Helm, glimpses of structures, moving residents,
lights, and routes beneath the surface establish that the city continues below.
After obtaining the helm, the reservoir becomes a second public realm rather
than merely a hazard. It contains:

- a deep communal immersion chamber;
- a geological observatory beside the active vent;
- a high-temperature forge and experimental materials workshop;
- lava-only passages between lower homes and work chambers;
- protected mineral and pattern archives made from fired, cast, or engraved
  media rather than paper;
- rare equipment and story encounters that reward returning with the helm.

Dry bridges, cooled stepping shelves, and lava routes form two overlapping
circulation systems. Residents can walk or swim between districts. Visitors
can understand the dry network before gaining access to the molten one.

## Civic life and occupations

The working census has **14 permanent residents**, including eleven adults or
elders and three youths or apprentices, arranged in six connected household
groups. This makes the
settlement slightly larger than Ohio's permanent population but smaller than
Ohio's usual population plus its regular day visitors. Its architecture and
infrastructure can be advanced without implying a large metropolitan economy.

Residents carry compatible secondary duties instead of every responsibility
creating another NPC. The six household programs should prioritize:

1. **Thermal stewardship:** a senior vent steward, a thermal engineer, and a
   youth learning inspection routes.
2. **Science and learning:** a geologist or seismologist, a combined teacher and
   archivist, and one child or young student.
3. **Forge household:** a metallurgist-armorer, a weaponsmith, and an older
   apprentice who also helps with heavy material handling.
4. **Glass and metal-art household:** two residents sharing structural
   glasswork, stained glass, and ornate forged work.
5. **Mineral household:** a forager and gem assessor who keeps the small public
   mineral counter, together with a specialist who prepares mineral delicacies.
6. **Care and civic household:** an elder temperer and mediator who also acts as
   occasional outside liaison and opens the guest house when needed.

The register below turns that allocation into a proposed family structure.
Council duties are shared among several households rather than consuming
separate full-time residents.

## Proposed named census

Status: approved (2026-10-06). These names deliberately avoid
English fire and geology words. A lava person has a personal name followed by a
household name; household names are inherited or adopted through family and do
not describe a profession. The current register contains fourteen permanent
residents in six households.

### Aro household: thermal works residence

- **Tovan Aro**, 63, is the senior vent steward. He reads reservoir height,
  pressure, gas, and vibration at dawn, convenes the five-seat civic council,
  and decides when a duct or lower chamber must close. He is cautious without
  being fearful and treats Lava Slide as a roaming neighbor, not an officer of
  the city. He works between the rim instruments, reservoir control gallery,
  and submerged primary manifold.
- **Miru Aro**, 38, Tovan's daughter, is the thermal engineer responsible for
  the lava-duct network. She balances household feeds, repairs valves and
  inspection hatches, and checks the guest house's insulated systems after an
  outsider arrives. She spends much of her day moving between residences and
  the lower service galleries.
- **Kes Aro**, 15, Miru's son, is apprenticed to both of them. He records gauge
  shapes, carries sealed samples, and may enter the submerged manifold only
  with an adult. His eagerness makes him useful and occasionally sends Miru
  looking for a tool he has already taken to the next inspection point.

Their compact home adjoins the thermal works without opening directly into an
industrial chamber. It has a shared immersion room, three personal resting
niches, a sample shelf, and a protected glazed outlook toward the entrance
beacons. A short service passage reaches the control gallery; the public door
faces the annular route.

### Iren household: observatory, school, and archive residence

- **Selka Iren**, 46, is the city's geologist and seismologist. She compares
  readings from the crater rim, the reservoir wall, and the deep vent and is the
  council's scientific voice during unusual activity. Lava Slide sometimes
  brings her descriptions of remote cones; she trusts the observation but
  verifies the interpretation herself.
- **Aru Iren**, 43, Selka's husband, combines teaching and archival work. He
  teaches the three young residents together in the morning, maintains cast
  basalt records and metal pattern plates in the afternoon, and records council
  decisions without turning the archive into a separate institution.
- **Mena Iren**, 10, their daughter, is the youngest resident. She studies with
  Aru, practices safe shaping in the shallow communal bath, and collects color
  fragments discarded by the glass studio. She does not have a labor schedule.

Their home stands beside a combined civic observatory, school, archive, and
council chamber. The public complex has one dry upper entrance and a lower
observation room against the reservoir wall. This consolidation is appropriate
to the population and avoids four oversized civic buildings with one worker in
each.

### Kel household: foundry and armory residence

- **Daro Kel**, 45, is a metallurgist and armorer. She controls the deep forge,
  tests alloys, fits armor, and keeps the finished-goods gallery. She holds the
  council's materials seat and will refuse a commission rather than weaken an
  alloy to meet a price.
- **Vesa Kel**, 42, Daro's younger brother, is the weaponsmith and mechanical
  fabricator. He makes blades, tools, bridge fittings, and the precise metal
  parts used by Miru and Selka. His work supplies the weapon display but does
  not require a second vendor stall.
- **Ruun Kel**, 17, Daro's daughter, apprentices to Vesa while learning Daro's
  assay methods. She moves stock between the receiving shelf, surface gallery,
  and submerged forge and is responsible for returning every tool to the wrong
  sibling's bench.

Their residence occupies a quiet terrace beside, not inside, the forge. A
protected bridge joins it to the foundry's surface receiving bay, finished
armor and weapon gallery, submerged hot-work chamber, secure alloy store, and
overhead handling route. Daro serves customers; Vesa and Ruun continue working.

### Vara household: glass and ornamental metal studio

- **Omi Vara**, 34, is the structural glassworker. He forms large clear and
  smoky panes, laboratory vessels, lenses, and the heat-resistant cells used by
  the city's controlled lights. He works slowly enough that people mistake
  precision for calm.
- **Talen Vara**, 36, Omi's wife, designs stained glass and the organic forged
  frames, screens, rails, handles, and branching supports that hold it. She
  selects no more color than a building can carry and is the person most likely
  to object when a civic commission becomes visually noisy.

Their home and studio share a daylight court but have separate entrances. The
studio contains a hot forming bay, annealing chamber, metal bench, glass rack,
design wall of colored samples, and a clean assembly floor. Their private rooms
sit behind a curved clear-glass wall overlooking the reservoir.

### Oren household: mineral house and public counter

- **Pela Oren**, 40, is a mineral forager, gem assessor, and keeper of the small
  public mineral counter. She surveys deposits outside the caldera, dives for
  dense samples in the reservoir, separates elemental gems from ordinary
  crystals, and maintains the secure assay cells. Her stock remains limited by
  the story's gem progression.
- **Savi Oren**, 33, Pela's younger brother, prepares mineral delicacies. He
  grades, crushes, heats, combines, and presents rock, crystal, ore, and metal
  for lava bodies, keeping careful note of how each mixture changes texture and
  color. His work supplies households and communal gatherings rather than a
  human food shop.

Their home sits above a combined assay room, secure gem store, mineral counter,
and preparation studio. Pela's receiving route reaches the outer dry ring
without crossing the public counter. Savi's heated work surfaces connect to the
same service spine but remain separated from untested ore.

### Nahl household: tempering hall and guest house

- **Eris Nahl**, 71, is the settlement's elder temperer and civic mediator. She
  treats cooling, cracks, and harmful mineral imbalance in the communal
  rejuvenation hall, occupies the council's care seat, and is the person other
  residents seek when a technical disagreement has become personal. She also
  keeps the small guest house because visitors are rare and because she best
  understands cooler bodies. She knew Lava Slide before most of the present
  council and does not mistake his distance from the city for disloyalty.

Eris lives in a small private suite attached to the tempering hall. The guest
house is a separate insulated volume on the entrance side of the complex, with
one broad party sleeping room, receiving room, washroom, imported-provisions
cabinet, wake marker, and safe party gathering area. A visitor never passes
through the lava-person treatment rooms to reach a bed.

### Civic structure and daily population

The proposed council has five functional seats rather than inherited offices:
Tovan for the vent and emergency system, Selka for science, Daro for materials
and industry, Aru for records and learning, and Eris for care and mediation.
Miru takes operational authority over the duct network when it is the source of
an emergency. Pela attends decisions about extraction and elemental gems but
does not need a permanent sixth seat. This arrangement remains small enough for
the whole settlement to gather when a decision affects everyone.

At an ordinary midday the player can encounter ten or eleven residents on the
surface terraces. Selka, Miru, Tovan, Daro, Vesa, Ruun, or Pela may move into a
submerged workplace according to schedule, but never all at once. Before the
Lava Helm, their movement and the visible lower lights make the inaccessible
district feel inhabited. After the helm, the same named residents can be met at
their real lower workstations rather than duplicated as extra NPCs.

Together, the named residents cover the following responsibilities rather than
leaving them to anonymous workers or another list of fire-word names.

- **Vent stewards and thermal engineers** monitor pressure, maintain channels,
  and decide when a district must be cooled or evacuated.
- **Geologists and seismologists** study the mountain through heat, vibration,
  gas, and changes in the reservoir. Their knowledge can be more advanced than
  that of neighboring kingdoms because their bodies sense the same processes.
- **Mineral foragers and miners** identify safe deposits, retrieve material,
  and understand which substances are useful, unstable, nourishing, or rare.
- **Assayers and metallurgists** test ores and operate the high-temperature
  forges.
- **Armorers and weaponsmiths** produce costly, high-quality metal equipment.
  Their shops give the Fire Kingdom a clear trade identity.
- **Gem cutters and mineral dealers** assess elemental gems and rare earths,
  keep them safely separated, and sell the subset permitted by the story's
  progression. A shop must not quietly make quest-specific gems purchasable.
- **Basalt casters, obsidian workers, and sculptors** turn lava and volcanic
  glass into architecture, tools, records, and art.
- **Glassworkers** form large structural panes, vessels, instruments, lenses,
  and colored glass using the city's precise command of heat.
- **Mineral preparers** combine stone, ore, crystal, and metal into treats and
  delicacies for lava bodies. Their work is culturally analogous to cookery,
  but none of it restores a human player's HP or masquerades as ordinary food.
- **Temperers** care for residents whose bodies have cooled, cracked, or taken
  on a harmful mineral balance. They fill part of the role of healer.
- **Teachers and apprentices** make the settlement generational rather than a
  row of adult vendors.
- **Archivists** preserve geological records, casting patterns, civic
  decisions, and the history of the volcano.
- **Council members and emergency coordinators** govern communal decisions and
  eruption response.
- **The occasional guest caretaker** understands the needs of cooler-bodied
  visitors. This is a secondary civic duty, not a full-time hospitality trade.

Inter-kingdom trade remains modest and partly unresolved. The armory and mineral
counter primarily express local expertise and serve residents, specialists,
and rare visitors. The plan must not invent regular caravans, a large export
market, or an outside supply chain before the world's trade relationships are
established.

A small council is a better starting proposal than a monarch. Seats can be
attached to stewardship of the vent, materials, education, outside exchange,
and households, while emergency authority changes according to the hazard at
hand. This remains a proposal until the exact population and political history
are approved.

## Jobs and workstations (user direction, 2026-10-07)

Metalworking and glassworking are the city's defining crafts. Every resident
has an explicit job and a real workstation, more like a modern craft
workshop than an ancient smithy.

The lava people work hot material with their own hands: a smith can hold a
glowing bar, and a glassworker can shape a gather with bare fingers. Their
tools are therefore for **precision and scale**, not protection. There are no
tongs, aprons or gloves. Instead they use:
- power hammers and hydraulic presses;
- welding and fixture tables;
- kilns, annealing ovens, grinders, lathes and polishing wheels.

## Cultural practices

These practices grow directly from physiology and place. Their names and exact
story meanings remain proposals.

- A youth's first deliberate full immersion can mark the point at which they
  can hold and reshape an adult body safely.
- Periodic communal immersion after a major cooling event or eruption can act
  as renewal, reunion, and civic accounting at once.
- Formal watches of the crater rim and vent can join scientific observation
  with inherited memory of past eruptions.
- Casting lava into permanent basalt sculpture can record a person, event, or
  shared decision. Fine metalwork and obsidian inlay can distinguish public or
  ceremonial pieces from ordinary construction.
- Mineral preparation can support hospitality and pleasure: measured samples
  are heated, crushed, alloyed, or presented at chosen temperatures according
  to individual taste.

## Architectural charter

**Status: draft for approval.** The city is scientifically and architecturally
advanced. Its buildings are not derived from a classical temple tradition or
from rustic forge houses.

### Composition

- **Primary, about 75 percent: invented volcanic modernism, in steel and
  glass.** The lava people are masters of heat, so their two great materials
  are **metal and glass**. **Revised, user direction, 2026-10-07:** the city
  is built as **exposed structural steel frames** filled with **large glass**.
  - **The steel:** columns, beams, portal frames and trusses, heat-blued or
    blackened, their joints welded by hand and left visible.
  - **The basalt:** cast basalt composite and refractory masonry form the
    plinths, the heat walls round hot work, and the curved shells of the
    landmark volumes.
  - **Services** are integrated into the construction. Ornament follows the
    shape and function of the building instead of being pasted onto it.
- **Secondary, about 20 percent: Gaudí-informed organic metal and glass craft.**
  This influence is confined to branching supports, hand-forged screens,
  railings, gates, handles, light fittings, catenary-like spans, colored glass,
  and selected mosaics. It does not turn the city into a copy of Barcelona or
  import Christian, Catalan, or classical symbols.
- **Accent, about 5 percent: living volcanic light.** Glowing mineral seams,
  molten service indicators, crystal lenses, and small areas of polished
  obsidian mark important thresholds and instruments.

The 60/30/10 material hierarchy (revised 2026-10-07) is:
- **60:** structural steel and large glass;
- **30:** dark basalt plinths, heat walls and landmark shells;
- **10:** molten or mineral colour. Dark structure is a foil
for light and color, not the city's whole visual identity.

### Color, glass, and metal palette

Most large glazing remains clear, lightly smoky, or faintly warm so interiors
receive daylight and preserve long views across the reservoir. Stained glass
then occupies selected clerestories, entry fins, privacy screens, bridge
balustrades, light housings, and small sections of broad window walls. It uses
abstract mineral and molten-flow patterns rather than human religious scenes or
copied cathedral tracery.

The shared stained-glass palette is:

- amber and molten orange for warmth and public gathering;
- garnet and deep red for forge and material work;
- cobalt and blue-violet for laboratories, observatories, and cooling rooms;
- emerald and mineral teal as a limited counterpoint;
- clear, smoke, and near-black glass to separate the saturated colors.

An individual building uses clear or smoky glass plus no more than two principal
stained colors. Landmarks may introduce a third. This keeps the city colorful
without turning every elevation into an unrelated rainbow. Stained areas should
cast visible pools or bands of color across floors and curved walls when the sun
or reservoir light reaches them.

Structural metal remains primarily blackened iron, dark steel, or nickel-rich
alloy. Color comes from heat bluing, controlled oxide finishes, and vitreous
enamel in garnet, cobalt, violet, teal, or amber. Bright stainless steel marks
precision laboratories, service controls, and selected frames. Gold and silver
appear as thin protected inlays, handle details, civic emblems, and fine craft
on cooler surfaces. They do not sit beside exposed lava, where their low melting
points would make them an implausible structural material.

The resulting city should read as luminous and materially rich in daylight and
at night: black structure, bright metal highlights, transparent depth, colored
reflections, and the orange movement of lava beneath it. Copper, brass, timber,
cloth, and other heat-sensitive materials only appear where insulation and
purpose make them credible.

### Form

- Buildings step into the crater slope but present confident modern volumes,
  transparent public fronts, curved galleries, and framed views across the
  reservoir instead of reading as caves or bunkers.
- Buildings meet sloping volcanic ground through a shared family of deeply
  socketed basalt foundations. Mild slopes use tapered continuous plinths;
  moderate slopes use stepped podiums; steep banks use bedrock shear walls,
  broad branching buttresses, or short supported cantilevers rather than thin
  stilts. Each base extends below the surrounding terrain far enough to prevent
  a visible seam, while the terrain is actually excluded beneath the footprint
  so visible floors and collision remain one coordinated surface.
- Primary masses use continuous SuperEgg shells and extruded superellipse plans.
  Their transitions are smooth and their cuts are exact. Rounded rectangles
  approximating the silhouette are not sufficient.
- Large windows are clear, smoky, or lightly colored panes set into the wall
  system. They follow broad superellipse openings, curved corners, or continuous
  glazed bays and bring sunlight deep into homes, laboratories, and civic
  spaces. There are no quaint shutters.
- Stained glass remains subordinate to clear glazing. It concentrates around
  thresholds, upper light, privacy, and crafted focal points so it colors the
  architecture without obscuring every view.
- Forged frames can branch like cooled lava veins to hold glass, canopies, and
  bridges. Repeating curves are structurally legible; arbitrary squiggles are
  not a substitute for design.
- Roofs are shallow walkable shells, folded or catenary-like canopies, and
  rounded terraces with integrated ash shedding and drainage. Classical
  pediments and copied domestic gables are excluded.
- Doors are broad superellipse openings with pivoting, sliding, or recessed
  metal-and-glass leaves. Public thresholds favor generous clear spans.
- Lava wells, molten floors, bridges, counters, and large instruments are
  physical traversal objects. Fine sparks, molten drips, service indicators,
  and surface inlays remain decorative or use the liquid hazard API.

### Building hierarchy

- **Landmarks:** council and observatory, communal rejuvenation hall, deep
  forge, and reservoir crossings. These may use the widest glass spans, the
  richest forged screens, colored glass, and visible branching structure.
- **Public buildings:** school, laboratories, shops, and guest inn. These use
  broad glazed fronts and clearly articulated entrances, with ornament
  concentrated at handles, screens, rails, and lighting.
- **Homes:** compact, asymmetrical modern dwellings with one strong glazed
  outlook, an integrated private thermal room, and restrained metalwork.
- **Industrial buildings:** clear long-span shells, large protected openings,
  overhead handling equipment, and visible service logic. Decoration follows
  the machinery and structure.

The city excludes copied temperate houses, classical columns and pediments,
timber gables, quaint shutters, textile awnings, ordinary flower beds, generic
braziers placed where the reservoir already provides light, fake exposed pipes,
and decorative lava containers with no thermal or civic use. Sparse pioneer
grass and flowers belong only to old cooled rock in the kingdom's outer wilds;
they do not become ornamental planting on the hot civic terraces. No living
plant survives within the caldera; its public landscape is geological and
civic, using rock formations, mineral deposits, fitted terraces, controlled
fire, colored glass light, and sculpture.

### Flame and night-lighting family

Open bowls with oversized turbulent flames do not suit this technically precise
city. The existing giant braziers and wall fires are replaced by a small shared
family of controlled fixtures:

1. **Arrival beacons.** The two threshold pylons hold slim vertical flames
   behind clear or lightly colored glass. Their forged cages are part of the
   pylon structure rather than separate torches attached afterward.
2. **Path lights.** A square-ended SuperEgg metal stem supports a small sealed
   glass capsule containing a low flame. Light falls onto the walking surface;
   the flame itself stays below eye level and never fills the path.
3. **Wall lights.** A branching forged bracket grows out of the building frame
   and carries a shallow glass flame cell. The bracket, frame, and nearby window
   mullions share the same metal vocabulary.
4. **Civic and workshop lights.** Larger fixtures appear only where scale and
   work require them. They use several small controlled flames or a glowing
   molten channel, not one enormous particle plume.
5. **Submerged markers.** Under lava, mineral lenses and molten service seams
   replace open flame entirely.

The procedural flame should hold a stable tapered body with a small flickering
tip, restrained turbulence, little or no spray, and no plume broader than its
glass housing. Emissive glass provides most of the apparent glow. A limited
number of real lights illuminate paths and activity areas so the fixtures stay
lightweight. Colored illumination should come mainly from the glass material,
projected-light decals, and a few carefully chosen area lights rather than one
dynamic colored light per pane. Large uncontrolled flames remain reserved for
eruptions, forge operations, and hazards, which preserves their visual force.

## Domestic and commercial interiors

A lava home still has public, private, work, and service gradients, but its
rooms answer different bodily needs.

- The entrance opens into a receiving and shaping room with mineral display,
  personal objects, tools, and a broad glass view. There is no obligatory
  human dining table.
- The private rest suite contains a radiant platform, shallow immersion well,
  or molten-floor rejuvenation chamber fed by the concealed duct network. Its
  cooler perimeter lets a resident leave tools and adornments before entering.
- A material pantry stores labeled-by-shape mineral samples, ores, and prepared
  delicacies in heat-safe vessels. It replaces both food pantry and wardrobe.
- A small shaping bench and polished reflective surface support repair and
  deliberate alteration of the resident's molten silhouette.
- Ventilation, pressure relief, and a service access point are part of every
  dwelling plan. They are not added as decoration afterward.

The weapon and armor shop must connect directly to its forge, finished-goods
gallery, secure material store, and delivery route. The gem and mineral dealer
needs an assay bench, separated display cells, a secure vault, and a visible
source ledger in the eventual `FireCalderaPlan`. The mineral delicacy maker
needs crushing, grading, heating, alloying, cooling, and presentation stations.
These three businesses cannot be generic counters with unrelated stock.

## Gameplay and reveal sequence

1. The player first enters on the dry outer ring. The city already reads as a
   functioning place, while the large reservoir is visibly inaccessible.
2. The outer market, visitor inn, council approach, and selected workshops are
   available without lava protection.
3. Lava Slide's existing story grants the Lava Helm. The route back to the city
   should make the now-accessible reservoir an obvious invitation without a
   text instruction.
4. Diving reveals the submerged district, additional residents, deeper
   scientific and civic spaces, rare craft access, and routes that reconnect to
   the surface city.
5. Party members who cannot enter lava gather on authored dry platforms rather
   than falling into the reservoir or teleporting onto roofs.

The reservoir must use the kingdom's existing canonical lava surface,
immersion, bubbling, steam, lighting, audio, and under-lava rendering systems.
It is not implemented as an oversized decorative cylinder.

## Existing implementation to replace

The current village script builds eight generic 2-by-2 one-storey houses,
eight generic adult residents, eight freestanding braziers, one ordinary inn,
and a central lava fountain only 8.4 metres across. Those elements explain why
the place currently reads as a placeholder.

The caldera terrain already provides a 72-metre floor radius inside a 112-metre
rim, which is enough for the proposed reservoir and surrounding terraces. Its
121-sample terrain mesh spans the entire kingdom, however, so the caldera has
roughly 15-metre sample spacing. Fine banks, walkable shelves, and submerged
rooms cannot depend on that coarse mesh alone. The implementation should use a
localized authored caldera mesh or another clean solid-geology layer tied into
the terrain's height and lava queries. It must not hide gaps with decorative
shore bands or floating slabs.

## Planning status and next gate

### Fixed

- Lava physiology, thermal and mineral needs, and the concealed civic
  lava-duct system determine the settlement rather than human domestic habits.
- The tiny fountain becomes a large natural reservoir with a surface city and
  a meaningful submerged public district.
- A continuous dry visitor route links the kingdom gate, arrival threshold,

  relationships.
- Five functional council seats with emergency authority following the system
