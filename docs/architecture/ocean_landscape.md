# Ocean Kingdom: Islands' Flora and Settlement Landscaping

Status: landscape plan for review (2026-10-06). Read with
`ocean_island_village_layout.md` and `ocean_cage_island.md`. The species palette,
zones and cultural notes are in the `landscaping` skill's
`references/hawaiian_flora.md`.

## 1. What exists now

`ocean_kingdom_terrain.gd` (`_build_island_nature`) scatters plants at random on
every island: on the main island 24 trees (mostly coconut palms, with every
fifth a banana and every seventh a pink flowering tree) and 48 undergrowth
pieces (bushes, grass tufts and a few orange, pink and yellow flowers); on each
satellite a few palms and bushes. Positions are random, species alternate by
index, and nothing relates to water, wind, ground or people.

This plan replaces the scatter with planting that follows the climate, the
geology and the people who live there.

## 2. Climate and the islands

- **Trade winds** from the world north-east. Windward faces are wet and green;
  leeward faces are dry and golden.
- **The main island** is an old, eroded volcano (28 m, with a windward valley).
- **The cage island** is a young crescent crater, mostly bare tuff.
- **The five satellites** are small sandy islets with 7 to 22 m of dry land:
  coastal strand and coastal forest only.

| Island (world centre) | Dry radius | Character | Planting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main, `(-118, 72)` | about 58 m after reshaping | old volcano, windward valley | full zonation, below |
| Cage, `(112, 88)` | crater about 32 m | young tuff cone | sparse pioneers, below |
| `(151, -26)` | 22 m | the largest islet; low and dry | *wiliwili* and *pili* grassland on its crown, *naupaka* ring |
| `(-34, -154)` | 17 m | sandy, sheltered | coastal forest of *kou* and *milo*, *naupaka* and *pōhuehue* |
| `(42, 138)` | 16 m | sandy | a *hala* grove, *naupaka*, *ʻākulikuli* on rocks |
| `(72, -118)` | 12 m | sandy | a coconut grove (*niu*) and beach strand |
| `(-152, -91)` | 7 m | a bare sand islet | *pōhuehue* and *hinahina* only, no trees |

Each satellite gets one character rather than the same palm-and-bush mix, so
they read as different islands from the water.

## 3. The main island

### The wild island (no tender)

| Zone | Where | Planting |
|---|---|---|
| Crown and ridges | above the valley walls | *ʻōhiʻa lehua* forest with red blossom, *uluhe* fern mats beneath |
| Valley walls | the steep sides of the valley | *kukui* trees in drifts, their pale leaves marking the valley from the sea; *hāpuʻu* tree ferns in the wetter lower half |
| Valley head | around the waterfall and pool | *hāpuʻu*, *uluhe*, moss and ferns on wet rock; the wettest, greenest place on the island |
| Leeward side | the far slopes | dry forest: *wiliwili* in scattered groups, *ʻaʻaliʻi*, *pili* grass going gold |
| Headlands | the two lava points | bare dark lava with *ʻākulikuli* mats and a few windswept *naupaka* |
| Coast away from the village | the rest of the shoreline | *hala* groves leaning seaward, *naupaka* in a continuous low band, *pōhuehue* on the sand |

Planting is in drifts and groves of one kind, never one of each.

**Palette by place.** The village keeps to about six kinds around homes (*kī*,
*ʻulu*, *maiʻa*, *melia*, *kou*, *niu*), repeated for coherence; the crops, the
wild valley and the beach each have their own short list.

### Ground and wear

- Worn earth paths from every *lānai* to the green and the beach path; a packed
  apron at the canoe house launch; trampled ground at the pier head and the
  store counter.
- Lava-rock edging along the terrace walls, the *ʻauwai* and the fishpond.
- Fallen *ʻulu* leaves under the breadfruit; coconut husks under the palms;
  drying *lauhala* (hala leaves) on a rack beside the Kahananui home.
- Footbridges of timber where paths cross the stream.

## 4. The cage island

Young volcanic ground: mostly bare dark-brown tuff.

- **Outer slopes:** sparse *pili* grass going gold, *ʻaʻaliʻi* with red seed
  capsules in wind-bent clumps, low *ʻilima*.
- **Inner ledges (the stands):** bare tuff with lichen, a few *moa* (whisk fern)
  in cracks; nothing where people sit.
- **Crater floor:** packed sand, trampled; no planting.
- **Landing beach:** *pōhuehue* and *naupaka* only at the horns.
- **One young *ʻōhiʻa*** in a sheltered crack high on the rim: the first tree on
  new volcanic ground.

## 5. Underwater (for completeness)

The sea folk city's kelp gardens, sea-flower beds and coral belong to the sea
folk city's brief and landscape. This plan covers dry land and the reef flat.

## 6. Implementation notes

- **New builders** in `NatureProps`, built from SuperEgg parts, in this order of
  need: *kalo* (crop clumps), *kī*, *hala* (stilt roots and spiral leaf tufts),
  *ʻulu*, *kukui*, *hāpuʻu* tree fern, *ʻōhiʻa* (gnarled, red blossom),
  *naupaka*, *pōhuehue* runners, *pili* grass, *wiliwili*, *hau*. Existing
  builders cover *niu* (`build_palm_tree`) and *maiʻa* (`build_banana_tree`);
  `build_flowering_tree` can be adapted for *melia*.
- **Placement** in clusters through a helper that reuses a small mesh set, keyed
  to zones in the plan data, replacing `_build_island_nature()`'s random
  scatter.
- **Collision:** trunks, *hala* stilt roots, terrace walls, rocks and the
  fishpond wall are solid; fronds, leaves, grass, crops and flowers are
  decorative.
- **Colour:** keep the island's greens varied by species (the pale *kukui*, the
  dark *ʻulu*, the grey-green *ʻōhiʻa*, the yellow-green *kalo*), with leeward
  gold and lava black. Avoid one uniform green.

## Fixed, proposed and open

**Fixed (from direction):** Hawaiian ecosystem and climate for the islands'
flora; landscaping around the settlements.

**Proposed:** north-east trade winds; zonation from reef to crown; a distinct
character for each satellite; canoe plants where people live and natives in the
wild; the species, tenders and jobs above; excluded invasives; the builder list.

**Open:** planting density for performance; whether any fauna (seabirds, the
*nēnē* goose, monk seals on the beaches) belongs in a later pass.
