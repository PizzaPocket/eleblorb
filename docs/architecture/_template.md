# <Settlement name>: architecture, plan, landscape and interiors

Status: DRAFT | APPROVED. Bible entry: `docs/world_bible.md` section "<name>".

## 1. Inhabitants and what they need (from the bible)
One row per named person or household: trade, household, what the building must provide (rooms, workspace, storage, privacy), who visits, what they bring, noise or fire hazards, personal quirks. Cite the bible line.

## 2. Architectural influences (the style charter)
Primary style (about 75 percent), one secondary influence limited to named elements (about 20 percent), accent for landmarks (about 5 percent). Closed kit of parts: roof types and one pitch band, wall materials and hierarchy, window family and proportions, door types, chimney type, eave and gable details, signature motif, palette (60/30/10), plinth height, structural module. Hierarchy of polite and vernacular buildings. Exclusions. Why these influences suit this people, climate and materials (see `references/styles.md`, `references/cultures.md`).

## 3. Town plan
Setting, terrain, water, the public heart, road and lane hierarchy, plots and frontage, where industry and noise sit, agricultural edge, how people arrive. A circulation diagram (who walks from where to where) and the resulting worn-ground network. Building list with plot, footprint, orientation and door positions.

## 4. Building briefs
One per building, in real terms: primary and secondary masses, roof types and pitch, plinth, middle and top, bays and openings, chimney, entry and porch type, balcony or bay type, materials and colours, ornament, which charter elements it uses, its one-line history, and its variation axes.

## 5. Variation matrix
Buildings by axes (plan, mass, roof, entry, upper feature, history) so differences are planned and checkable.

## 6. Landscaping plan
Gardens, hedges, orchards, trees, greens, ground cover, water edges and wear, each tied to a tender and a job (see the `landscaping` skill); plant palette for the settlement.

## 7. Interior briefs
One per inhabited building: the occupants, room program and adjacency, circulation, door positions, furnishing and decoration by layer, lighting, and the personal items that tell their story (see the `interior-design` skill).

## 8. Data and validation
Where the machine-readable plan lives, and the last validator result.
