## 2. Architectural influences (style charter)

**Primary (about 75 percent): English medieval and early-modern village vernacular, half-timber and stone.** Hall-house massing with cross-wings and lean-to outshuts, jettied upper storeys, steep gables, clay-tile roofs, plastered walls over a stone plinth.
**Secondary (about 20 percent): Georgian simplification on civic and polite buildings only**, limited to a small pedimented door case on the Vey house, a regular bay rhythm and a boxed eave. The meeting house and the inn take open timber gabled porches (carved bargeboards, posts, brackets) in the English tradition; a pedimented portico is withdrawn because it read as Greek. Cottages and workshops stay purely vernacular.
**Accent (about 5 percent, landmarks only):** the windmill and the bell turret.

**Kit of parts.** Roof: gable, half-hip, lean-to or catslide, one pitch band of 38 to 45 degrees, clay tile, bargeboards, eaves about 0.4 m. Walls: stone plinth (0.5 m) and ground storey, plastered upper storey on an exposed oak frame, jettied 0.3 m on street fronts, limewash. Chimney: external gable-end stack built into the wall, a visible breast stepping in, clearing the ridge by 0.8 m. Windows: tall casements with mullions, superellipse frames of the house's own timber, stone sills below, shutters only on dwellings. Doors: ledged and braced board doors for homes, a double panelled door under a portico for the hall. Signature motif: carved bargeboards and a rolled bracket under every jetty and canopy. Palette: walls cream and ochre limewash (60), roofs clay red-brown (30), oak trim with one accent per building (10).
**Hierarchy.** Polite: meeting house, Holt Inn, Fask and Prewitt house. Vernacular: all other homes and workshops, granary, sheds, stalls.
**Exclusions.** No stacked-log walls, no Craftsman tapered columns, no pagoda or Alpine elements, no Georgian sash on cottages.
**Option.** If the user prefers Craftsman as the secondary influence, confine it to porch columns on stone piers and exposed rafter tails on the civic and inn buildings, and drop the pedimented portico.

## 3. Town plan

### 3.1 Assessment of the current plan (why it is being reset)

Checked against the skill's town-planning rules and the top-down render. The village grew one request at a time and it shows:
- **No street structure.** A hub with five spokes radiates from the fountain to nowhere in particular; two lanes stop at single houses; the market frontage is a smear of worn ground and not a street.
- **It ignores its defining feature.** Ohio stands on a headland with the plateau rim 85 to 95 m east, yet every building sits west of x = 40 and the east is empty grass. A village on a cliff edge should have a place that looks off it.
- **Polite buildings are scattered.** The Fask and Prewitt house (polite, by charter) sits 30 m from the green; the mason's house sits at the green's south-east with nothing to do with the water works it keeps, which are now a long way up the hills.
- **Wren's hedge line.** She patrols a hedge line that does not exist; resolved by dropping the idea: she patrols the outer lanes. The entrance walls end in open meadow and will be shortened.
- **Open strips with no job.** The south-east of the green is a gap with a stone yard in it.

### 3.2 The plan (APPROVED with the changes below; built from `OhioPlan`)

Organising idea: a **headland village on one axis**. Travellers enter at the west, walk the main street, reach the green, and the road carries on east to an overlook on the rim. Three nodes (gateway, green, overlook), a clear hierarchy of ways, and one boundary.

**Nodes**
- **Gateway** (built): two lantern piers at (-52,-18), the sign on a stone-ringed verge island inside the piers, the inn's roadside gable as the village's face. 
- **The green** (public heart): an oval about 30 by 22 m, fountain at the origin, the harvest-supper stretch on the north side, shade trees NE and SW, benches facing the fountain.
- **Play lawn** (south-east of the green, where the mason's house stood): flat open lawn with the rope-swing tree; Petra's pupils and the blorb-watchers use it.
- **Overlook** (78,2): a circular terrace of individually fitted flagstones on the natural gentle grade, with two benches, a lantern and one old tree. It has no rim wall. The worn east road ends on its inland edge rather than projecting down the cliff, and the terrace terminates the main axis as the village's place to watch the evening over the lake.

**Frontages and plots**
- **Market row (north of the green, facing south):** Vey and his stall, the meeting hall at the head (centre), the smithy and Brinna's stall at the east end. Kept as built.
- **Gateway street:** Holt Inn (north) and Barrow bakehouse (south) face each other across the main street. Kept.
- **Milling quarter (exposed west rise):** windmill, Ferris millhouse and the fenced grain yard. Kept.
- **Homes:** Fask and Prewitt house moves to (-21,20) on the West lane, close to the green and to the hall, as a polite house should be. Wren's warden cottage stays at the lane end by the hedge. Cray and Thorne cottage stays at the East lane end beside the orchard.
- **Water-keeper's quarter (north-east):** the Ruskin house moves to (47,-30) beside the mill road's start, with the stone yard east of it. Tam's work is the race, the spring works and the fountain, and now the road to them begins at his door.
- **Orchard:** five or six fruit trees east of the Cray cottage (30..46, 32..48), the edge walk along its far side.

**Boundary.** None. The user ruled out a hedge ring, a rim wall and walls around the town: the village edge is open meadow and the cliff is left bare. The gateway is two lantern piers and nothing else (the wing walls are removed). Wren's patrol follows the outer lanes and the back lane.

**Outdoor lighting.** Ohio uses one fixture family in three scales. The entrance has compact glazed lantern crowns seated directly on the masonry piers. The main street, green and overlook use substantial square oak standards on stone feet, with knee-braced arms projecting capped iron-cage lanterns toward the way they serve. The inn, meeting house and working public entrances use smaller wall brackets from the same family. Private houses do not each receive a light. This establishes a legible nighttime hierarchy without rows of generic poles or a fixture on every facade. Interior lighting is not part of the current completion pass.

**Open entrance (user direction, built):** the entrance felt choked. The inn stays; the bakehouse, the windmill and the Ferris millhouse with its grain yard all moved south, so that the south side of the main street is open meadow. The straight line from the piers to the fountain used to clip the inn's south-west corner, so the gateway moved 6 m south to (-52,-18) and the main street was straightened onto that line; the validator now fails if anything crosses a 5.6 m corridor from the piers to the fountain. The bakehouse is at (-25,2), turned to face the green. The milling quarter moved 6 m south: windmill (-44,-2.5), millhouse (-40,16), grain yard, cart shelter and granary with it. The Fask and Prewitt house is at (-18,26) on the West lane; the Ruskin house at (53,-27) beside the mill road, its stone yard at (61,-27.5).

**What stays fixed (gameplay anchors):** fountain and Water Gem at the origin; the three stall spots; the false-hero spot near the fountain; the inn, its wake room and the keeper; the enterable windmill.

**Order of work once approved:** the data file first (section 8) so every number below lives in one place read by the generator and the validator; then lanes and the market street as authored ways; then the moves (Fask house, Ruskin house and yard, play lawn); then the overlook, the boundary and lamps; then re-run the validator and render.

## 7. Interior briefs

Persona dressing is in the `interior-design` skill; these are the building-level briefs.

- **Holt Inn.** A symmetrical two-post open timber porch frames one broad central bay; no post occupies the doorway centreline. Its knee braces rise from the outer posts toward the inner beam span, visibly supporting rather than hanging from it. Because Ohio has a mild climate and the porch already shelters the threshold, a 2.7 m double doorway opens directly into a generous arrival zone in the common room instead of forcing guests through a narrow enclosed vestibule. Pegs and a mat sit beside that arrival zone. The common room gathers around one great gable fireplace, with settles either side and tables in groups (including a window table for travellers). The fireplace and its single wall-bonded chimney rise through a broad wall-clipped superellipse void to form a double-height hearth hall; a curved upper gallery overlooks it so heat and light reach both storeys. The counter stands well east of the door with Mira's office directly behind it (desk, ledger shelf, strongbox, window onto the yard). The kitchen shares the warm chimney wall without inventing a second fireplace mouth, and has a pot rack, yard door, pantry and privy closet against the outside wall. Upstairs, two separately locked chambers flank the chimney, with clear landings between their doors and the hearth rail. Only the south cache room stops short at its east end, creating the deliberate railing parkour route. Its chest visibly contains the Holt Inn Key, two Fire Gems and Blorb Slime, all picked up as individual objects. After parkouring back out, the key opens the cache-room door and the single door of the fully enclosed north room. That north room holds boxing gloves, another Fire Gem and Blorb Slime together with Hollis; opening his door frees him and earns 10 Tokoins. The remaining guest rooms and Mira's room open normally from the landing.
- **Meeting house.** Double-height hall with the hearth bay running up into the roof, benches along the walls, a raised end with Halda's table and a ledger stand, lamps on the beams; records room with shelves of boxes and a strongbox; Petra's corner (slates, a long table, small stools, a chest of readers, a copying desk by a window) and her small room behind; the bell rope in the corner under the belfry.
- **Fask and Prewitt house.** Hall with the hearth, a table where Halda spreads ledgers and Dorran leaves clamps, a drawing of the roofline on the wall that Halda has never corrected; a workshop-style lean-to with his joinery tools; upstairs bedrooms off a landing, one with a crooked beam they never mention.
- **Cray and Thorne cottage.** Hall and kitchen with Oswin's worn chair and tool rack, a pipe on the mantel, a cushion on the porch where a wild blorb sleeps; Ivy's loft with sketches pinned to the rafters, jars and a notebook.
- **Sallow cottage.** Hall with herb bundles from the beams, a gathering basket, boots at the door, a map of the hedge line, a small lamp always low; drying shed and physic garden beside.
- **Ruskin mason's house.** Workshop with stone samples and mallets, a drawing table of fountain carvings, an hourglass that no longer runs, a lime store at the rear, a second place laid for Wren on Thursdays.
- **Vey house and storehouse.** A tidy narrow house with cabinets of curios and labelled boxes, a good chair, a locked strongbox; a secure storehouse behind with shelves and crates.
- **Kest smithy.** Forge bay with hearth and bellows, anvil on a stump, quench trough, tool rack, fuel and iron stock; stone floor; Brinna's room above with a cot, a caravan guard's old cloak and a rack of tested blades; Cob's mended cart in the yard.
- **Barrow bakehouse.** Brick oven with peel and rakes, floured table, bread racks, flour bins, a scale; a shop hatch to the street; a rear door to the yard with a handcart; Tess's small stool by the table.
- **Fask water sawmill.** This is a timber mill, not another grain mill. Water in the leat turns the undershot wheel; the axle passes through the north drive wall to a faster crank gear; an offset pin and connecting rod reciprocate the complete frame saw over a straight rail-mounted log carriage. An unsawn log sits on the input carriage, with sawdust at the cut and squared boards on the output side. The long gabled building follows that linear workflow. Offcuts, shavings and timber racks occupy its work yard; Pip's loft bed sits above the dry side of the saw floor with a tally board of orders.
- **Windmill and Ferris millhouse.** The windmill is a two-stage smock mill rooted in Ohio's stone-and-oak vernacular. Its octagonal form has a masonry grinding stage, a lighter timber machinery stage, rounded oak corner posts and one centred soft boat-cap rather than a misaligned faceted roof. The sails, windshaft and brake wheel are one rotating assembly. A wallower turns the upright shaft; the great spur wheel drives the smaller stone nut and spindle; the runner stone grinds grain fed from the hopper and shoe; meal leaves through a chute into a bin and sacks. A service deck surrounds the central gearing rather than intersecting it, and two broad wall-side switchback ramps reach that deck without railings or blocking the working floor. The adjacent Ferris millhouse is not a second mill: it is Cob's home and the receiving, weighing, storage and bagging side of the same grain business, with a barometer-charm on a nail, his grandmother's chair by the fire and Wick's sack stand.

## 8. Data and validation

`scripts/ohio_plan.gd` (`OhioPlan`) is the single source for the layout: buildings (name, position, yaw, cells, floors, spec), the inn, windmill, stalls, lamps, the entrance, the overlook, every way and worn-ground blob, yards, gardens, trees, residents' home spots and the water works (tarn, mill, aqueduct course, bridge). `TownGenerator` builds only from it (the building, stall, lantern and spot markers were removed from `scenes/main.tscn`). `tools/validate_village.gd` runs against what was built and also reads the plan: every plan building and key element must exist, nothing may cross the view from the entrance to the fountain, and the existing checks (overlaps, door clearance and reach on foot including the mill, facing, gates, path gaps) run over the whole village including the mill 140 m away.

To move anything, edit `OhioPlan` and re-run: `Godot --path . tools/validate_village.tscn -- --village=ohio`, then render with `tools/village_aerial_capture.gd`.
